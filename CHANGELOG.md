# Changelog

All notable changes to Idyll Lives. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); each release is a git tag `vX.Y.Z`.

## Unreleased

- **A living town to zoom out to:** the play camera zooms out past the home, in one movement with no switch, to a bird's-eye view of the whole town from up to 900 m (it stopped at 95 m). On the way out the tilt eases into a view from above, the haze thins and the house in view stands whole under its roof; wheel back in and the camera is where it was. The town is drawn for that distance: every building far off as one of five simple shapes, the far woods as crowns, the roads, the centre's paving, water and fields as one colour map over the land, and cars, taxis, bikes and residents as small shapes where they really are. Its lights follow its life: a shop's front is lit while it is open, the homes' windows go out one by one towards midnight, and a household's house glows only while someone is up in it. The buildings nobody lives in stand on something (a paved forecourt, a yard, a garden with a hedge) with a way to the road, a shop has a shopfront under a fascia, and farmland's fields end along straight lines (their edges stepped along a 10 m grid).
- **Terraces whose roofs don't run into each other:** a terraced house's outline, as its neighbours and the town view draw it, took in next door's wall on the shared line and was a metre too wide, so the roofs of a row cut into each other. Each ends at its own wall now.
- **A denser town:** a main town has rows of terraced houses and semi-detached pairs on narrow lots (real homes with a household and a price: a world has about 1.35 to 1.55 times the homes it had), a centre with a high street and a square, and a backdrop of 60 to 125 buildings nobody lives in (flats, shops, offices, a town hall, a church, a school, workshops; a hamlet has 8 or more, a farm its barns). The simulation never sees the backdrop. Every town has named places with opening hours, and every career a workplace among them.
- **Town tags:** from the town view, tags name the household's residents and where they are ("Theo · at work · Brightfield Fire Station · home 20:00"; a click glides down to them and follows them), family, partners and friends elsewhere in town (a click glides there and offers a visit or a call), the places (open or closed and till when, who of yours is there, how many are inside), the homes for sale nearest the middle of the view (price and size; a click opens the town map on that lot) and the streets and areas. The household's own tags are there from the moment the town view comes in (the rest from about 200 m out), and its home has a tag of its own while nobody is in. They are laid out as the menus' tags are: the household's own always, on top and clear of each other, then your people, the places, the homes for sale and the names, each tag near what it names or not shown, never cut off at the screen's edge or under the interface. Farther out there are fewer: a street's worth of places close in, 14 over the whole town from 400 m, 6 from 900 m, and twelve homes for sale close in, six from 400 m; a hamlet's places show while the camera looks into the hamlet, a hamlet's name tells two places of one name apart, and the pointer on a place brings its tag up. A small menu turns each kind on or off.
- **The day at a glance:** a bar under the clock shows the day from sunrise to sunset, and from the town view a card lists the household's next shifts, school days and planned visits.
- **Bigger towns:** a town holds up to 240 residents (160 before); a new world starts with about 120, as before, and the newcomers who move in fill the rest.
- **Faster with a full town:** the hitch at 09:30, when the town's jobs are handed out, is gone (the new hires' commutes are worked out one a tick: the worst tick went from 70–88 ms to 22 ms in a town of 222), who is within earshot is found far more cheaply with the same answers, and the work taxis' kerbs at the start of a game take 4 ms instead of 82.
- **A porch you build:** the roofed entrance over the front door (a low landing with a step, two white posts, a little gabled roof in the house's roof colour with a gable in its wall colour, and a lamp) is the simulation's now, not drawn where the garden path met the house. Build › Construct has a Porch tool: click any outside door on the ground floor for a porch over it ($600; free in Creative), or click a porch to take it down for $360, like furniture sold. It needs open ground in front of the door, no wall or fence across it and nothing where its posts go; a wall, fence or diagonal can't be built through one. Walled up, turned into a window or taken out, the door takes its porch with it (sold back); a room moved with Move takes its porches along. The tool also builds a porch of your own from pieces, in your house's colours: a post ($60; nobody walks through it), a gabled canopy 1 to 3 tiles across and up to 6 deep ($90 a tile; its ridge runs the way it faces, over posts or against a wall, and over the driveway as a car port) and a step platform up to 4 × 4 ($30 a tile, a low step along its front, walked across); R turns them and the panel sets their size. Porches and pieces can be undone, are kept in blueprints, and stand on the lot from the street and in the menus' town as they do in the game. A new town puts a porch where the street drew one before, so towns look as they did; saves from before keep theirs too.
- **Traffic lights only where they're needed:** only a big junction is lit now, where four roads or more meet or two main roads do (a street meeting a street or a country road): about 11 in a town, where every junction of three roads or more was (77). Every other one, a lane meeting a street or another lane, or country roads meeting out in the open, gives way: cars take turns through it as they do at any junction, with no stop for nobody, and a street through it has a zebra on one side of the lane's mouth (it had one on each arm and a pole on each corner). Two such junctions close together along a street (within 40 m) have one zebra between them, not one either side, and none by a lit junction's crosswalk. At a lit junction, a side road's crosswalk is walked while the main road has green, its head showing walk, and cars turning into the side road stop for those on it; someone walking along the main road's pavement past the side road no longer turns every light red. A car whose way round a taxi standing at the kerb is laid while it's in the middle of a junction keeps its way through it, where another coming the other way was let in and the two stood nose to nose for good. At a zebra a car stops at its line only for someone on it or about to step onto it, and waits for them to cross; then it drives on. The lights left are smarter: with nobody waiting they rest on the main road's green rather than wherever they stopped, a car coming up to a green keeps it on for it, and the walk is over as soon as those crossing are across, not after its whole 10 seconds of every arm on red; someone who stopped waiting at the kerb no longer brings the walk round for nobody.
- **Shorter loading:** the game reaches its menu in about 6 seconds instead of 16 (it drew portraits of 118 residents nobody saw there, and made the menu's town one step after another on the page: its town is laid out in the background now while the rest gets ready, and its land and streets are built side by side), "Choose a household" shows the faces in view in about a second and all of them in under 3 (8 before), the ones on screen first, and a household's game opens about 8 seconds sooner (its world is built in longer steps behind the loading screen, its town's houses are ready from the menu's, and the town's other faces wait until it is up). Portraits are kept between visits.
- **Under the hood:** the production build's compressed copies of the assets (KTX2 textures, quantised and meshopt-compressed models, gzipped residents) are made by a tool of the repo's own in Rust, `cargo assets`, instead of a JavaScript one with four packages behind it; `cargo assets check` reads every copy back and compares it with its source (all 2.19 million triangles within 0.29 mm, their shading and the animations exact). The game's pictures are the same but for the fine noise of the roughness and occlusion maps, whose smaller levels are averaged as the data they are now. The pins, the check of PlayCanvas's internals, the world maps and the bench's picture statistics moved to Rust as well (`cargo pins`, `check-chunks`, `world-map`, `shots`).
- **Save format 39:** the town's backdrop, places and centres (38) and the porches and porch pieces (39) are in the save. 0.8.0's saves open and play as before, their porches where they were drawn; 0.8.0 can't open the new ones.

## 0.8.0 — 2026-11-12

**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

Big towns that work: every town is a big one now, every junction is a real one with traffic lights and crosswalks, and cars, taxis and bikes go at the pace of the game. Residents who look like real people, each with a face of their own, and a world in daylight: a lawn with life in it, warm sun and cool shade, cloud shadows, worn streets, leaves on the trees. And stairs in three shapes, four more bikes, beds and sofas for a family with a grown child, and resolution as 720p to 2160p. Save format 37: 0.7.0's saves open, and 0.7.0 can't open 0.8.0's.

- **Only big towns:** a new game's town is always a big one, its hamlets joined by country roads; Cosy and Normal are gone from the neighbourhood creator. Every town houses up to 160 residents. Games saved in a Cosy or a Normal town still open.
- **Junctions with real traffic lights:** every junction where three roads or more meet is lit (in the biggest towns only the first few hundred lights were), and the arms across from each other go green together: a crossroads in two turns, a T in two (the road through, then the side road), a car turning across the way of those coming the other way waiting for them to pass. Each arm has a crosswalk at the corner and a stop line just short of it, where cars wait on red, the light's pole beside it on the kerb; a car let through just as the light changes but held up before the line stops after all. Someone on foot crossing at a junction waits at the kerb for the walk, which their waiting brings round: every arm red, a few seconds to step out and time to get across, then the cars again. The lights where a lane begins at a road's kerb stand round the middle of the road, not off towards the lane. The roundabouts work as before.
- **Vehicles at the pace of the game:** cars, the work taxi, bikes, e-scooters and the kids' bike go on the traffic's own clock, four times as fast as real time: cars drive at 32 km/h in the hamlets (21.6 before) and 58 on country roads, speed up quicker, keep their speed straight through a junction (the lights stop them) and take bends a little faster, so a drive across a big world takes 20 to 25 game minutes, 50 at most, instead of two to five hours, and a bike's ride reaches four times as far in the same game minutes. Everything about how they move and take turns keeps its shape (speeding up and slowing down, bends, how far ahead they look, turns at junctions, jams and the traffic lights' timing all run on the same clock), so a junction lets four times as many through as before and traffic flows as it did, only faster; walking, getting in and out and the taxi's stand for its riders keep their pace. Etas follow: the taxi beats a walk sooner, and visits and outings further off fit in the day by taxi or bike; the town's taxis for visits and outings are six at a time, there and back (with no limit a big world's evening had twenty out and jammed in view), and with all six out someone setting off goes another way or somewhere nearer. Saves from before (format 36) load with every car and taxi where it was along its way (format 37).
- **Junctions that work as junctions:** two side roads meeting a road a few metres apart, either side of it, are one crossroads with one set of lights (each was a T with its lights standing in the middle of the other); a car doesn't drive onto a crosswalk without room to clear it, so nobody waits on one; and where two lit junctions are too near each other to wait between, a car waits at the first's stop line till it can go through both on their greens. Those on foot cross at a lit junction on its zebras, on the walk, never through the middle of the junction or just past a zebra, and someone walking by a crosswalk on the pavement no longer stands there waiting for the walk; keeping them off the road there never shuts a house's gate or a bit of pavement off. A new world no longer lays a country road off a street's end where another street carries on.
- **Junctions that look like junctions:** where roads meet, one surface of asphalt joins them, its corners rounded by a 3 m kerb return, and the sidewalks turn the corner with their kerb, where they used to stop square and leave a bare square of road at each corner. Every arm of a junction into a street or lane has a white zebra crosswalk across it just out past the junction and a stop line across the lane coming in, where cars wait on red; a country road's arm gets only the stop line, while its lights are lit. Town sidewalks stop at a country road's mouth instead of running across it. Each traffic light's pole has a small head for those on foot facing across the road: a red standing figure, and a green walking one while the junction's walk phase is on, when every car head shows red. Roundabouts look as before.
- **Resolution as 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 2160p:** Settings › Performance › Resolution picks how many lines of pixels the game draws, 1080p to begin with, never more than the screen has (on a 1080p screen 1440p and 2160p draw its own 1080). On a Retina or 4K screen 1440p or 2160p is sharp, and 720p or 1080p runs faster. Auto and the 50–100 % slider are gone; a setting saved with either starts on 1080p.
- **Dev play log** (dev server only): every page load writes what happens while playing to `dev-logs/<time>.jsonl`: the browser, screen and GPU, console warnings and errors, screens and settings changes, games opened and closed, a sample every 5 seconds (frames, the resolution drawn, draw calls, the simulation, memory, the game's time and speed), resolution changes and stalls between frames.
- **Garden gates against houses and hedges:** a fence or gate meeting a house wall hangs from the wall instead of a post standing in it, a hedge beside a gate stops short of the gate's post instead of burying it in its rounded end, and a wooden gate's brace no longer flickers in the rails behind it.
- **Residents on the land:** out on a big world's hills between the hamlets, residents walk and ride on the ground, not sunk into it or floating over a hollow.
- **Bedtime for a family with a grown child:** the parents' double bed is theirs. A son or daughter with no bed of their own sleeps on the sofa (*Sleep on the sofa*: lying along it under a blanket, curled up on a two-seater under a throw pulled up to the neck and tucked round the seat, at full length on a three-seater) rather than in the parents' bed, and takes it only with nowhere else at all; a child shares a parent's bed for the night only with the other parent out. Before, an eighteen-year-old who went to bed first took half of it and his father, kept out of the bedroom, fell asleep on the landing. A race car bed is a child's: a generated house no longer gives one (nor toys only a child may use) to a teenager, a neighbour household swaps it for a bed when its child turns thirteen, and the player is told the teenager has no bed. Worn out with the bladder low, a resident sees the toilet through before going to bed, instead of being up from one for the other until they fall asleep on the floor. The quilts of a bed upstairs are drawn on its storey (they were drawn on the ground floor, under the house, so sleepers upstairs lay uncovered).
- **A house looks the same from the street and inside:** a neighbour's house with a porch at the front door (a gabled canopy on two posts over a white step, a lamp beside the door) still has it when you go into that household, where the door used to open straight onto the path. It is the same porch, drawn over the front door the garden path meets, so a door moved off the path or out of the front wall in Build mode takes it with it; it is only for looks (the doorway and the path are walked as before), and in the cutaway or with the walls down its canopy and lamp go with the front wall, its posts drop to stubs and its step stays. The player's house in the menus' town has it too. A two-storey house's upper floor and roof are those of the house below, the same as from the street: its look was picked again for the upper floor (brick upstairs over siding, say, under a roof of another colour), and the roof the player chose for a two-storey house showed only from the street.
- **Roofs that meet at corners are one roof:** where the storey above stands on the middle of the one below and the walls are square to each other, the roof left round it (an L, a U or a ring) was roofed a rectangle at a time, each rectangle with its own hips, ridge and eaves: two surfaces lay over the same tiles where they met and butted together at every corner, a step in the slope, a ridge that stopped and started again, a fascia inside the roof. The area is roofed whole now: one line of eaves runs round its outline, and the faces meet in hips, ridges and valleys, as a house with that plan would be roofed.
- **Stairs in three shapes:** the Stairs tool picks from three: the closed flight as before, an open flight of 14 oak boards on two slim steel plates, and a spiral of 14 wedge treads winding round a steel post over two tiles by two. A spiral is walked onto from any side of its square and left from the corner its last step reaches.
- **Four more bikes:** beside the city bike there is a mountain bike (knobbly tyres, a suspension fork), a road bike (drop bars, thin tyres, the quickest), a cargo bike (a box up front) and an e-bike (a battery in the frame, a motor at the pedals). A new town's gardens mix them, the city bike still the most common.
- **Residents with real proportions:** a head is a real body's share of its height (it was a seventh larger on a grown-up, nearly twice on a baby), the eyes a real size and as far apart, the arms and feet a real length (the shoes were clogs). Ears have a rim and a hollow and are a real ear's size; the teeth have lines between them and the nose's underside its shade; a hand at rest curls a little more towards the little finger.
- **A face of their own:** every resident's face is their own: jaw, chin, cheeks, nose, brow, eyes, mouth and the face's length vary from one to the next, a child's face is blended from the parents', and the household creator has a slider for each. Residents from saved games get a face of their own as they load.
- **Skin, eyes, hair and clothes in the light:** skin lets a little light through, warm at the edge of a shadow, with a softer highlight; the whites of the eyes lie in the lids' shade; hair has a sheen across its strands where the sun or a lamp catches it. Clothes stand off the body as far as they are loose: a tee hangs from the chest, a trouser leg falls straight over the shoe, a cuff stands off the arm. Tees, v-necks, polos and long tops are worn out over the trousers, not tucked in.
- **Classic shading by default:** the game draws its lit surfaces with physically based shading, so skin, grass and wood keep their own colours. The painterly Soft look is still in Settings; a game set to Soft before starts on Classic once, since Soft was only the old default.
- **Things shade what they stand on:** on Medium and High a sofa shades the wall it stands against, a chair's legs the floor and a pot the sill (contact shading), so a lamplit room has depth too. Low leaves it out.
- **Rooms in the shine:** a glossy thing indoors reflects the room round it, not the open sky: a chrome tap is bright in a lit room and dark in a dark one, by day and at night.
- **Leaves on the trees:** the oak, maple, birch, cherry, apple, magnolia, Japanese maple, willow and lemon tree have crowns of leaves, sprays of them over a darker core, where they had lumps under a pattern of leaves. Far off they are as before.
- **A calmer picture:** the lawn, most of what you see while you play, is quieter: its blades are close to one tone from root to tip instead of dark roots under bright yellow tips, and its green is a little deeper and less yellow, so the house and the people in it stand out. The picture's corners are less darkened, and a garden at night is dim under the moon, not near black.
- **A lawn with life in it:** the lawn is no longer one green from fence to fence: it has drier patches and lusher ones, a few metres across, the same ones every time you come back to a lot. From where you play it is calmer too: the ground between the blades is nearly the blades' own tone, so the grass no longer flickers as the camera moves. A lot is mown short and even, and the land between and beyond the lots is rough: longer, darker grass in clumps. Most lots show the mower's stripes, faintly. The grass is darker and lusher at the foot of walls, hedges and fences, most where their shade lies, and under trees. Feet have worn it to bare earth outside a door, at a gate, where a path ends, under a swing and before a bench, and beds and trunks stand in soil. Its flowers grow in drifts, as daisies and clover do, instead of one here and one there, with a patch of daisies, buttercups or violets here and there in a garden.
- **Warm sun, cool shade:** by day the sun is warmer and stronger and the sky's light cooler, so a lawn in the sun, the shade of a tree on it and a room lit through its windows differ in colour, not only in how light they are.
- **Cloud shadows:** by day soft, light shadows of clouds drift slowly over the land, so part of a garden lies in the sun and part in a cooler shade. They hold still with Reduce motion.
- **Things sit on the lawn:** the grass is shaded round a house's walls, along fences and hedges, under and round garden chairs, benches, beds and the water butt, and round a tree's trunk, so they stand on the ground instead of on a picture of it.
- **Cut walls look cut:** the tops of the walls cut away in front of a room are the grey of a wall sawn through, not bright white bars: the eye goes to the people and the furniture, not to the outline of the rooms. Nothing white is left on them: the small white ticks beside a doorway next to an outside wall are gone, and the cut ends of door frames and corner boards are grey too.
- **Floors with a middle and edges:** a floor is darker along the walls and lighter out in the room, palest in the doorways where everyone walks, so a room is no longer one flat colour from wall to wall.
- **Walls with depth:** a room's walls are darker at the floor and into the corners and vary a little over their length, where each was one flat tone.
- **A street that has been driven on:** the road is paler where wheels run and darker between them and along the kerb, with a patch of newer asphalt here and there; the pavement has a grey kerb and a course of grey stone at its back, and a darker gutter.
- **Paths that belong in the garden:** a garden path and a drive are darker and mossy along their sides, where they meet the lawn, instead of ending with a ruler's edge.
- **Finished roofs:** a hip roof has a course of cap shingles up each of its hips, as its ridge has.
- **Petals under a tree in blossom:** under a garden's tree in blossom its fallen petals lie pink on the grass.
- **Trees, shrubs and hedges with the light on them:** seen from above, a tree's crown, a shrub and a clipped hedge have a lighter, sunlit top over darker sides and undersides, where they were one dark green all over, and two trees or two shrubs of a kind differ more in their green. A clipped hedge is no longer ruler-straight: its top and sides are in soft mounds. The borders along the houses have beds of lupins and marigolds among their shrubs, in dark soil.
- **A smaller download for the residents:** the bodies, faces, hair and clothes are 3.9 MB to download instead of 5.1. A finer copy of the body, for close views, was sent with every game and used by none; it now comes only to the household creator, whose resident stands close enough for it to show: the outline of the head, the jaw, the shoulders and a tee's neckline are smooth curves there, where they had corners.

## 0.7.0 — 2026-11-09

**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

Getting about town: bikes and e-scooters, lifts from the neighbours and a shared taxi to work and out. And a host who offers a bite to eat, a thing asked for carried in and set up where you can see it, promotions after a working week, visitors let in, and only your household's news in the feed and the Journal. And a calmer day: 30 minutes long at normal speed, the sim running 60 times a second.

- **Bikes and e-scooters:** most households in a new town have something to ride, standing in the garden by the house or the garage, clear of the driveway, the paths and the doorsteps: about a city bike for each teen or grown-up (up to three, more often in a home with no car), a kids' bike with stabilisers for each child of four or more, and an e-scooter in about one home in six. Off to work or school with no seat in the household's car, a resident takes the fastest free one at home that fits them when the ride out of town is longer than 60 m: they walk over to it, get on and ride out of town (the city bike three times as fast as walking, the kids' bike a little over twice, the e-scooter four times), pedalling as its wheels and cranks turn, or stood on the deck, and its spot stands empty while it's out. Home again, they ride back to its spot and get off. Their way to work is timed by the ride, so they leave later and still arrive on time. The household strip and the town map say who is "Cycling to work" or "Riding the e-scooter home". Furnish sells all three under Outdoor › Bikes & scooters ($260, $120 and $420), and the bikes can be tuned up. Towns saved before keep what they had: no bikes.
- **A shared taxi to work, and out:** off to work or school a long way from town's way out on foot (longer than 10 game minutes), with no seat in a car and no bike or e-scooter for a ride out of an hour and a half at most, a resident in an open-world town rides a taxi the work sends when it gets them out sooner than the walk. Those going out of town the same way share one, whatever their work or school, when they start within 15 minutes of each other, four to a taxi, housemates side by side. The taxi comes in by that way out and pulls in at each rider's kerb: parallel to the lane and in it, on a straight stretch clear of every driveway's mouth (theirs too, by a car's length where it can be), of junctions, public parking and the lamps, hydrants and bins along the pavement, one taxi to a stretch at a time (another comes later rather than go without); riders leave home in time to wait at their kerb ("Waiting for the taxi"), sooner from upstairs or the end of the garden, walk over to it as it stands and get in by a door of their own ("Taxi to work · there about 08:40"). It stands only a short while: one not at the kerb or at its door two minutes after it came is left and booked again. It drives out in time for the first one's start, round a junction or a street where cars are held up when that's not much longer than waiting it out, else waits at the edge of town for it to clear, a while at most, and its riders' times follow the way it comes; one held still half an hour where nothing gives turns back, those it was to pick up are booked again, and those it was taking home wait at work for another, or walk if it's a short way; it comes in by another way in where cars queue at its own, and runs into town by one way are spread a few minutes apart. Those already too late to walk (as a game begins, say) share a late run their way, and someone the taxi missed is booked again once, before they make their own way; a rider's leaving home, and their free time before it, follows the taxi as it runs late. Home again it puts them down at their kerb ("Taxi home"), and those with a long walk home from work go by it too. Visits and outings out of walking reach go by taxi as well, for those with no car or bike of their own to go by, when it beats walking there: they wait at home till it's near, it puts them down at the place's kerb, and a taxi back comes when their stay is over; those off to the same place, or home from it, about the same time share one, and the taxi's way counts in the hour and a half an outing may be away and in being home by 23:00. In place of the bus 0.7.0 was to have: a Big world's bus stops (ways out on foot until now) are gone, and riding there is by taxi; the shelters at the ends of the rows of houses are taxi ranks now (one a street at most, none within 60 m of another), the Market Square's too, and the taxi is drawn yellow with a chequered band and a TAXI sign.
- **Lifts from the neighbours:** someone off to work or school with no seat in their own household's car gets a lift from a friend next door (friendship 30 or more) whose car stands on its driveway within 25 m of their front door and who sets off at about the same time, when there's a seat free: they walk over, get in by a door of their own and ride along, and come home with the driver, or make their own way if the driver works late.
- **A host who offers a bite to eat:** a quarter of an hour after a guest comes in, a free host asks if they'd like something (*Offer a bite to eat*), and again every two hours or so, only with something at home to bring; a hungry guest says yes more readily. The host takes the snack (or whatever home has that a guest could have, toast say) out, carries it over and hands it to them; the guest sits down with it, the plate is the host's to wash up, and the story tells it ("Ada brought Bo a snack"). Whoever eats with company, at home with a guest or at a host's, sits down beside them, within talking range, so the two talk over the meal.
- **Things asked for, set up where you can see them:** a yes to "Mika would like to buy a cookbook lectern" no longer makes it appear somewhere in the house unnoticed. It's paid for and comes as a parcel to the front door: Mika picks it up, carries it in both hands to where it goes, puts it together there (15 minutes), and it springs into place as things bought in Build mode do, the camera looking on; the story says who set it up. Whoever asked does it as soon as they're home and free (a grown-up does it for a child); after two hours any grown-up at home may, and after a day it's set up for them anyway. With no room for it, it goes back to the shop and the money comes back. Neighbours nobody plays still buy for themselves at once, now only with the next week's bills left over. Build mode's catalog still places things at once.
- **Promotions after a working week:** a shift filled 2.5 % of the performance bar at every grade, so a promotion took 19 to 31 workdays. Now a worker in a fine mood is promoted after about five workdays at first (four when happy) and seven to ten near the top; an unhappy one clearly later, though a shift worked in the worst mood no longer takes any of the bar away (only a missed one does). A shift started more than half an hour late counts half. The next grade's skills still hold a full bar back. The Career tab says when the next promotion comes at this pace ("about 3 more workdays"), or that it waits on the skills shown.
- **Visitors let in:** a visitor knocking while someone awake is at home is let in: answering the door comes before the next step of a routine, and a busy host is waited for. Residents no longer set off on a visit hours away or to a house where everyone is asleep: a visit they choose, or are invited to on the phone, is one they can reach within 45 minutes, in visiting hours, with time to stay; it lasts from when they're let in. Out of walking range they go in the household's car when they drive it, else on a bike or e-scooter of theirs, else by the taxi (above) where it beats the walk: they ride there ("Cycling to Corner Café"), leave it on free ground by the place, clear of its paths and its door, where it stands while they're in, and ride it home; only somewhere near is walked. The cook holds a knife, a wooden spoon, a turner or a whisk and stirs the pot that is then carried away, what they carried in goes down on a cool free spot, and food eaten by hand is bitten from the hand. The cookers leave their hobs and grates free for the cook's food, their knobs turn by the burner that lights, the toaster, blender, stand mixer and espresso machine move while used, and the held food looks as it's served (a salad, a barbecue and a tasting plate, a stack of dirty plates). Walking and driving keep their pace, so the same walk takes a little less of the day than before. A Big world keeps up at the fastest speed: planning that was worked out again every moment for what hadn't changed (a taxi's kerb, the way out of town) is kept.
- **Big towns keep moving:** in a big town, traffic out of sight no longer jams. It backs up slowly into clear room, round the corner it came by or into a driveway's mouth, and goes another way, or waits out of the way while the others go by. A work taxi stuck in view never vanishes from the road: it turns round and drives off out of town. Cosy and normal towns, drawn whole, get the same.

- **Walks no longer jump at the start and the end:** at speeds 4 and 5 a walk's first and last step snapped, because only steps that moved in both snapshots were smoothed. A step that starts or ends standing now glides too.
- **Retirees aren't told they're "between jobs"** or offered job suggestions: they have a pension and can still browse for work.
- **Progress bars no longer make the browser lay out the page every frame:** the skills, needs, career and goal bars animate a transform instead of their width.
- **Licence links show the licence:** the voice, dictionary and language-model licences end in `.txt`, so Credits' links open them instead of downloading them.
- **Cross-origin isolation is retried:** the one-time reload flag is cleared once the page is isolated, so a page whose service worker was later evicted tries again on the next load.
- **Interface:** right-clicking the game no longer opens the browser's menu (except in text fields); with an object's or a resident's menu open the camera still turns, pans and zooms; the top bar, the HUD and the menus fit small windows and large interface scales, and the new-game screens stack on narrow windows. A new game's first story event shows in the feed.
- **Building:** nothing stands off its tile through a wall, straight or diagonal; only curtains hang over a window; things stand with their backs on the wall's face, not in it; every kitchen worktop is 0.95 m high, so a run of counters lines up.

### Development
- **Every push and pull request is checked:** a new `ci.yml` runs clippy, the simulation's tests and the sample-house and sample-world tests. The release build and Pages deploy stay in `pages.yml`.
- **The tools are Rust:** `cargo art` builds objects in Blender, `cargo check-glb` gates every model file (size, triangles, draws, clips, far versions), and `cargo content`, `cargo characters` and `cargo tex` replace the Python scripts. Python is left only inside Blender. How to build an object is in `docs/design/objects.md` and `tools/art/blender/README.md`.
- **Checking in a browser:** `tools/bench/check.mjs` takes the model gallery, the clips bench (`/clips.html`) and buy-and-use pictures headless with its own server; the simulation's tests write named game states (`DUMP_STATES=1`, opened with `?state=<name>`); `?debug` adds `__sims()`, `__addFunds`, `__camera` and following.
- **The generated-house and generated-world tests can't pass vacuously:** they require `HOUSES`/`WORLDS`, and CI makes the sample files first.
- **The benchmark can measure the interface:** `tools/bench/render.mjs --hud 1` (the default is `0`, the renderer alone).
- **Crash stacks from the site can be read:** CI's wasm artifact is now the site's own optimisations with the name section kept (`wasm-opt --names`), not the pre-optimisation wasm with different function numbers. `wasm-opt`'s results are cached between CI runs.

### Simulation
- **Every object keeps an id of its own for life:** commands, the view, the renderer and saves name objects by a stable id instead of their place in the list, so selling, moving or undoing one thing (or moving a whole room) can't retarget another, and a menu left open on something sold does nothing.
- **Goals nobody can reach are refused at once:** the lot's walkable regions are labelled once per change to the house, and a resident asking for a way to another region is turned down before the search starts. No more flooding the whole town for a bed behind a wall.
- **People are remembered too:** a resident who couldn't get to someone they meant to talk to or join doesn't try again for half a second; things and places keep the content's retry time.
- **Walkers go round a change, or give up:** when a wall, door, stair or thing placed in their way changes the route they were walking, they work out a new one from where they stand. If there is none, they stop, and remember it if they chose it themselves.
- Looks, repairs and crops no longer rebuild what walkers can reach or the objects-by-plot index; only changes to walking do, and rooms are rebuilt only when the lot's layout changes.

### Objects
- **Ice cream from the freezer:** the fridge, built-in fridge and French-door fridge have "Get some ice cream", so their freezer doors and drawer open for a use instead of carrying a clip nobody played. The other modelled parts without a use stay decorative, documented in `docs/objects/open.md`.

### Settings
- **Settings are versioned and migrated in order.** Values from a hand-edited, truncated or older file are put back in range (numbers, switches and choices) instead of being used as they are.

### Voices
- **Voices choose where they run:** Settings › Voices › Runs on is Auto, Graphics card or Processor. Auto uses the graphics card when WebGPU is there, starts on the processor when it isn't, and steps down from a card that cannot start the voices or turns out too slow, with a non-blocking message. A manual choice is never overridden. The processor keeps up with speech at about 2.5× real time, so a laptop without a usable graphics card can still hear its residents.
- **Voices are about a quarter faster on the graphics card:** the voice model's working data stays on the card between passes, and six passes replace eight, sounding the same. On an M3 Pro a warm line takes about 185 ms instead of 233.

### Graphics
- **Low draws far less:** the village orbit by day falls from 447 draws and 5.1 M triangles a frame to 293 and 1.2 M, and from 12 long tasks to none (M3 Pro proxy, 1920×1080). The quilts and held things draw only their live rows instead of the whole town's capacity, the street uses its far models, the street and residents cast no sun shadows on Low, and one merged shadow caster replaces the lot's furniture templates.
- **Less drawn everywhere:** props nobody holds aren't drawn, a reflection draws only what its glass shows and only the residents in it, and the graphics-preset switch no longer keeps the old renderer in memory.

### Compatibility
- **Saves:** the save format is version 29 (0.2.0 wrote 23, 0.1.0 wrote 17); saves from 0.2.0 and 0.1.0 load and play on. Their objects get stable ids, their styles are dropped (every object shows its own model, in its colours as made) and lamps start switched on. A 0.2.0 build can't open a 0.3.0 save.
- **Runs on:** desktop Chrome, Edge, Brave or Safari with WebGPU; other browsers use the WebGL2 fallback. The language model needs WebGPU; voices run on the processor without it. Tested on Apple Silicon Macs in Chrome. There is no frame-rate target for particular machines: Low is for older laptops, best effort, and hasn't been measured on Intel graphics.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-10-13

**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

In this release babies are cared for, meals are cooked from start to finish, everyone carries a phone and some houses have basements, in worlds of eight kinds. Residents can speak with new voices and, if you like, in their own words, written by a small language model on your computer. Before the release the whole game went through a review of animations, objects, bugs, performance and release readiness, and this release fixes what it found (details in `docs/release-0.2.0-review.md`).

### New
- **Babies:** a baby lies in the crib, curled up asleep or kicking awake, and cries when they need feeding, changing or a wash. Grown-ups pick them up for a cuddle, and at night a crying baby wakes one parent (partners take turns), or whoever is up comes. Nobody leaves a baby home alone: when everyone has to be at work, a babysitter comes for the day ($40), or a grandparent looks after them. A child who outgrows the crib gets a bed: neighbours buy one, and you're told when your own child has none.
- **Cooking:** meals are made from start to finish: fetch the ingredients, prepare, cook, serve, sit down at the table together, and wash up after. Nine recipes, from a sandwich to a tasting menu, a barbecue feast and pie. Food goes off, dirty plates make a room dirty, and "Clear away" scrapes, washes and puts away. Residents wash their hands after the toilet and eat snacks at the table.
- **A phone for everyone** from teens up: text, call, invite someone over, ask to come over, or scroll. A Phone tab shows contacts closest first and who is free, asleep or at work, and greys out what can't be done, with the reason. The phone box is gone from the market square.
- **Basements:** some generated houses on medium and large lots come with a basement down the stairs (a games room, a gym, a workshop, a laundry or a storeroom), lit by its own lamps. Build down there yourself too.
- **A new world first:** New game starts by generating a world of eight kinds (small, medium or dense towns, a farm, a seaside village, a mountain hamlet, a lakeside town, a forest clearing), each from a seed with its own name, streets and landscape. Towns get public places round the park (a playground, a market square, a café, a grocer, a bakery), and the streets are dressed.
- **Resident voices on the graphics card** (experimental, off by default): residents speak with Supertone's Supertonic 3 on WebGPU, about 28 times faster than real time on an M3 Pro. Ten voices, each resident with their own pitch, depth and speed, shaped in the household creator; children from the higher voices. About 217 MB, downloaded once. It replaces KittenTTS.
- **Residents in their own words** (optional): Settings › Voices › What residents say › Language model. A small language model, Liquid AI's LFM2.5 350M, runs on your graphics card and writes each line from who the resident is and what's going on, one to five sentences; lines show in the speech bubble too. About 259 MB, downloaded once.
- **Residents made in code:** new bodies for every life stage, faces with expressions and lip sync, hair, clothes and animations, all generated by the game's own tools. A build slider next to height.
- **Furniture:** 196 more things to buy, with tables and chairs apart, chairs that pull up to tables, and a Tabletop category of small things that stand on tops, plus 28 things for the town. Resize anything (whole tiles, or to the centimetre with Shift), drag furniture anywhere on its tiles, and make wide windows and double doors. Mirrors reflect the room (on High). Every model is built from primitives; no downloaded models are left.
- **Homes:** generated houses are furnished (dining sets, counter runs, kitchen islands, things on tops), have more and smaller rooms, and light every room at night. The lights go out in a room when everyone in it is asleep.
- **Everyday life:** residents look after their needs before they run out, sleep one proper night and rarely nap, take breaks at work, and want things of their own (they save up and ask before buying). Guests keep their hosts company, may use the toilet and say goodbye before they go home. A third and a fourth resident can join a conversation. Residents keep to the street instead of cutting through gardens.
- **Interface:** two modes, Live and Build (Furnish, Construct and Home in one dock), with Creation as a step of the new game. First-use tips instead of explanations always on screen.
- **Sound:** showers, taps, washing up, baths and the washing machine sound like real water.

### Stability and saves
- **A crash screen** instead of a silent freeze: if the game hits an error it says so ("Your last save is safe."), offers to reload, and can save a debug report. If only drawing or the interface broke, it offers Save and reload. After a graphics reset (a driver update, a GPU switch) the game offers Save and reload too, and if WebGPU won't start it draws with WebGL instead.
- **Damaged and too-new saves:** a save that can't be opened no longer blocks Continue. One the game finds damaged is removed with a short notice. One from a newer version, or one this version fails to load, is kept and marked ("This version couldn't open it"): Continue skips it, Load game can still export it, and a later version tries again.
- **Saves you can keep:** Load game can export any save as a file and import it again, and the game asks the browser to keep its saves.
- **Autosaves per game:** each game keeps its own two latest autosaves, so one game no longer overwrites another's. The game also autosaves when you leave the tab, and says so if an autosave fails. "Game saved" only shows once the save is really stored.
- **Long games:** from game day 1167 (about 7.8 hours at the fastest speed) births, ageing and deaths used to stop. They don't any more.
- **Furniture edits are safe:** selling or moving furniture no longer crashes the game or sends a resident to the wrong thing, a held key no longer turns the wrong object, and selling a fruit bowl or a rug leaves the dinner on the table.
- **After an update:** a tab left open across an update offers to reload instead of hanging, and a new release no longer mixes with files cached from the last one.

### Graphics
- **Graphics is Low, Medium or High,** and Medium, the game's intended look, is everyone's default. Low is for older laptops: simpler shadows, edges and lamps, half the lawn's tufts and flowers, and no shadows from the woods round the town. High adds sharper shadows and live mirrors. Ultra, Custom, the fine-tune switches and ambient occlusion are gone.
- **Resolution: Auto** draws the screen's own pixels up to about 1920×1080 and scales up beyond, so 4K and Retina screens run about as fast as a 1080p one; it follows browser zoom and a move to another screen. Custom draws 50–100 %.
- **The frame rate cap no longer judders:** frames are paced in whole display refreshes.
- The WebGL2 switch is under Settings › Performance › Troubleshooting.

### Performance and load time
Measured on an M3 Pro in Chrome, 1920×1080:
- **Faster start:** the menu appears about 1.4 s sooner for returning players (2.83 → 1.42 s with a save, warm cache). The game's data arrives in one download instead of about 60, the sky's lighting starts downloading with the page, and returning players load the game's scripts from their browser. A first visit at 10 Mbit/s reaches the menu in 3.14 s instead of 3.44.
- **Smaller download:** the simulation and voice WebAssembly are optimised and stripped (620 KB smaller, 44 KB gzipped).
- **Lighter drawing, the same picture:** lamps that are off by day cost nothing, grass, flowers and woods out of view aren't drawn, and the sun's shadows are drawn only from what stands near your lot and only when something in them moves. Medium by day on an M3 Pro (WebGPU, 1920×1080) now takes about 5.4 ms of graphics-card work a frame instead of 8.6 before these savings: about 154 fps instead of 96 without a cap. Medium also uses 141 MB of render targets a frame instead of 216.
- **Nothing drawn behind menus:** behind the pause menu, Settings, Load game, Credits and the planner the game stops drawing until you close them, and the menus' town is drawn at about 30 fps. High's mirrors take video memory only while they reflect.
- **Lighter interface:** progress and need bars no longer make the browser lay out the page on every frame, speech bubbles are placed without forcing a layout, goal ideas are worked out only when a plan changes, bird songs are made off the main thread, furniture for other lots is built in small steps, and the light changing at dusk no longer stalls a frame. With the processor slowed 3× to stand in for an older laptop, at speed 5 a frame now takes 7.2 ms of the main thread on average instead of 7.9 before the release review (9.4 instead of 12.4 for the slowest one in twenty), with no long stalls, and the town behind the menus about half the main thread's time it took. F3's "Busy (frames)" now counts the browser's layout and paint too.
- **Smoother motion:** residents glide instead of jumping at the fastest speeds, jog instead of sliding, face where they're going, and keep their animation when something in the house changes; the camera and clouds no longer jump after a hidden tab.

### Fixes
- **Residents:** a resident too tired to talk no longer pulls others into chats all night; nobody follows someone across town for a chat; practising a skill no longer stutters on and off; worn out with every bed taken, residents no longer give up the toilet at once; residents no longer use something that broke on the way, nor the neighbours' things when sent; two housemates no longer both walk over to the same repair; newborns are born at age 0; a family moving into town brings a grown-up child, not a baby left at the door; new residents start with the skills their traits give.
- **Money:** a recipe given up after buying the ingredients gives the money back. Residents at retirement age (65) without a job now retire with a basic pension of $500 a week, and every pension from now on is at least that; a job you give a retiree sticks.
- **Kitchens:** buying a range cooker no longer sells the fridge, and a stove without a fridge no longer counts as food at home, so hungry residents order takeout instead of going round in circles.
- **Saves and crashes:** Load game lists a save's own household, not everyone in town, and no "Autosave failed" notice shows over the crash screen. A game in which building left a ceiling lamp without a ceiling opens again, without that lamp.
- **Input, interface and sound:** after Alt+Tab, Build mode no longer picks up the floor on a plain click; a wall or carried object no longer sticks to the mouse; held keys no longer flicker pause or panels; the arrow keys turn the camera as fast at any frame rate; reduce motion also stills the scene's decorative movement. News in the feed can't be clicked once it has faded, and with reduced motion on it shows for 10 seconds instead of not at all. A master volume, sound stops when the game is in the background ("Mute in background", on), and an option to pause when the window loses focus.
- **Content packs:** content that would break the game (a walking speed of 0, a resale price above 1, a recipe of 300 steps, an unknown tag) is refused with a clear message (`docs/content-packs.md`).

### Compatibility
- **Saves:** the save format is version 23 (0.1.0 wrote 17); saves from 0.1.0 and from the builds after it load and play on. Saves now remember the seed the game started from.
- **Settings:** a Low graphics setting from 0.1 stays Low; everything else becomes Medium. Players who never changed it had High's settings, so they now get Medium, which has no live mirrors. Resolution starts on Auto.
- **Runs on:** desktop Chrome, Edge, Brave or Safari with WebGPU; other browsers use the WebGL2 fallback. Voices and the language model need WebGPU. Tested on Apple Silicon Macs in Chrome. On older Intel laptops (HD 620-class graphics) Medium's target is 30 fps, but it hasn't been measured on Intel hardware: scaled from Mac measurements, Medium is estimated at about 10–21 fps there by day (8–13 at night), so it probably falls short until someone measures it on a real laptop. Low there is best effort.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-10-10

**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

The first release: a home-building game with a living simulation, in the browser. You build and furnish a home; its residents live in it on their own while you watch. (Earlier development builds were called Littlelives, numbered 0.3–0.20, and MIT-licensed; their notes are in the git history. Saves, settings and downloads from them carry over.)

### In this release
- **A living town:** create a neighbourhood (town size, neighbour households), then play one of the households who already live there or make your own in the 3D household creator (gender, who they're attracted to, skin, hair, clothes, traits, perks, starting bonds, voice) and move into a vacant house. Neighbour houses are generated, with one or two storeys. Everyone in town lives, works, socialises, visits friends and falls in and out of love on their own; the rest of the town keeps simulating while only your lot is drawn.
- **Watching:** left alone, the camera follows what happens at home (conversations, fights, first kisses, guests, someone using what you just built). - **Buy mode (V):** about 210 things to buy across Kitchen, Bathroom, Bedroom, Kids, Living, Office, Fitness, Hobbies, Decor, Lighting, Outdoor and Garden, shown as 3D pictures, with styles (Modern, Cozy, Minimal), upgrades (★ quality), things on walls and ceilings, rugs, lamps that light their room at night, and over 50 plants residents tend and harvest.
- **Build mode (B):** walls (straight and diagonal, full or half height, with coverings), rooms, paint, doors and windows in styles, stairs to a second storey, and walls up, cut away or down (W).
- **Resident voices (experimental, off by default):** the resident you're looking at, and conversations you start, are spoken in English by KittenTTS nano running on your computer (about 32 MB, downloaded once): eight real voices, mixed so every resident sounds their own, children higher and smaller, about 2,350 lines per item, interaction, mood and thought. Voices pause for the session if this computer can't keep up.
- **Sound:** birds by species singing in bouts through the day, crickets at night, and the sounds of things in use at home.
- **Saves, settings and tools:** save and load, Settings (graphics quality, audio, controls), a performance overlay (F3) and debug reports (F8). Content packs can add objects, art and voice lines (see `docs/content-packs.md`).
- **Runs on:** desktop Chrome, Edge, Brave or Safari with WebGPU; other browsers use the WebGL2 fallback. The simulation is Rust compiled to WebAssembly in its own worker.
