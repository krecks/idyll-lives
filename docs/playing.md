# Playing Idyll Lives

Everything the game does, as a player meets it: starting a game, Live and Build, jobs, skills, money, the garden, the controls and the graphics settings. The short version is in the [README](../README.md#playing).

## A new game

Create a world (a small, medium or dense town, a farm, a seaside village, a mountain hamlet, a lakeside town or a forest clearing, each generated with its own name, roads and landscape: a winding high street, a crescent round the village green, a crossroads with crooked lanes, a lane through the fields, a road along the shore, a switchback up the valley or a ring round the clearing, its lanes joined up into blocks with a court or two; every house stands on a lot cut to fit it, its garden snug, roomy or big as the world goes) → choose the neighbour households → either play one of the households who already live there (**Play** on its card or its lot; they keep their home, jobs and friendships) or make your household in **Creation** (drag to turn a resident, scroll to zoom to the face; gender, who they're attracted to, skin, hair, clothes, traits, perks, starting bonds) → choose a vacant house (and how much garden it has, where the neighbours leave room) → move in. Everyone in town lives, works, socialises and falls in and out of love on their own, your household included; you build their home and watch. The town round the camera is drawn as you look about, and the lot it settles on in full (its rooms, its things, who's indoors); a pill under the top bar names the lot and the hamlet you're looking at. The rest of the town keeps simulating in the background.

## Live and Build

- **Two modes:** **Live** (L) is the household and the world in motion; **Build** (B) is the home: furnish it, build it and see how its rooms are doing (time stands still). **Creation** belongs to the new game; a home built first gets its family later from the empty card in the household strip.
- **Live:** leave the game alone and the camera follows what happens at home (conversations, fights, first kisses, someone using what you just built, guests); any input takes it back. The household strip at the bottom shows everyone at a glance; click a resident to look closer (and step in from their panel), **F** follows them, **H** shows the whole house, **J** opens the journal (your household's own story: *Our home*, or only its *Milestones*; the feed in the corner keeps to your household's news too). A resident's panel shows their needs, what they're doing and their plans. Speeds 4 and 5 are time-lapse; quiet hours (everyone asleep or at work) skip ahead.
- **Planner (P):** each resident's week: drop activities on a calendar (train a skill at 18:00 Mon/Wed/Fri, sleep 23–7, cook dinner) and set life goals (get a job, get promoted, find love, save money). Plans are a strong nudge, not orders: urgent needs and personality can win, and each block shows whether it was kept and why not. Household blocks apply to everyone. Activities with nowhere to do them become wishes that open the catalog. When a resident asks to buy something and you say yes, it's paid for and comes as a parcel to the front door: the one who asked carries it in and sets it up where it goes (a grown-up for a child; after two hours anyone grown-up at home, after a day it's set up for them), and it springs into place.

- **Jobs (1000 of them):** residents look for work on their own (and may quit or be let go); you can also pick a job yourself. 25 categories (journalism, detective work, fire & rescue, social media, IT, TV, mechanics, finance, …) × 4 tracks × 10 grades **A–J**. The grade sets the base pay per hour ($12/h at A up to $60/h at J) and the skill level the job expects; each track pays a little more or less than the field's average, so a software developer earns more than an IT support technician at the same grade. Open the job board from the *Career* tab, filter by category, grade or what your resident can take, and join any position you qualify for — one level short is allowed *on probation*. Promotions need a full performance bar **and** the next grade's skills, and pay a bonus of half a week at the new grade. A worker in a fine mood fills the bar in about five workdays at the first grades (four when happy) and seven to ten near the top; a bad mood slows it, a shift started more than half an hour late counts half and a missed one takes some off. The *Career* tab says about how many more workdays the next promotion takes at this pace, or that it waits on the skills shown.
- **Skills:** Intelligence, Strength, Endurance, Charisma, Creativity, Technology, Handiness, Cooking, Writing, Perception, Dexterity, Business and Gardening (0–10). Work trains the job's skills; at home, residents practise with things you buy (treadmill, weight bench, chess table, computer desk, easel, mirror, workbench, telescope, piano, gourmet cooking, studying, tending the garden, pruning a bonsai). Traits give starting skills and faster learning in some.
- **Cars:** most households have a car on their driveway. Only its owners drive it, and only grown-ups (18 and up); whoever else in the household leaves for work or school at the same time rides along, each by a door of their own. They leave early enough for the drive, so they're at work on time. It drives off along the street and brings them home after work (one finished first waits up to an hour for the others). A parked car (and a garage) can't be walked through or built on; a car on the move stops for anyone in its way. A car moves house with its household. Build over its spot while it's out and it stays put, unused. Build a garage at the end of its driveway, its door towards the street, and the car parks in front of it, nose in (back on its old spot once the garage is gone). **Lifts:** someone with no seat in their own household's car rides with a friend next door (friendship 30 or more) whose car stands within 25 m of their front door and who sets off at about the same time, when there's a seat free, and comes home with them (or makes their own way if the driver works late).
- **Bikes and e-scooters:** most households in a new town have a city bike for each teen or grown-up (up to three), a kids' bike for each child of four or more and now and then an e-scooter, standing in the garden; Furnish sells them under *Outdoor › Bikes & scooters*. Off to work or school with no seat in a car, a resident rides the fastest free one at home that fits them (the city bike three times walking pace, the kids' bike a little over twice, the e-scooter four times) when the ride out of town is over 60 m, and rides it home to its spot again; their way is timed by the ride. The household strip and the town map say who's cycling. (0.7.0's bus is gone: a shared work taxi takes its place.)
- **Workweek:** the standard week is set by the job (mostly Mon–Fri). Skills change it: on probation residents work an extra day, two levels above the requirements a day less (*Flexible*), four levels above two days less (*Expert*). The weekly salary stays the same, so a shorter week pays more per shift.
- **Money:** household funds are in dollars. A home is bought on moving in, paid from the household's savings: its price comes from its lot, its house and what's in it, and the savings from the residents' ages and positions (a young person can't buy a villa); a home they can't afford can't be moved into, but an empty lot (the land alone) can. Rent and bills are charged every Sunday at noon: rent depends on the lot size, and bills grow with the value of everything the household owns and with how many live there (food and household goods, $150 a person). A working household saves a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars a week: a new room every week or two. Items cost nothing per use; only takeout, deliveries and similar purchases do. A household that can't pay goes into debt and its residents worry about money.
- **Day and night:** Residents are active by day and sleep through the night (couples share a double bed), get up early for an early shift, eat and wash in the morning, socialise in the evening and use the bathroom before bed. Tiredness builds up faster when they stay up late.
- **Build › Furnish (V):** time stands still while you shop. The catalog shows every item as a 3D picture; point at one and move across its preview to turn it around. Furniture and training equipment cost money, and what you buy decides what your residents can do: point at an item to see the needs it fills (and how fast), the skills it trains, the feelings it can give, whether a skill makes it better (a skilled cook gets more out of the stove), any cost per use and how many residents fit. Pick a category along the top of the catalog, or search by name, need, skill or personality. Pictures, curtains, shelves and wall lamps hang on walls (they turn to face away from the wall you point at), ceiling lamps and fans hang from a room's ceiling and rugs lie on the floor: they take no floor space, so a picture can hang over the sofa and a table can stand on a rug. Things on a wall go with their wall when walls are cut away, and a wall something hangs on can't be torn down until it's moved. Lamps light their room at night: residents switch a dark room's lamps on as they come in and off as the last one leaves or goes to sleep, and a click on a lamp turns it on or off (a room without windows is dark by day too). Every object is built in Blender, and many have moving parts that work as they're used: cupboard doors and drawers open, the fridge opens for food and its freezer for ice cream, clocks tell the game's time, and the doors of the house swing open for whoever goes through them and shut behind them. Some furniture takes a **colour** of your choice (marked "Any colour" on its card): pick one of the swatches or a colour of your own while it's in hand, or recolour what you own for free; *Every part…* opens the colour studio, which colours each part on its own (a fridge's body, handles and inside) with the item up close. Colours never change price or quality. Click an owned object to pick it up: it follows the pointer until a click puts it down anywhere on its tiles (Esc puts it back), and its panel upgrades, rotates, recolours or sells it (60% back). Furniture can't be resized for now (moving parts such as a fridge's doors don't follow a resize yet); something resized in an older save keeps its size, and "Reset size" takes it back. With the Door or Window tool, click a door or window to resize it with its blue arrows (Shift: its sill and top to the centimetre): side by side, windows make one wide window and doors a double door.
- **Garden:** the *Garden* category (Trees · Shrubs & hedges · Flowers · Fruit & veg · Houseplants) has over 50 plants: oaks, maples, birches, cherry blossom, apple, magnolia, Japanese maple, weeping willow, spruce and cypress; roses, hydrangeas, lavender, hedges and topiary; beds of tulips, daffodils, daisies, sunflowers, poppies, lupins and more; vegetable patches, tomatoes, strawberries, herbs and pumpkins; and house plants from monstera to bonsai. Garden decor (bird bath, fountain, pond, gnome, lantern) is under *Outdoor*. Trees, beds and other garden things go outdoors only. Residents water, tend and trim them, smell the roses, relax in the shade and harvest a free meal; all of it trains **Gardening**, and good gardeners harvest more, feel proud of their beds and get Fresh herbs (better cooking for a while) from the herb planter. Gardening is the main skill of the Gardening & Horticulture career and helps farmers; Nature Lovers start with some and learn it faster. Birds live in the trees, bushes and hedges round the lot: tits, chaffinches, warblers, blackbirds and collared doves sit and sing in them, fly between the gardens, come down to the lawn to peck and fly up when someone comes near, and roost there at night; a garden with more trees and shrubs has more of them.
- **Upgrades:** instead of pricier versions, objects get better by upgrading. Upgrades are bought in Build › Furnish (click the object → *Upgrade to ★n*) and happen at once; each quality star makes everything the object gives 25% better (a better shower cleans faster, a better treadmill trains faster).
- **Build › Construct (C; Wall · Room · Fence · Gate · Garage · Porch · Stairs · Roof · Paint · Floor · Door · Window · Remove · Move · Blueprints):** Press on a corner and drag to draw a wall, with a live preview and its cost; release to build. Walls can be full or half height and come with a covering (paint colours, wallpaper, siding, brick, wood panelling, stone, tiles); the Paint tool re-covers the side of a wall you point at (drag along walls, or Shift-click for a whole room). Doors and windows come in styles (painted, oak, half-glazed and French doors; classic, cottage, picture, floor-length, high-light and transom windows); pick another style and click to replace one. Tearing down is free. Drag at about 45° and the wall runs diagonally, corner to corner across the tiles. Clicking a corner and then another also works. The Room tool draws all four walls of the rectangle you drag out. Put doors and windows into walls (straight or diagonal) with a click, and drag along walls with Remove to tear them down on your own lot. Esc or a right-click while drawing cancels. Windows let light in but not residents. The Stairs tool puts a staircase (1 × 4 m) in hand: it leads up to the storey above (build that first) and is climbed from the front; R turns it, click your stairs to move them and Delete sells them. A diagonal wall costs 1.414 × a straight one per tile (it is √2 m long) and splits its tile into two halves that can belong to different rooms; nobody can stand on that tile and furniture can't go there (a diagonal door tile can be walked through). Draw a room's walls first, then add its door; only a resident or furniture can't be shut in. Fence and Gate fence a garden (leave a gate so nobody is shut in); Garage builds one garage on your lot ($2,500): click the driveway for one at its end beside the house (or use the panel's button), or anywhere outdoors on the ground (R turns its door); it can't go over walls, fences, furniture, residents or the car's spot, nor shut anyone in, and a click on it (or Delete) takes it down for $1,500, like furniture sold; Porch puts a porch over an outside door on the ground floor ($600: a landing, two posts, a gabled roof in your house's colours and a lamp; it needs open ground in front of the door with nothing and nobody where its posts go, nobody walks through its posts or puts furniture there, it can't shut anyone in or cut off the way to the door, and the door takes it with it when it goes), or builds one of your own from pieces: posts ($60, nobody walks through them), gabled canopies 1 to 3 tiles across and up to 6 deep ($90 a tile, with a lamp under the ridge at the front) and step platforms up to 4 × 4 ($30 a tile), turned with R and sized in the panel; a click on one (or Delete) takes it down, sold back like furniture; Roof picks the roof's shape and colour for the whole house; Move drags a room with its walls, doors, windows, floor and furniture; Blueprints save the house you built or build a saved one on a cleared lot.
- **Visits:** neighbours visit friends on their own. On their own or invited over the phone, they set off only when the way takes 45 minutes at most and gets them there in visiting hours (on their own, only to a home with someone awake to let them in); a visitor is answered whenever a grown-up of the house is home and awake, a busy one being waited for. A while after a guest comes in, a free host offers them a bite to eat (*Offer a bite to eat*) and brings it over, and whoever eats with company sits down beside them. Open the town map (**M**) to send your resident to visit anyone; the view follows them (Esc lets go), and while they're on their way the household strip and the map say where to and when they'll be there. On the map, the wheel or + and − zoom, a drag pans, and a double-click (or a lot picked and *Look here*) takes the camera there; the lots also take Tab and Enter.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Left-click a resident / their card, Tab (Shift+Tab: back) | Look at them (their panel); Tab only while no button or field has the focus |
| Double-click a resident's card | Follow them |
| Left-click another resident while looking at one of yours | Social menu (chat, flirt, argue, …) with success chances |
| Left-click an object | Interaction menu |
| Left-click the floor | Walk there (while looking at one of yours); otherwise close the panel |
| F · H · J · P | Follow the resident · whole house · journal · planner |
| Left-drag (or middle-drag) / right-drag (or Ctrl+left-drag) / wheel | Rotate / pan / zoom |
| Arrow keys, + / − | Rotate (Ctrl+arrows: pan), zoom, once the view has the focus (click it) |
| Left-drag on the lot (Walls, Remove) | Draw or tear down walls along the drag (at 45°: diagonal); right-click or Esc cancels. The camera then turns with middle-drag or by dragging off the lot |
| Space, 0–5 | Pause, game speed (4, 5: time-lapse) |
| W | Walls up → cutaway → down |
| M | Town map (who's home, visiting) |
| L / B | Live / Build (B again goes back to Live) |
| V / C / O | Build › Furnish / Build › Construct / Build › Home (rooms tinted by how they're doing; repairs, adopting) |
| R, Delete | Rotate / sell the object in hand or selected (Build › Furnish) |
| E (or hold Alt/Option) | Eyedropper (Build): click a wall, floor or object to pick up its look |
| Page Up / Page Down | Storey up / down |
| Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z) | Undo, redo (Build) |
| Esc | Steps back: put down what's in hand, leave Build, close the journal, stop following, close a resident's panel — then the pause menu (save, load, settings, quit); with the pause menu open, closes it |
| F3 | Performance overlay (with *Save debug report*) |
| F8 | Save a debug report: screenshot, game state, settings, the build's version and commit, and recent errors. With `bun run dev` it goes to `debug-reports/<time>/` in the project; open `?debugReport=<time>` to load it with the same camera. Other builds download it as a file, which any build can play: paste it into Settings › Interface › Diagnostics › Game state. |

With `bun run dev`, every page load also keeps a **dev play log**, `dev-logs/<time>.jsonl` in the project (`dev-logs/latest.txt` names the newest; `?devlog=0` turns it off). One JSON object a line: the browser, the screen and the GPU; every console warning and error; the screen shown and every settings change; a game opened and closed; every 5 seconds what is drawn (frame rate, frame time, the resolution drawn, draw calls) and, in a game, the simulation's steps, memory and the game's time and speed; the size drawn whenever it changes; every stall of 100 ms or more between frames; and every step timed on the way (`perf`: start-up, the menus' town, each batch of portraits, a game's preparation and each lot its warm-up builds), so a slow load can be read step by step afterwards. Code: `web/src/debug/devlog.ts`.

The game's keys rest while the planner, the job board, the pause menu or a settings window is open, and while Ctrl, ⌘ or Alt is held (but for undo and redo), so the browser's own shortcuts work as usual. Reduce motion (Settings › Interface, or the system's setting) cuts the camera's flights to a jump.

## Graphics

Settings › Performance has three presets. Medium is the default and the game's intended look, with one mirror reflecting the room, drawn again when something in it changes, and the two lamps nearest what you look at casting shadows at night; Low is for older laptops (FXAA, 1024 shadows, the four lamps nearest the biggest rooms, none of them casting shadows, half the lawn's tufts and flowers, the street's far models, no sun shadows from the woods, the street or the residents, the lot's furniture as one merged shadow, its mirror's reflection without multisampling and drawn at most 10 times a second, and no ambient clips such as a ceiling fan's); High adds 4096 shadows, four lamps casting shadows and two mirrors drawn every frame. Shading is Soft (the default: wide, gentle light) or Classic, the shading the game had before. Resolution is 720p, 1080p (the default), 1440p or 2160p lines of pixels, never more than the screen has, and the frame rate cap (60 by default) is paced in whole display refreshes. Every knob, its cost, and the benchmark: [design/graphics.md](design/graphics.md).
