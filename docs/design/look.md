# Look

*Design notes for how Idyll Lives looks. Inspired by classic life-simulation games, described
here on our own terms. Status: 0.7.0 and what has landed since. This is the Medium graphics preset, everyone's default; Low
and High change a few things (softer or sharper shadows, fewer lamps, half the lawn, no cloud shadows, no shadows
from the woods, the street or the residents and the street's far models on Low, two mirrors
reflecting every frame and the miniature focus on High), listed in
[graphics.md](graphics.md).*

Changes to the look are judged at the camera a game is played from (24 m from the lot, and the street
and town views), from pictures of the same scene side by side, and the owner chooses
(`tools/bench/play.mjs`, [tools/bench/README.md](../../tools/bench/README.md)): at that distance a pixel is
16 mm, so what decides the picture is the lawn, the light and the colour, not what is small.

The world is drawn in physically based shading (**Classic**, the default since 2026-11-10, the owner's
decision: the residents and the models should look real, and skin, grass and wood keep their own colours
in it) with golden-hour light and blue moonlit nights. The soft, painterly shading that was the default
until then (**Soft**) is still in Settings. All art is original or CC0.

Code: `web/src/render/look.ts` holds the look as data, in the units it was tuned in up to 0.3.2 (a
sun or a lamp of strength 1 lights a white surface to 1/π); `web/src/render/pc/*` applies it on
PlayCanvas (its units converted in `lightUnits.ts`, see [graphics.md](graphics.md#the-renderer)),
and `shaders/softLook.ts` holds the game's light for both shadings and, with Soft chosen, redraws every
lit surface in the soft shading below (Settings, Performance, Shading).

## Goals

- **A warm, lived-in neighbourhood.** Houses, gardens and people should read as a small,
  cared-for world: a warm, stylised-realistic look rather than photorealism or flat cartoon.
- **Time of day you can feel.** The look is driven by time-of-day keyframes (sun, sky fill,
  image-based light, horizon, zenith, exposure and lamps every few hours), so mornings, golden
  hour, blue hour and night each have their own mood.
- **Only the sun turns warm at sunset.** The sky fill and ambient light stay neutral to cool, and
  the horizon haze stays pale and desaturated. Shadows read blue, greens stay green, and only
  sunlit faces turn golden. The sun fades out around sunrise and sunset, so the switch between
  sun and moon never pops.
- **Warm lamps against cool nights.** At night a moonlight grade turns dark tones blue-grey
  (grass alone would otherwise read dark green) while lamps and lit windows stay warm.
- **Readable at a glance.** The dollhouse view has to show who is where and what they are doing,
  so clarity beats extra effects. There is no bloom (tried on 2026-11-11: at night's exposure only a
  threshold low enough to bloom the whole day picked out the lamps, and then faintly). The miniature
  focus, below, softens only the picture's far and near edges, and stays out wherever every tile is
  looked at.
- **Real surfaces by default, painted ones by choice.** Classic lights a surface as light falls on it.
  With Soft, light falls off widely and gently, shade takes the sky's tint instead of going black, and a
  quiet rim keeps silhouettes readable: closer to a hand-painted illustration than a photograph, without
  turning flat or cel-shaded.

## Light and grading

| Aspect | Choice |
|---|---|
| Light | Saturated warm sun on an arc, lowered (`sunHeight` 0.8) for long, dramatic shadows. A low peach sunrise, a golden (not red) late afternoon, an orange sunset only as the sun touches the horizon, then a blue dusk with lamps coming on. |
| Sun against shade | From mid-morning to late afternoon the sun is warm and strong (4 to 4.5, `#FFDCA8` to `#FFE4BC` to `#FFD294`) and the sky gives less light, and bluer, than its picture would (the image-based light at 0.61 to 0.66, a blue hemispheric fill of 0.22, `#7FA8FF`): the lawn in the sun is warm, a tree's or a cloud's shade on it is cooler and deeper, and a room lit through its windows is cool where the sun's patch on its floor is warm. The exposure is 5 to 7 % up for it, so the picture is as light as it was (measured: the median within 3 of 255). A bluer fill alone had hardly shown (2026-11-11): the sky's picture was most of the shade's light. |
| Nights | A blue moon (0.8) and sky fill (0.65) with a strong night grade (0.85), so nights are clearly blue but not murky, and a garden is dim, not near black; warm lamps (`#FFC27A`) and glowing windows. |
| Grading | ACES tone mapping, contrast 1.1, saturation +14. Split toning: warm highlights (hue 38) and cool shadows (hue 222), faint (a filter of 2 and 3 %; stronger, and a cooler sky fill by day, were tried on 2026-11-11 and hardly differed). |
| Effects | A light vignette (weight 0.8: the corners keep about three quarters of their light) and soft distance haze. |
| Miniature focus | The picture's far and near edges out of focus, as in a photograph of a model, so the eye rests on the home in the middle (`render/shared/tilt.ts`, drawn in the compose). What stays sharp is a band of the ground by its distance from the eye (0.85 to 1.16 of the camera's distance from what it looks at), so it follows the camera's tilt: the rows 0.34 to 0.72 of the picture at the camera a game opens with. The blur grows to 8 px (of a picture 1080 high) at the picture's edge. It fades out as the camera comes closer than 18 m (gone at 9 m), and stays out in Build mode, in the basement and in the menus' town. On with High by default, and anyone's to turn on or off (Settings, Performance, Miniature focus). |
| Cloud shadows | By day soft patches of shade drift over the land with the wind (1.3 m/s), 30 to 60 m across, most of the land in the sun between them, so the light is not the same from one edge of the picture to the other and the sunlit parts read as sunlit (`render/pc/cloudShadows.ts`). Under one only the sun is dimmed (by half in its middle); the sky's light stays, so a cloud's shade is cooler than the sun beside it. They are a small tiling map every lit surface reads where it stands (`shaders/clouds.ts`): no draw and no pass. None at night or in the low sun of early morning and evening (all of them from about 9 to 17 h), whose long shadows are the picture then. They hold still with Reduce motion, as the birds do, and in the menus' town, whose picture is held. Not on Low (`cloudShadows` in [graphics.md](graphics.md)). |
| Shadows | Soft, filtered shadows from one sun shadow map over a fixed area round the lot. Its size comes with the graphics preset: 2048 on Medium (the default), 1024 on Low, 4096 on High. |

## Shading

With **Soft** chosen, every lit PBR surface is drawn with the **soft look** (`render/pc/shaders/softLook.ts`, a shader
feature of the game's materials in GLSL and WGSL, for WebGPU and WebGL2). It keeps the light PBR computed — the sun and the lamps, with their cast
shadows — and redraws it as a wide, soft ramp in the light's own colour, so golden hour stays
golden, lamps stay warm and night stays dark. The environment (the sky, with its occlusion) fills
the shade at just over half, tinted cool, so shadows read blue-grey rather than black. A gentle
fresnel rim lifts silhouettes and a little extra saturation keeps the palette lively. There are no
ink outlines: the look is soft and illustrative, not cel-shaded.

It replaces the material's colour just before fog and image processing, so ACES, the exposure
and the night grade still apply, and it runs after the wall cutaway and the basements.
Metals shade in their base colour (PBR gives them no diffuse colour); unlit materials keep their own
look. Water, the sky and glass panes that keep their reflections keep their own materials on
purpose.

**Classic**, the default (Settings, Performance, **Shading**), draws the PBR shading as it is computed;
switching is live: every lit material (the game's and the preview studios') gets the
other define and its shaders compile again (`MaterialLibrary.setShading`). The item pictures in Furnish and the portraits are drawn in the chosen
shading and cached per shading; Furnish keeps its pictures between visits per app version, so a
change to the look's code bumps the picture store's version in `preview/items.ts`. On the dev
server `?look=soft|classic` overrides the setting.

## The world

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Houses | `house.ts` generates walls from the simulation layout: siding or brick per house, interior paint per room, a tiled wainscot in bathrooms, a stone plinth, corner boards, a frieze, baseboards, windows with muntins, shutters and sills, door casings, painted doors, and gable or hip roofs (34° pitch, 0.4 m overhang, closed gables; a course of cap shingles up each hip of a hip roof, 14 cm down either slope and a fifth darker, as the ridge's cap is: a hip was a bare seam between two tones). |
| Cutaway | Done on the GPU. Each wall vertex stores which view directions it hides, and the vertex shader lowers it to a 0.35 m stub; what stands above the stub (windows, door leaves, the wall over and beside an opening) goes, every vertex of it to one point, so it is hidden on every storey, not only below the ground. With an upper floor in view, the storeys below are cut away as they are on their own. A wall's top is the wall's own section, a mid grey (`HOUSE_LOOK.cap`, `#8D877E`), not the trim's off-white: from the play camera the tops of the cut walls are an outline round every room, and in white they were the brightest thing in the picture, a plan drawn over the home (a darker grey, tried on 2026-11-12, drew the plan in dark lines instead). A half wall's top is a ledge and stays white. Roofs show only with walls up and when not zoomed in. |
| Floors | A floor is not one tone from wall to wall (`HOUSE_LOOK` in `render/shared/house.ts`): it is darker at the foot of the walls (34 % less light at the wall, fading over 1.6 m, and warmer the darker it is: the floor beside it lights it), a little under its covering's own colour out in the room (95 %; at 87 % the rooms came out dull), palest in and before a doorway, where feet wear it (its own colour, over 1.7 m), and 7 % lighter and darker in patches a few metres across. So a room has a middle and edges, and the ways through a home show. By vertex, four quads a tile: no texture, no read, the same on every preset. |
| Walls indoors | A wall's face is not one tone either: 24 % darker at the floor, fading over 1.3 m up it, 28 % darker into a room's corner (over 0.9 m), and 6 % lighter and darker in soft patches, as plaster and paint are (`HOUSE_LOOK`). The owner found on 2026-11-11 that indoor whites were not too bright (about 150 of 255) but featureless; this gives them a gradient at the scale of a wall. The stub a cut wall leaves is its darker foot, so the cut-away walls are quieter too. By vertex, in rows up the wall: no read. |
| Night interiors | Every lamp gives warm light (clustered lighting, up to 32 lamps) on Medium and High; on Low only the four nearest the biggest rooms do, the storey in view first. Each lights only its own room, on every preset (a room per half of a tile split by a diagonal wall). A room with no lamp gets a dim fill. Lamps switch on and off (the player per lamp, residents per room as they come and go); a room whose lamps are all off goes dark at the windows. When everyone in a room is asleep its lights go out and its windows stop glowing, and a neighbour's windows glow only while one of its residents is home and up. Windows glow from outside at night only. |
| Contact shading | On Medium and High, things shade what they stand on or against: a soft dark edge where a wardrobe meets the floor, behind a sofa at the wall, under a counter's overhang (`contactShading` in [graphics.md](graphics.md)). It reaches 30 cm from where things touch, and shades the sky's light fully and the lamps' and the sun's by a third, so a lamplit room, whose lamps cast no shadows, still has depth without dark corners. Not on the lawn or the plants. |
| Reflections indoors | A glossy thing in a room of the lot in view (a tap, a shower's frame, a glazed tile, a polished floor) reflects the room, not the open sky: a soft picture of it, its walls and ceiling as bright as the light that reaches the thing (the sun through the windows, the lamps, the sky's fill) and its floor darker (`ROOM` in `shaders/envBrdf.ts`). So chrome is bright in a lit room and dark in a dark one, by day and by night, where it used to shine with the sky like a car in a field. Other houses' rooms, storeys not shown and everything outdoors reflect the sky. |
| Sunlight indoors | The sun comes in through windows and doorways only: walls cast in every wall mode under a shadow-only ceiling. A room without windows is dark by day, as a basement is, and its lamps light it day and night. |
| Garden | A calm green lawn (its blades close to the ground's own tone from root to tip, a little less saturated and less yellow than the world's lawn colours as stored: it is most of a play picture, and read as bright noise beside flat walls and furniture until 2026-11-11), foundation shrubs, lot trees, clouds and a sun glow in the sky. The broadleaved trees' crowns are sprays of leaves, cut-out cards from one atlas over dark cores (objects.md, "Leaf cards"): alpha-tested, never blended, their edges softened by the multisampling on Medium and High and cut clean on Low; far off, in the far woods and on Low's street, they are the leafy lumps they were, toned to match. |
| Lawn | The lawn is not one green from fence to fence. A map of it, a texel a town tile (`render/shared/lawn.ts`), which the blades and the ground under them both read (`render/pc/shaders/grass.ts`), makes it paler and drier in patches and deeper and lusher in others (by its colour more than by how light it is: a patch only lighter or darker read as a shadow on the lawn), 4 to 10 m across, mostly a tenth either way and 45 % at their strongest (most of a play picture was within one step of one tone before: 74 % of the home's at midday, 63 % with the patches). The same town always has the same lawn. At a pixel it is calm: the ground between the blades has nearly the blades' own tone, and 60 % of its texture's dark speckle is taken out under the lawn. Measured from pictures a quarter of a pixel apart, the light blades against the darker ground between them and that speckle were what flickered as the camera moved, not a blade's own shading. A plot is mown, short and even; the land between and beyond the plots is rough: longer grass in clumps, darker and more olive, and a little of it along the foot of a fence, where no mower reaches. Most plots show the mower's stripes, a metre wide, 6 % lighter and darker, along the lot or across it (a plot in six has none). The grass is darker and lusher at the foot of walls, fences and hedges, more on the side their shade lies on most of the day, and under trees, which ties the house to the ground. Where feet go the lawn is worn to bare earth with a ragged edge: outside a door, at a gate, past a path's ends, under and round a swing, a slide or a seesaw, before a bench. Raised beds, flower beds, shrubs and trunks stand in soil. Its flowers grow in drifts a few metres across, with a single one here and there between them, about as many in all as when they stood evenly over it. |
| Ground shade | What stands on the lawn sits on it: the ground is shaded round a house's walls, fences and hedges, the things in a garden and a tree's trunk, most where they touch it (an outside wall takes 65 % of the light at its foot and reaches 1.8 m, a fence half over 0.9 m, a thing 65 % over 0.8 m round its footprint), as less of the sky reaches the ground there (`render/shared/groundShade.ts`). A byte a texel in the lawn's mask (four a metre), which the blades and the ground already read, so it costs no draw, no pass and no read, and is the same on every preset. Before, a chair or a water butt on the lawn had nothing under it by day (its contact shadow lies on the ground, under the blades), and a fence stood on the grass like a line drawn on it. Paths, patios and floors don't take it: things there have their contact shadows. |
| Street | The asphalt is worn and patched, not one grey: pale where wheels run (0.8 m either side of a lane's middle; a road under 5.2 m between its kerbs is one lane), about a third darker between them, a fifth on the crown and nearly half along the kerbs, 10 % lighter and darker in patches a few metres across, with a rectangle of newer, darker asphalt in one stretch of 4 m in seven (`render/shared/paving.ts`, laid out by `roads.ts`). The pavement's slabs are a warm off-white, a little lighter and darker along it, between a kerb and a course at its back of grey stone (`#A9A7A2`), and the gutter's shade is half the light, so the kerb is a line from the play camera and the pavement has two edges. By vertex, the same on every preset. The roads out of town are worn the same way from edge to edge, without repairs, and the town from afar has the same roads (a worn street met a plain grey country road at the town's edge when only the streets were: 2026-11-12). |
| Paths and drives | Paving does not end on the lawn with a ruler's edge: along the sides the lawn is on it is darker and greener, moss and earth in its joints (38 % of its light at the edge, fading over half a metre), and 10 % lighter and darker in patches along it (`pavedRects` in `render/shared/paving.ts`). A path a metre wide is clean down its middle. A drive is a fifth darker where a car's wheels run, 0.75 m either side of its middle. A path's ends, at a door or the pavement, and a side another path goes on from stay clean. By vertex, in the town from afar too. |
| Selection marker | A small marker over the selected resident. Its colour follows their mood through a five-step palette (`marker` in `look.ts`). |
| Residents | Made in code (`cargo characters`): smooth bodies in a real adult's proportions (the head 1/7.4 of the height, a baby's a quarter; the eyes a little over life size, so they still read at the game's distance) from a cage of rounded cross-sections, subdivided once, with a low-detail copy for far residents and shadows. Women and men are separate bodies; each life stage, from a baby to an elder, has its own proportions, and a build slider makes anyone slimmer or broader. Everyone has a face of their own: sixteen sliders in the creator (the jaw's width and line, the chin, the cheekbones and cheeks, the nose's length, width and bridge, the brow, the eyes' size, spacing and tilt, the mouth's width, the lips, the face's length), a few millimetres each either way from the family's shared face, drawn at random for whoever the game makes up and blended from the parents for a child; the lids, brows, lips and beard follow the skin, and the skull under the hair stays, so every hairstyle fits every face. An expression never deforms the head: eyelids, brows, lips, teeth, tongue and the beard are small shapes moved by morphs (ARKit face units for moods, one whole-face recipe per emotion adapted from TalkingHead's expressions; idle micro-expressions; blinks and lids following the gaze; visemes for lip sync). Eyes are drawn by the shader (iris in the resident's eye colour, pupil, catchlight) and turn on bones. Faces have a nose shaped by the head's cage, full lips with a cupid's bow, a lid crease and tapered brows, and soft folds that come with age. Hands have five fingers with a knuckle and a middle joint, curling together into a fist. Eleven hairstyles, bald and a beard, built in locks (grooved caps with a whorl and a parting, thick curtains with curled, uneven hems, tufts, puffs, a plaited braid), in the hair colour with clump bands and a soft sheen that shows on dark hair; long hair and tails follow the neck and shoulders. Clothes are painted on the body by its shader (tops, bottoms and socks in the creator's colours, with soft edges, turned hems, cuffs, a waistband, a neck rib and stitch lines; a shirt of its own in an open jacket) and stand off it as cloth does: a tee worn out hangs from the chest over the waistband, a trouser leg falls straight from the knee over the shoe, a cuff stands a step off the arm. Shells only where the silhouette needs them (jackets and suits with banded edges, collars, skirts, shoes: an upper with a collar and laces on a sole that stands out round it). Skin takes the creator's tone, warmed as real skin reflects, deep tones keeping enough value for their features to read. Creases are baked into every part as the furniture's are (ambient occlusion: armpits, neck, inside a jacket, between fingers, deep in the hair), deepening with age. Clips are made in code (`characters/clips`) with arm and leg IK: residents stand relaxed with the weight drifting from leg to leg, walk with planted feet and a hip sway (more in women), sit back onto seats, lie under a blanket, and carry dishes on their palms. See `tools/characters/README.md`. |

## Birds

Garden birds live about the viewed lot (`render/shared/birds.ts`, drawn by `render/pc/birds.ts`; presentation only, nothing in the simulation or the save): blue tits, chaffinches, willow warblers, blackbirds and collared doves, Blender-built (`tools/art/blender/families/birds.py`) in five rigid parts the renderer moves (body, head, tail and the two spread wings, hidden while perched). They are drawn 1.25–1.6× life size, the small ones most: at real size a tit is about 5 px from the game's usual camera, and the game's apples and flowers are a little larger than life too, so they still differ in size as they should.

- **Homes:** each bird belongs to a plant near the lot (the lot's garden trees and shrubs, garden things outdoors, the street's bushes and hedges, the woods near the town), as many as its size and the preset allow (`birds` in [graphics.md](graphics.md)), made from where it stands, so a rebuild brings back the same birds. Species go by habitat: tits, finches and warblers in broadleaved trees and bushes, blackbirds low in bushes and on the lawn, collared doves in pairs, high up and on the roof ridge (while the roof shows).
- **Perched:** on a perch point of the plant's model (manifest `perches`, on its outer twigs), swaying as the wind bends the branch (the vertex shader's bend, worked out on the CPU). Small jerky glances of the head, a cock of it, a flick of the tail (a blackbird holds it up a moment), preening now and then, a hop to a nearby twig or a turn on the spot. Small birds fidget; doves sit still and long. A bird that sings lifts its head and holds still.
- **Flying:** hops within a plant, flights to another plant, the lawn or the roof, along a curve lifted over any house in the way (walls and roof, and never inside; a flight that would have to climb a wall is not taken). A burst to take off; tits, finches and warblers bound (beating on the rise, wings shut on the dip), blackbirds fly low and direct, doves flap steadily with glides; they bank into turns and flare to land.
- **On the lawn:** blackbirds run and stop to listen, chaffinches hop, doves walk with their heads bobbing; they peck and look up. Never on paths, patios, in rooms or under furniture.
- **Wary:** a resident within a few metres (3–4 m on the lawn, less up in a bush), or the camera within 1.8 m, sends them up into the nearest plant away from it, high; they keep off the lawn for 20–60 s.
- **The day:** busiest at dawn and early evening, quieter at midday. At dusk each flies home and roosts deep in its plant, head turned back into its feathers, puffed up; they wake at first light (blackbirds first, doves last), when the dawn chorus starts.
- **Time:** their clock runs with the game's: paused, they hold still (perched ones still sway with their branches); at speeds 3–5 they hurry only a little (1.5–1.75×). **Reduce motion:** no flights or hops, only the way home from the lawn, slowly; slow, eased head turns and no tail flicks.
- **Song:** the birds in view sing from where they sit (`audio/world.ts`): placed left to right as on screen, louder nearer; a few unseen ones further off keep a softer chorus behind them. Each species has its own song (`audio/birdsong.ts`), the blackbird's a fluting, varied phrase that trails off into a twitter.
- The menus' town has none (at that distance a bird would be under a pixel).
