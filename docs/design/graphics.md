# Graphics settings and performance

Code: `web/src/render/pc/` (the renderer, `PlayCanvasRenderer.ts`), `web/src/render/quality.ts` (the presets), `web/src/render/shared/resolution.ts` (the resolution), `web/src/render/shared/pacing.ts` (the FramePacer), `web/src/settings/settings.svelte.ts` (settings and their migration), `web/src/debug/bench.ts` and `benchFlags.ts` (the benchmark), `tools/bench/` (its runner).

## The presets

Settings › Performance › Graphics. Medium is everyone's default and the game's intended look. High is Medium with sharper sun shadows, four lamps casting shadows (Medium: two) and two mirrors drawn every frame. Low is Medium with trims for older laptops (integrated Intel graphics), including far fewer draws and triangles. A preset applies the next time a game starts or loads. `?quality=low|medium|high` overrides the setting for testing.

The same on every preset:
- **No canvas MSAA.** Only the last full-screen pass draws to the canvas, so its 4× MSAA smoothed nothing: 66 MB of memory traffic a frame at 1080p. The scene is multisampled in its own half-float target instead (`msaaSamples`, `render/pc/post.ts`).
- **The sun's shadow map is depth only** (PlayCanvas's `SHADOW_PCF3_32F`).
- **Resolution** as 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 2160p (below).
- **The FramePacer** for the frame rate cap (below).
- **The menus' town** is drawn at the player's frame rate cap, as the game is: standing still it is held (`holdStillFrames`, above), so it costs nothing then.
- **The camera's near plane** moves out with its distance, to a fiftieth of it (0.3 m up to 15 m out, 1.9 m at the game's farthest, about 6 m from the town overview's 300 m): with it at 0.3 m from that far, the depth buffer couldn't tell the roads, 1.2 cm over the ground, from the ground, and the ground showed through them in green patches.
- **Dark lamps leave the cluster:** a lamp that is off gets a range of 1 cm, so it covers next to no tiles.
- **Lamps are spot lights** (2026-10-21): a lamp lighting all round has a cone of a whole turn, which the cluster takes as a point light and the forward lights' glTF falloff (an inner cone as wide) as no cone; a shade's (`light.aim` down, up or out) half a turn, full along it, about half 30–45° off it and next to none side-on (the cluster's physical falloff is a little narrower than Low's glTF one). An uplighter's (`ceiling`) is all round from 30 cm under the ceiling. Village orbit at 21:00 against point lights, medians of 3 (M3 Pro, a loaded machine): GPU within noise on every preset and backend (Medium WebGPU 6.96 → 5.97 ms, WebGL2 6.53 → 6.57; Low 3.12 → 3.32 and 3.49 → 3.49), the same draws.
- **The landscape is never lit by the lamps**, the lawn included: lit by the cluster, the ground alone cost 1.1–1.5 ms of GPU a frame at night on Medium (WebGPU 5.97 → 7.44 ms, WebGL2 6.57 → 7.71). The garden's lamps (a lamp post, a street lamp, a fire pit, a bulkhead aimed out from its wall) cast pools of light on the lawn instead, as the street's lamps do: discs added to the ground, as bright in the middle as `intensity / height²` and as wide as twice the height, one draw at night for all of them, on Low too (`gardenPools.ts`). The viewed house's lit rooms throw theirs through their ground-floor windows the same way, in the same draw: half a pool at the foot of the wall under each window ([look.md](look.md), "Window light on the garden").
- **A glow wins only its own faces' depth** (a few depth-buffer steps, `zOffsetUnits`), not a slope-scaled offset, which pulled it through the parts in front at a glancing angle.
- **Nature is culled in groups, in the game:** the landscape's plants are grouped by where they stand (the woods in 8 sectors round the town; a part with fewer than 36,000 triangles in all, such as bark or flowers, stays whole), so the camera leaves out those out of view. Each group is a mesh of its own, culled by its box, and every mesh costs the main thread a little each frame, so the sectors are wide. The lawn's grass is chosen 2 m patch by patch against the frustum on the CPU, again only when the eye moves 0.4 m or turns a third of a degree (`grass.ts`). The menus' town, with the whole landscape in view, and the game's sun shadows (the near woods) draw the plants whole (`nature.ts`).
- **The sun's shadow map** draws only the casters that can reach its square round the lot, and is drawn again only when something in it changed: the light, a thing placed or moved, walls, roofs or storeys, or a resident's pose. Paused, in Build mode and behind menus it isn't drawn at all.
- **Only the residents in view** have their pose rows uploaded each frame.
- **Nothing is drawn behind full-screen menus:** the pause menu, Settings, Load game, Credits, the planner and the crash screen get 2 more frames, then none until they close; the frosted glass keeps blurring the last frame.
- **Mirrors' memory on demand**, the **lighting at most 10 times a second**, and a **100 ms limit on every frame step**, the build effects' particle clock included.

**Settings from 0.1** (`migrateGraphics`): a stored Low stays Low, whether it was the old quality preset or its parts as the old Low set them. Anything else becomes Medium. The old defaults were High's, with live mirrors, so players who never changed them now get Medium, whose nearest mirror reflects on demand (live mirrors on Medium came back on 2026-10-15). The old resolution slider isn't carried over: everyone starts on Auto. The old graphics keys are dropped.

## Targets

There is no hardware target (the owner, 2026-10-15): no frame-rate goal for particular machines. Low is for older laptops, best effort.

## Resolution

Settings › Performance › Resolution: 720p, 1080p (the default), 1440p or 2160p (`render/shared/resolution.ts`). Auto and the 50–100 % slider were removed after 0.7.0: Auto drew a Retina or 4K screen at about 1920×1080 pixels and, with the step-down tried on top of it, looked blurry and flickered as the canvas was resized.

- **Lines drawn:** the setting is how many lines of pixels the game draws, the width following the window's shape: the canvas's device-pixel height × `min(1, lines / height)` along each side, and the browser scales the picture up to the canvas. Never more than the canvas has: a window shorter than the setting is drawn at its own size. The interface is still drawn at the screen's own resolution, so text stays sharp.
- **Rounding:** the drawn size is CSS size × device pixel ratio × scale, cut down to whole pixels.
- **When it's applied:** on start, on a settings change, when the window resizes, and when the device pixel ratio changes. Browser zoom and moving the window to a screen with another density change the ratio, which doesn't always resize the window, so a `matchMedia` resolution query watches it. PlayCanvas's own pixel-ratio scaling is off (`maxPixelRatio` 1); it would apply the ratio a second time.
- **No step-down:** the resolution never changes on its own while the game runs.
- **Settings from before:** `auto` or a share saved by 0.7.0 starts on 1080p.

## The frame rate cap

Settings › Performance › Frame rate cap: 30, 60 (the default), 120, No cap, or a custom number from 10 to 500.

The FramePacer drives PlayCanvas: the app has no frame loop of its own, and the renderer draws a frame when the pacer says so.

- It measures the display's refresh rate from the `requestAnimationFrame` timestamps: the usual short gap between refreshes, worked out again every 32 refreshes. A drift under 0.5 % doesn't change it.
- It draws every N-th refresh, counted from the last frame drawn.
- **Rounding rule:** N is the smallest divisor whose rate isn't more than a quarter above the cap (with 2 % leeway for a measured rate a little off the nominal one): `N = max(1, ceil(Hz / (cap × 1.25 × 1.02)))`. So a cap the refresh rate doesn't divide becomes a rate it does, rounded up when that's within a quarter, and down otherwise.
- **No cap** draws every refresh.
- **The game's clocks** run on the timestamp of the refresh a frame is drawn for, not the moment its code runs. The residents' interpolation, the step counter and the director get that timestamp, and the renderer's frame time (camera glides and inertia, clouds, wind) is measured between those timestamps, so motion advances in the same even steps the frames are shown in. The frame time is clamped to 100 ms.
- **The menus' town** is paced by the same rule at the player's cap (see above).

## Troubleshooting

Settings › Performance › Troubleshooting › Graphics API: Auto uses WebGPU where there is one, WebGL2 otherwise; WebGL2 forces the fallback. `?renderer=webgl` does the same for one page. When WebGPU has an adapter but fails to start, the game draws with WebGL2 for the session and says so. The language model needs WebGPU either way; voices use it when they can and otherwise run on the processor.

**Weak-GPU policy.** Low stays on WebGPU. WebGL2 remains the automatic fallback when WebGPU cannot start, and the manual switch under Settings › Performance › Troubleshooting; the game does not switch by GPU vendor or adapter name, because integrated covers both an M3 and an HD 620. If an HD 620 still stalls at Low, the next step is to measure that adapter and consider a WebGL2 default for it, not to guess from the vendor string.

## The renderer

After 0.3.2 the game moved from Babylon.js 9.30 to PlayCanvas 2.23 (pinned exactly in `web/package.json`), on WebGPU or WebGL2.

- **Layout.** `render/pc` is the renderer: `PlayCanvasRenderer.ts` implements `Renderer` (`render/types.ts`), and the house, street, nature, models, residents, lamps, mirrors, the town overview, picking, build tools and post-processing each have their files; the game's shader features are in `render/pc/shaders/`. `render/shared` holds what needs no engine: the orbit camera's input and inertia (`orbit.ts`), matrix and quaternion helpers in the game's conventions (`math.ts`), mesh builders (`modelParts.ts`), the residents' behaviour behind a `ResidentView` (`characters/`), birds behind a `BirdView`, the build effects' particles, the image-processing uniforms, frame pacing and resolution; `web/tests` (`bun run test`) checks it. `render/preview` draws the previews (the creator's resident, portraits, the catalog's pictures) on PlayCanvas apps of their own, which leave the game's app the page's current one (`createSideApp`).
- **One import point.** Everything imports PlayCanvas through `render/pc/engine.ts`, which re-exports the release build by path (`playcanvas/build/playcanvas/src/index.js`). The bare `playcanvas` package resolves to the debug build under Vite's dev server, which wraps every WebGPU draw in validation error scopes (a GPU-process round trip each: a few frames a second on a full scene); ESLint refuses the bare import.
- **The mirrored world root.** Game space stays left-handed (x east, y up, z north, yaws clockwise from above), as the simulation, the generators, the residents' files and saved camera poses have it; PlayCanvas is right-handed. Everything in the world hangs under one entity scaled (1, 1, −1) (`createWorldRoot`), so it keeps its game-space numbers, and PlayCanvas turns the faces' winding round for the negative scale. `render/pc/space.ts` is the only place that converts: cameras, lights, picking rays, points projected on screen, mirror planes, the overview's and thumbnails' cameras. glTF files, which PlayCanvas loads right-handed, are baked into game space as they load (`models.ts`: their x turned round and their winding swapped).
- **Shader hosting.** The game's shader features (the soft look, the cutaway, digging and basements, rooms without windows, patterns, plain finishes, vertex and instance tints, wind, blankets, residents, the diag views) reach PlayCanvas's `StandardMaterial` as one set of chunk overrides: the engine's own chunks with a wrapper of ours, each feature behind its own define (`GAME_*`), GLSL and WGSL side by side (`render/pc/shaders/chunks.ts`), applied by priority, lowest first (`ShaderFeature.priority`). Where no per-material chunk reaches, a few engine-wide patches add the game's light (the sky's hemispheric fill and the ambient's split from the direct light in `litForwardBackendPS`, the sun kept out of basements, lamps kept in their rooms in `clusteredLightPS` and, for forward lights, `lightFunctionLightPS`, the fade at the shadow map's edge), acting only under the game's defines. `bun run check:chunks` (`tools/render/chunks.json`, in both CI workflows) checks every engine line they are inserted at and every private name the renderer reads (marked `SAFETY` in the code), so a PlayCanvas upgrade that moves one fails in CI rather than drawing without it.
- **The picture.** The scene is drawn in linear light into a half-float target, multisampled as the preset says, and PlayCanvas's `CameraFrame` composes it onto the canvas with the image processing (exposure, vignette, fitted ACES tone mapping, contrast, analytic colour curves) and the night grade, all driven by uniforms, so the look changes live without a shader compiling (`post.ts`, `shaders/post.ts`); Low has FXAA instead of multisampling. Colour maps load in a linear format and are decoded with `pow(c, 2.2)` in the material rather than by the sRGB curve, which left them brighter than the look up to 0.3.2 (`textureWithFallback` in `materials.ts`).
- **Light units** (`render/pc/lightUnits.ts`). The look (`render/look.ts`) keeps the units it was tuned in up to 0.3.2: a sun, moon or lamp (at a metre) of strength 1 lights a white surface facing it to 1/π, the sky's hemispheric fill of strength 1 lights it to 1. PlayCanvas lights a white surface to a light's strength itself, so the renderer converts: the sun and the moon by 1/π, the lamps likewise (`LAMP_UNITS` in `lamps.ts`), the sky's hemispheric fill as it is, the image-based light 5 % up (PlayCanvas takes the specularity off the ambient once more), and the lights' highlights by a calibrated factor (`SPECULAR_UNITS`; π by the units alone). They were calibrated against the game's pictures up to 0.3.2 at eight hours of the day, with `?diag=linear,nosims` and `?lightmix=` lighting one part at a time. What wasn't a matter of units was matched to those pictures first: the sky's ambient is the same spherical harmonics (`cargo envconv` works them out from the sky's HDRI as the game did up to 0.3.2 and bakes them into the `.pcenv`), and the haze, the lights' Fresnel and the lamps' falloff are the same formulas (`shaders/fog.ts`, `envBrdf.ts`, `roomLights.ts`).
- **Code from Babylon.js.** So that the game moves and looks as it did, parts of the renderer were ported from Babylon.js (Apache-2.0): the camera controls, the matrix maths, the mesh builders, the build effects' particles, the image processing and FXAA, and a few shader formulas. Each such file starts with a header that says what was ported and how it was changed; Babylon's licence and notice are in `crates/tools/texts/` and in every build's `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt` (`cargo licenses`, whose test keeps the list of those files and their headers in step).

## The town view

Zoomed out past `TOWN_FROM` (120 m of orbit radius, `render/shared/lens.ts`), the camera eases into the
living town's view ([living-town.md](living-town.md)), and the renderer draws the town at town scale, all of
it by `townView.blend` and none of it at 0: up to `TOWN_FROM` the play camera draws exactly as before (pixel for
pixel: `?diag=notown` leaves it out to compare).

- **Buildings** (`render/pc/townView.ts`, `render/shared/townBuildings.ts`, `shaders/townBuilding.ts`). Every
  backdrop building of the world (`WorldMeta.backdrop`) and every far lot's house (the street's far LOD, the
  lots outside the chunks streamed in; the street's own far groups are hidden meanwhile) is a copy of one of
  five unit meshes: a box of walls, and a gable, hip, flat (a parapet round a felt deck) or mansard roof. One
  instanced draw each, five for the whole world, in one material. Each copy carries its wall and roof colours,
  its plinth (the walls go 0.4 m under its lowest corner's ground) and its windows: the fragment shader draws
  them over the walls in bands a storey (3 m) apart and in bays along each face in the face's own metres (on a
  shop's long faces a shopfront: wide, paler glass under a fascia in one of four colours), fading to their average
  where a bay is under a pixel or two; at night a share of them glows with the street's window glow by the lamps'
  level. The share follows the life there (`litAt`, written again only when one changes): a place with opening hours
  is lit while it is open and all but dark when closed, a home nobody plays has its evening's lights going out one
  by one from ten till half past midnight and on again from half past five (`homeLights`), and a far lot's house is
  lit only while someone is up in it (`findTownUp`, twice a second from the snapshot's positions), as the
  neighbours' houses near the camera are. A backdrop building's colours come from
  the backdrop palette by its `look` (warm town tones: red and orange tile roofs, some brown, a few grey slate,
  over cream, sand, ochre, render and brick; a barn's boards and tin, a church's stone and slate), each a little
  different, its pitched roof at 0.72 of its palette colour (as bright as the palette they read as lit beside the
  game's own roofs); a lot's house keeps its own (`HousePlanner.farLook`). A footprint that isn't a rectangle is
  drawn as the rectangle round it. A backdrop house's garden has a hedge (`gardenHedges`): low boxes in the same
  draws, along each side but the one its path leaves by and those that would stand on a lot, a road or another
  building's ground. They fade in by a dither over the first 3 % of the blend (to about 148 m), so nothing pops.
- **Far woods** (`render/pc/nature.ts` `FarCrowns`). Every woods tree is gathered into crown clusters, one per
  9 m square (as wide as its trees spread, as tall as they are, a dome or, where conifers are most, a cone), two
  instanced draws. In the town view the woods draw their own trees only in the squares within
  `560 → 260 m` of the target (by the blend), crowns in the rest.
- **Ground** (`render/pc/groundTiles.ts` `TownGroundMap`, `shaders/townGround.ts`). A colour map over the whole
  world, baked once per town on a 2D canvas (2048², half a metre a texel): the streets and their pavements, the
  junctions' pads, the trails and paths, the centre's paving (round its lots, laid in courses 5 m apart), water and
  farm fields; a big world's
  farmland as its ground colours it (`LandscapeLayout.farmland`: each field of the patchwork a rectangle in its
  crop's colour, shown as strongly as the terrain shows it; the ground's own colours are a vertex apart, and from
  above a field's edge stepped along the grid); and what the backdrop's buildings
  stand on (`backdropGround`: a paved forecourt under flats, shops and halls, a bare yard under a workshop or a
  barn, and each one's way to the nearest road within 45 m, a garden path for a house). It is laid over the ground
  tiles' colour over the first 3 % of the blend, with the buildings. Past the chunks streamed in the street draws no
  road: this is the road there. The ground tiles' material gets the define only while the blend is over 0.
  (The farmland's zone itself is evened out where the landscape reads it, `shared/zones.ts` `evened`: the generator
  outlines it in 10 m cells, a staircase along every slanted edge.)
- **Movers** (`townView.ts` `TownMovers`). The cars, taxis, bikes and residents the near drawing leaves out (past
  the chunks streamed in, past the residents' radius; not those indoors or in a car) as tiny instanced shapes in
  their own colours (a car's paint, the taxi's yellow, a resident's top), where the snapshot has them, up to 2.4
  times as large at the full view so they read.
- **Culling.** By altitude and the frustum, not the preset's distances: the ground tiles' levels by the eye's
  distance (the radius times the blend added to the target's), the woods as above, the far lots all drawn (no
  `farCull`), and the sky's dome widened past the land the camera sees (`LotContents.skyReach`).
- **Backdrop as built land.** The landscape counts backdrop buildings as built (`builtField`): no tree grows in one,
  and the land round them is flat as round the lots (a world without a backdrop is as before).

## The benchmark

Opening the game with `?bench=<scene>` plays a saved game state, `web/public/bench/<scene>.json.gz` (`village`: the menus' valley as a new game makes it, with a household of four at home at 17:55 on day 1; `forest`), with the camera on a path fixed in time. It warms up for 5 s, measures for 20 s, and then shows a summary with a "Copy result" button; the result is also in `window.__benchResult`, and with the dev server in `bench-results/`. It works on the deployed site too, so it can run on any laptop. Nothing changes for players without the flag: the settings it changes are for its page alone and never saved, its save slot is deleted, and the renderer's hooks are null checks.

| Flag | Values | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `bench` | `village`, `forest` | The scene to play. |
| `quality` | `low`, `medium`, `high` | The preset; without it, the player's setting. Works without `bench` too. |
| `hour` | e.g. `13`, `21` | Holds the lighting at that hour; without it, the game's clock. Works without `bench` too. |
| `cam` | `orbit` (default), `wide`, `build`, `dusk`, `mirror`, `mirror-still` | `orbit`: 24 m round the home lot, a turn in 40 s. `wide`: 80 m, 90 s. `build`: the orbit in Build mode, paused. `dusk`: the orbit at speed 5 from the scene's 18:00, the light changing every frame. `mirror`: 6 m in front of the home lot's first mirror (the village's bathroom cabinet), swinging 30° either side every 20 s; `mirror-still`: held straight in front of it. |
| `res` | `auto` (default), `1`, `0.5`–`1` | The render scale; `auto` is what the game picks. |
| `secs`, `warmup` | 20, 5 | Seconds measured, after the warm-up. |
| `cap` | 0 (default) | Frames per second at most; 0 draws every refresh. |
| `speed` | 1 (`dusk`: 5) | The game speed while measuring. |
| `radius`, `beta`, `tx`, `tz` | e.g. `400` | The orbit's distance (to `TOWN_MAX`, 900 m: the town view), its angle and its target moved. |
| `townCentre` | `1` | The orbit round the drawn world's main town (`bench=bigworld`) instead of the home lot. |
| `hud`, `gpu` | `0` | `hud=0` hides the interface and the summary, `hud=game` only the summary (the game as a player has it: the town set's pictures); `gpu=0` doesn't time the GPU. |
| `label` | text | Kept with the result. |
| `diag` | `noshadows`, `noclouds`, `noaa`, `nonature` (or one part of it: `nowoods`, `nofarwoods`, `noflowers`, `nograss`), `nolamps`, `nomirrors`, `noglass`, `nonight`, `freeze`, `albedo`, `normals`, `nosims`, `linear`, `notown` (the town view's drawing left out) | Leaves features out, to measure what each costs (a comma list). `freeze` stops time for screenshots that compare pixel for pixel, at `at=<s>` along the path. `albedo` and `normals` draw every lit surface as its base colour or world normal (no light, no image processing), `nosims` leaves the residents out, and `linear` draws the light without tone mapping, exposure or grading: the port compared geometry, colour and light apart with them. Works without `bench` too. |
| `renderer` | `webgl` | WebGL2 instead of WebGPU. |

The result has the frame interval (average, p50, p95, p99), the main thread's work split into the game's update and the renderer's draw (PlayCanvas's update and render), the GPU time (PlayCanvas's GPU profiler: WebGPU timestamp queries, WebGL2 timer queries where offered), draw calls and triangles (all passes, shadow map, mirrors), the render passes with their pixels and bytes, the residents' posing time, lighting updates and reflections drawn a second, long tasks, the adapter, and a fingerprint of scene counts that shows two runs drew the same scene. The render passes and target bytes count the shadow map as depth only, which it is. `diag=shaders` logs every shader and pipeline compiled, with its material, target and phase: after `warmGame` a session in play compiles only for models never seen before. Its `fps` counts its own `requestAnimationFrame` ticks, which is right without a cap and misleading with one.

Other debug flags: `?lights=clustered|forward` forces the lamps' mode, `?roomlights=0` lets lamps light other rooms, `?lampshadows=<n>` sets how many lamps cast shadows (0–4), `?lightmix=sun,sky,env` scales the look's three lights, `?shadowrate=<Hz>` sets the shadow map's rate for the residents' poses, and `?snapshot=0` turns off held frames (the full list is in the [README](../../README.md#url-options)).

**The runner.** `bun tools/bench/render.mjs` opens the benchmark in Chromium on the real GPU at 1920×1080, device pixel ratio 1, for every preset × backend × scenario (`orbit-day`, `orbit-night`, `dusk`, `build`, `wide`) × CPU slow-down × diagnostic, and writes JSON and a markdown table (medians with `--runs`). It hides the interface (`hud=0`) so a cell measures the renderer; `--hud 1` measures the interface too. That matters most in Build mode: at Medium in the village, the same orbit runs at 194 fps hidden and 79 fps with the dock and its catalog pictures (M3 Pro, headless, 1920×1080; GPU 4.2 → 6.2 ms while the main thread stays at 4.6 ms). `--preview --build` measures the production build, which is what players get (compressed textures and models, minified code); `--throughput` turns vsync and the frame-rate limit off; `--throttle 1,3` slows the CPU through CDP; `--diags none,noshadows` adds diagnostics. `--shots <dir>` takes screenshots of 9 fixed poses with time frozen (and, with `--poses`, the mirror poses `mirror-day-0`, `mirror-day-15` and `mirror-night-0`, which look at the home lot's bathroom mirror with the walls up) (`--pages` the dev pages', held still), and `--compare <dirA> <dirB>` counts the pixels that differ. `--jobs <file.json>` runs a list of command lines in one go, sharing the Vite servers and browsers; the browser is kept for the whole invocation with a profile under `bench-results/.browser/`, so the HTTP cache and compiled GPU shaders carry over (the site's storage is cleared before every page), and `--fresh` starts each page with nothing kept (see [tools/bench/README.md](../../tools/bench/README.md)). Two runs of the same build in a window differ by at most 1 of 255 in a few pixels, apart from the far road; two headless runs, in at most 0.12 % of their pixels. `bun tools/bench/scene.mjs --name village` makes a scene. Both need `bun run wasm` first, and Playwright (`PLAYWRIGHT=<path to playwright/index.mjs>` if it isn't installed). Measurements run in a window, for the real display's timing; shots and scenes run headless on the real GPU (Chromium's full build; `chrome-headless-shell` draws in software). Compare shots taken the same way: a window's come out about 2 of 255 brighter (the screen's colour profile).
