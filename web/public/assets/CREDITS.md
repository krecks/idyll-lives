# Third-party assets

All of the third-party *art* below is **CC0 1.0 (public domain)**. You don't have to credit it, but we list it so it's easy to trace and replace. The voice model, the dictionary, the language model, the typeface and the decoders further down have their own licences, and so do the libraries the game is built with (`../THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt`, written by every build: each one with its licence text).
The textures under `textures/` and the sidewalk's are generated in code by `cargo tex synth`
(`crates/tools/src/tex/synth.rs`): every albedo, normal and ARM map is original, tileable, and CC0. They are
reproducible from the source tree; no downloaded texture maps are used.

## Models

Every model of a thing in the world (furniture, appliances, decor, the town's street furniture and
shops, trees, bushes, flowers and garden things, food and kitchen tools) is generated by a family's module
under `crates/art/` on the `artkit` kernel and written as `.glb` (glTF, original, CC0); they were built in Blender
by Python scripts until November 2026, and the Rust generator was proved against those builds. They take their surfaces from the finishes and textures above. No downloaded
models are used.

The lumps, boulders and bark of those models are pushed about by a smooth noise: Ken Perlin's original gradient
noise as Blender computes it (`mathutils.noise.noise`). The models' generator in Rust (`crates/artkit`) computes the
same noise, and takes the noise's two tables (a permutation of 256 and 256 unit gradients,
`crates/artkit/src/noise_tables.rs`) from Blender as data: Blender Foundation and contributors, GPL-2.0-or-later
(this project is GPL-3.0-or-later). The moving parts' curves (`crates/artkit/src/motion.rs`) follow Blender's
F-curves to the bit, ported from its source (the auto-clamped handles and their smoothing pass, and the conversions
between matrices, quaternions and Euler angles): Blender Foundation and contributors, GPL-2.0-or-later; the easings
are Robert Penner's equations (BSD-3-Clause), as Blender has them. The bevelled edges (`crates/artkit/src/bevel.rs`)
follow Blender's bevel (`bmesh_bevel.cc`: its offsets and their limit, the points that slide and the evening of their
widths, the corner patches, the hardened normals), and the triangles a slab's faces are cut into
(`crates/artkit/src/tri.rs`, `fill`) follow its fill (the `BLI_scanfill` sweep and the beauty pass of
`BLI_polyfill_beautify`), worked through again in Rust so that a bevel comes out point for point as its modifier's:
Blender Foundation and contributors, GPL-2.0-or-later. None of this ships with the game: it only decides the shapes
and the clips of the models built with it.

## Characters (`characters/`)

The residents are generated in code (original, CC0): their bodies at every life stage, faces and
their expressions and visemes, eyes, hair, beards and clothes by `cargo characters`
(`crates/tools/src/characters`; `cargo characters build` writes `rig.json` and the `.bin` files),
and their animation clips by `render/shared/characters/clips` (baked when the game loads). Bed
blankets are generated at runtime in `render/shared/characters/blanket.ts`. No downloaded models,
textures or animations are used. The facial expressions (`MOODS` in
`render/shared/characters/face.ts`) and the idle micro-expressions (`MICRO`) are adapted from the
expressions of TalkingHead by Mika Suominen (MIT, https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead).

## Street and gardens (`street/`)

The neighbourhood dressing (`render/shared/street.ts`, drawn by `render/pc/street.ts`: sidewalks, kerbs, streetlights, mailboxes, hydrants, bins, cars, picket fences, flower beds; neighbour porches, chimneys and garages, and the porch pieces, in `house.ts`) is built from primitives in code (original, CC0). The flower-clump models are generated (`packs/street`, above); the hedges are the plants pack's (`models/plants/hedge.glb`). The sidewalk surface is `cargo tex synth sidewalk`, generated like the other textures (original, CC0).
