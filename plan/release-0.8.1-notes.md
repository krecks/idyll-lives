**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

A living town to zoom out to: the play camera rises from the home to a bird's-eye view of the whole town, now denser, with terraces, semi-detached pairs, a centre with a high street and a square, and tags that say where your people are and what is open. A porch you build, traffic lights only where they are needed, a game that reaches its menu in about 6 seconds, lamplit rooms and lamps that cast shadows, sounds with a place, footsteps, cloth and hair that move. And a lighter game: about half the memory in play, no hitch when a car sets off, a paused game held still, and from now on a new release downloads only what changed. Save format 39: 0.8.0's saves open, and 0.8.0 can't open 0.8.1's.

- **A game holds less memory:** the town behind the menus (the whole neighbourhood, its streets, gardens and land) stayed built through every game, for the next visit to the menu, and took about 800 MB of the page's memory, 230 MB of buffers and 130 MB of graphics memory, more than the game's own world. It is let go a second after the game comes on screen now: a game of the village holds 189 MB instead of 999, a Big world's 882 instead of 1,738, and a game continued from the menu 1.2 GB instead of 2.0 (dev server). Leaving a game you continued or loaded now shows the menu's painted backdrop for about three seconds while its town is built again, as leaving a new game always has; starting, continuing and moving in are as fast as before.
- **Buying and building in a big world without the stall:** a thing bought, moved or sold, or a fence or a wall put up, held a Big world's screen for a long frame: on an M3 Pro, its longest task went from 264 to 90 ms for a chair bought, from 280 to 81 ms for one sold and from 292 to 114 ms for a fence (medians of 8, before and after taken in turn). The lawn round an edit is made again only where it changed: the ground's shade round what stands on it, the lawn's mask and which patches grow grass were made again over the whole town, millions of texels, after every edit (the lawn's refresh 187 → 81 ms, the shade 64 → 7 ms), and the mask the flowers are hidden by is no longer made twice. The simulation sends only what changed: the thing bought or sold instead of all 19,000 things again (2.2 MB, 8–10 ms to take in), only the parts of the lot that changed (a fence: the fences, not 1.6 MB of walls, doors, windows and floors) and the rooms only when they change (7.4 MB); and a game no longer starts by making the whole town's walls and floors (34 MB, which the page never used: it asks for its home's at once). What is drawn is the same, pixel for pixel.
- **A paused game that stands still is held:** with the game paused and nothing on screen changing, in Live or in Build mode, the picture is drawn once and held, as the menus' town already was, instead of drawn anew every frame (545 draw calls and about 3.3 ms of GPU a frame on WebGPU, 5.4 on WebGL2, at Medium): held, it draws nothing and the GPU does no work, and the main thread's work a frame falls from 3.4–4.7 ms to 0.4–0.5 (Medium, the village paused by day and by night and in Build mode, both backends, M3 Pro). It is drawn again at once for whatever changes it: the camera, the tile under the pointer, a resident selected, Build mode's previews and the thing in hand (drawn all the while it is held), the walls' mode and the storey, a purchase or an edit, the lamps, the light, files streaming in, the window's size or the settings, and the game going on. While the picture is held, the wind in the trees and the grass, the clouds' drift and the selection marker's bob stand still; they go on from there when it is drawn again.
- **A living town to zoom out to:** the play camera zooms out past the home, in one movement with no switch, to a bird's-eye view of the whole town from up to 900 m (it stopped at 95 m). On the way out the tilt eases into a view from above, the haze thins and the house in view stands whole under its roof; wheel back in and the camera is where it was. The town is drawn for that distance: every building far off as one of five simple shapes, the far woods as crowns, the roads, the centre's paving, water and fields as one colour map over the land, and cars, taxis, bikes and residents as small shapes where they really are. Its lights follow its life: a shop's front is lit while it is open, the homes' windows go out one by one towards midnight, and a household's house glows only while someone is up in it. The buildings nobody lives in stand on something (a paved forecourt, a yard, a garden with a hedge) with a way to the road, a shop has a shopfront under a fascia, and farmland's fields end along straight lines (their edges stepped along a 10 m grid).
- **Terraces whose roofs don't run into each other:** a terraced house's outline, as its neighbours and the town view draw it, took in next door's wall on the shared line and was a metre too wide, so the roofs of a row cut into each other. Each ends at its own wall now.
- **A denser town:** a main town has rows of terraced houses and semi-detached pairs on narrow lots (real homes with a household and a price: a world has about 1.35 to 1.55 times the homes it had), a centre with a high street and a square, and a backdrop of 60 to 125 buildings nobody lives in (flats, shops, offices, a town hall, a church, a school, workshops; a hamlet has 8 or more, a farm its barns). The simulation never sees the backdrop. Every town has named places with opening hours, and every career a workplace among them.
- **Town tags:** from the town view, tags name the household's residents and where they are ("Theo · at work · Brightfield Fire Station · home 20:00"; a click glides down to them and follows them), family, partners and friends elsewhere in town (a click glides there and offers a visit or a call), the places (open or closed and till when, who of yours is there, how many are inside), the homes for sale nearest the middle of the view (price and size; a click opens the town map on that lot) and the streets and areas. The household's own tags are there from the moment the town view comes in (the rest from about 200 m out), and its home has a tag of its own while nobody is in. They are laid out as the menus' tags are: the household's own always, on top and clear of each other, then your people, the places, the homes for sale and the names, each tag near what it names or not shown, never cut off at the screen's edge or under the interface. Farther out there are fewer: a street's worth of places close in, 14 over the whole town from 400 m, 6 from 900 m, and twelve homes for sale close in, six from 400 m; a hamlet's places show while the camera looks into the hamlet, a hamlet's name tells two places of one name apart, and the pointer on a place brings its tag up. A small menu turns each kind on or off.
- **The day at a glance:** a bar under the clock shows the day from sunrise to sunset, and from the town view a card lists the household's next shifts, school days and planned visits.
- **Bigger towns:** a town holds up to 240 residents (160 before); a new world starts with about 120, as before, and the newcomers who move in fill the rest.
- **Faster with a full town:** the hitch at 09:30, when the town's jobs are handed out, is gone (the new hires' commutes are worked out one a tick: the worst tick went from 70–88 ms to 22 ms in a town of 222), who is within earshot is found far more cheaply with the same answers, and the work taxis' kerbs at the start of a game take 4 ms instead of 82.
- **Less memory for the town:** the game no longer keeps a second copy of the town's shapes in the browser's memory once they are on the graphics card: the streets, houses, gardens and ground, the menus' town and the residents. A Big world with the menus' town held 369 MB of such copies and holds 82 MB (the browser's memory in all 2.74 → 2.48 GB on the dev server), a village 246 MB and 43 MB (1.54 → 1.35 GB). What is drawn and what a click picks are the same. The copies left are those something reads again: placed objects and the things residents hold (their shapes are read for shadows, a table's tucked-in chairs and the selection star), the viewed house's roof (where the birds land) and what moves (fences with gates, the sky, instanced copies).
- **A smoother morning in a Big world:** at 08:00 a new game works out each resident's walk out of town to work or school, and for those who leave within minutes it worked the same walk out again straight away: two searches across the town, from the same place in the same tick (in Snowdale a third of the walks, up to 49,000 tiles each). The walk is worked out once now, with the same times. And a search tells a tile is nowhere near a stair without looking it up among every step in town, which was the largest single cost of a long search. In Snowdale the tiles searched in six hours from 08:00 fell by a fifth, the first hour's ticks take about 30 % less time in all and the six hours about 12 % less, with every result the same.
- **No more hitches when a car or a taxi sets off:** working out a drive's way along the roads looked through every point of the path (some 1,200 in a Big world) for each of its lane's 900 to 1,000 points, twice, and took 10 to 35 ms each time: on Rowan Hollow it was every tick over 8 ms. It now looks only at the points near each lane point and finds exactly the same ones, in a few milliseconds; and a taxi coming into town takes the way it was timed by when nothing on it has changed, rather than working out the same way again, often on the next tick. Across seed base 7's Big worlds a third to two thirds fewer ticks take over 8 ms (Rowan Hollow 52 to 5 in six game hours, its worst 1 % of steps 76 to 29 ms of processor time).
- **A porch you build:** the roofed entrance over the front door (a low landing with a step, two white posts, a little gabled roof in the house's roof colour with a gable in its wall colour, and a lamp) is the simulation's now, not drawn where the garden path met the house. Build › Construct has a Porch tool: click any outside door on the ground floor for a porch over it ($600; free in Creative), or click a porch to take it down for $360, like furniture sold. Its two posts stand in the way as a post on its own does: nobody walks through them and nothing can be put where they stand, while the way up to the door, through the middle of the porch, stays open. It needs open ground in front of the door, no wall or fence across it and nothing and nobody where its posts go, and it isn't built where its posts would shut anyone or anything off or cut off the way to the door; a wall, fence or diagonal can't be built through one. Walled up, turned into a window or taken out, the door takes its porch with it (sold back); a room moved with Move takes its porches along. The tool also builds a porch of your own from pieces, in your house's colours: a post ($60; nobody walks through it), a gabled canopy 1 to 3 tiles across and up to 6 deep ($90 a tile; its ridge runs the way it faces, over posts or against a wall, and over the driveway as a car port; the porch's lamp hangs under its ridge near the front, lit at night with a pool of light under it, and a canopy against the house goes with that wall in the cutaway, lamp and all, as the porch's canopy does) and a step platform up to 4 × 4 ($30 a tile, a low step along its front, walked across); R turns them and the panel sets their size. Porches and pieces can be undone, are kept in blueprints, and stand on the lot from the street and in the menus' town as they do in the game. A new town puts a porch where the street drew one before, so towns look as they did (residents now walk round its posts); saves from before keep theirs too, their posts standing as they load.
- **Traffic lights only where they're needed:** only a big junction is lit now, where four roads or more meet or two main roads do (a street meeting a street or a country road): about 11 in a town, where every junction of three roads or more was (77). Every other one, a lane meeting a street or another lane, or country roads meeting out in the open, gives way: cars take turns through it as they do at any junction, with no stop for nobody, and a street through it has a zebra on one side of the lane's mouth (it had one on each arm and a pole on each corner). Two such junctions close together along a street (within 40 m) have one zebra between them, not one either side, and none by a lit junction's crosswalk. At a lit junction, a side road's crosswalk is walked while the main road has green, its head showing walk, and cars turning into the side road stop for those on it; someone walking along the main road's pavement past the side road no longer turns every light red. A car whose way round a taxi standing at the kerb is laid while it's in the middle of a junction keeps its way through it, where another coming the other way was let in and the two stood nose to nose for good. At a zebra a car stops at its line only for someone on it or about to step onto it, and waits for them to cross; then it drives on. The lights left are smarter: with nobody waiting they rest on the main road's green rather than wherever they stopped, a car coming up to a green keeps it on for it, and the walk is over as soon as those crossing are across, not after its whole 10 seconds of every arm on red; someone who stopped waiting at the kerb no longer brings the walk round for nobody.
- **Shorter loading:** the game reaches its menu in about 6 seconds instead of 16 (it drew portraits of 118 residents nobody saw there, and made the menu's town one step after another on the page: its town is laid out in the background now while the rest gets ready, and its land and streets are built side by side), "Choose a household" shows the faces in view in about a second and all of them in under 3 (8 before), the ones on screen first, and a household's game opens about 8 seconds sooner (its world is built in longer steps behind the loading screen, its town's houses are ready from the menu's, and the town's other faces wait until it is up). Portraits are kept between visits.
- **Lamplit rooms at night:** a room with its lamps on is warm all through. The night sky and the moon lit a room as they light the garden, so whatever no lamp faced (the side of a fridge, a bed's far edge, the wall between two lamps) was blue, and a lit room half warm, half lavender. A lamplit room now keeps a little of the night's light, and its lamps' light comes off the walls and the floor to what they don't face. A room with its lights out is moonlit as before, and nothing changes by day.
- **Under the hood:** the production build's compressed copies of the assets (KTX2 textures, quantised and meshopt-compressed models, gzipped residents) are made by a tool of the repo's own in Rust, `cargo assets`, instead of a JavaScript one with four packages behind it; `cargo assets check` reads every copy back and compares it with its source (all 2.19 million triangles within 0.29 mm, their shading and the animations exact). The game's pictures are the same but for the fine noise of the roughness and occlusion maps, whose smaller levels are averaged as the data they are now. The pins, the check of PlayCanvas's internals, the world maps and what the bench does with its pictures (statistics, comparisons, side-by-side sheets) moved to Rust as well (`cargo pins`, `check-chunks`, `world-map`, `shots`), and sharp is no longer needed.
- **A voice over the room:** while a resident speaks, the sounds of the world drop to half (about 6 dB) and come back when the line is over, so a line is heard over a running tap or the television. Everything now passes a limiter before the master volume: a shower, a blender and two voices at once are held under full scale, not clipped; on its own no sound reaches it.
- **Hair and hems that move:** long hair, a ponytail and a braid trail a little behind a resident who walks, swing on when they stop or turn their head and come to rest; the hem of a skirt, a jacket or an apron swings with the hips. They never pass into the neck, the back or the legs, are still when the resident is, and move the same at any frame rate, on every graphics preset.
- **Feet that stay put:** a resident who sets off lifts one foot and steps off the other, which stays where it stood, and one who stops brings their feet together in a step each. Both feet used to slide: 30 to 50 cm along the floor on the first step and 20 to 40 cm on stopping; it is now 3 to 5 cm and 1 to 4 cm. A woman's standing foot no longer drifts 6 cm sideways and back with the sway of her hips at every step.
- **Clothes that are cloth:** near the camera, trousers, suit trousers, shorts and capri, and tees, polos, shirts, long tops, tank tops and blouses, are cloth of their own over the body instead of paint on the skin: a hem or a cuff stands off the leg or the arm with its own dark inside, a neckline stands off the neck, and a tee worn out hangs over the waistband as a second layer. Far off, and on the household creator's stage for tops, they are drawn as before.
- **Sounds with a place:** a sound made indoors has its room around it, a short reverb by the room's size (a bathroom, a bedroom, a living room), and so has a voice; out in the garden they stay dry. Zoom in on a room and the sounds of the other rooms come through the walls, duller and quieter. Zoom out and the house's sounds fall away and lose their highs, half as loud at twice the distance, down to a murmur from the town view. At the distance a game opens at, the whole house is heard as before.
- **Footsteps:** the residents in view are heard walking, each step as the foot lands: shoes on a wooden floor, on tiles, on carpet, on grass and on paving each sound their own, bare feet (out of bed, off to the bath) softer, a jog and a run harder. They are quiet, the more walkers the quieter each, placed and in their room like the other sounds of the world, and off with "Sounds of the world".
- **The street and the wind:** a car or the work taxi driving past is heard, its engine and its tyres rising as it nears the middle of the picture and falling away behind it, higher the faster it goes. The wind in the leaves and the grass follows the gusts you see roll over the lawn. Zoomed out into the town view, the town's own hum comes in under the birds.
- **Voices as loud as one another:** every spoken line is brought to the same loudness as it is made. One resident's voice was up to 2.4 dB over another's on average, a short "Hi!" or "Ouch!" up to 4.7 dB over the same voice's sentences, the loudest line 7.7 dB over the quietest. Nothing about a line's timing or its mouth shapes changes.
- **Music that goes somewhere:** the stereo, the radio and an instrument being played no longer repeat one four-second loop: there are three pieces of half a minute and more, made in code, each sixteen bars with a tune that comes back. A plucked folk tune by day, a slow waltz in the evening and at night, and a dance with a beat while someone dances; the gramophone always plays its waltz.
- **Lamps cast shadows:** at night the lamps nearest what you are looking at cast soft shadows: a bench's across the rug under a pendant, a table's on the kitchen tiles, a resident's up the wall as they walk past a floor lamp. Two lamps at a time on Medium, four on High, fading in and out as the view moves through the home; Low has none.
- **Window light on the garden:** at night a lit room's windows throw a soft warm glow on the lawn under them. It follows the room: when its lamps are switched off or everyone in it falls asleep, the glow goes out with the windows.
- **Windows of a lit room at night:** the inner sides of a window's opening are no longer vivid blue beside a warm room, nor is the back of a shower or a wardrobe standing against an outside wall, and the lawn seen through a lit room's window is the night's dark blue, not olive green.
- **Softer sofas and beds:** cushions, pillows and bedding look stuffed rather than cut from foam. A seat cushion's corners are pinched in where its seams pull and its top sinks a little towards the front edge; throw pillows are thin at their seams with corners that stand out; a duvet lies in soft folds, rolls over the edge of the mattress and hangs unevenly down the sides; bed pillows are plump in the middle. The designer sofa's buttoned back and the pouf's top are really dented at each button. Every sofa, armchair and bed of the living room, the bedroom and the kids' room, and the upholstered things of the personality packs; rounded edges everywhere (fridges, washers, drawer fronts) are a little rounder for the same cost.
- **Leather that looks like leather:** the leather armchair, the designer lounge chair, the leather pouf, the executive chair, the executive desk's writing pad, the high bar stool and the heavy bag were an even satin; they now have a hide's soft creases and an uneven sheen, polished where they are sat on.
- **Rounder turned legs:** the cabriole legs of the classic sofa, the armchair, the wingback, the fireside lounge's chair, the journal desk and the vanity are round (twelve sides, where six or eight showed as flats and streaked in the light).
- **A returning visit is quicker:** the main menu's town, its houses and its preview are kept from the last visit (in the browser's cache, under the build's id: a new build makes a new valley, and a world started from step 1 still rolls its own), so the menu comes up in about 4.3 seconds instead of 5.9. The town is not laid out a second time, and the entry is written in the town worker while the menu comes up.
- **The town's roads are made off the main thread:** the whole town's road mesh, a 0.3-second pause on the page as the town is built, is made in the render data worker while the street is planned, so building the town no longer pauses the page for it.
- **Lighter town tags:** zoomed out over a Big town, the tags cost about a quarter of a millisecond a frame instead of a whole one. Each tag that has to find room is tried only against what lies within the few pixels it may move, which places every tag exactly where trying it against all of them did; a still camera over a still town doesn't lay them out at all, and a tag that stays put isn't written to the page again. The homes for sale's tags (a Big town has hundreds, each with a price to format) are made when a home is bought or sold, not every game minute.
- **Save format 39:** the town's backdrop, places and centres (38) and the porches and porch pieces (39) are in the save. 0.8.0's saves open and play as before, their porches where they were drawn; 0.8.0 can't open the new ones.
- **A smaller deploy:** the build ships the compressed copies of the game's models, textures and character data on their own, not the sources beside them (every one has a copy, and the game only ever fetches the copy). `web/dist` is 113 MB smaller (499 to 386 MB), and a release uploads and publishes that much less. What the build doesn't compress (icons, the sky, manifests, a modder's pack) still ships as it is.
- **A Big world's street in a third of the memory:** the street kept every chunk it had planned twice over, once as the pieces it was planned from (each lot's house and garden, each chunk's fences) and once as the chunk's merged geometry, besides the vertex arrays it draws from, and in a Big world it plans all 112 chunks at load for the 28 or so it draws. A chunk now keeps only its vertex arrays and how they are drawn, and the pieces are kept only for the chunks drawn, the ones a focus moving next door or a thing moved plans again. The game's street in the benchmark's Big world went from about 755 MB of the page's memory to 113 MB (the whole page from 1.85 GB to 0.75 GB), a village's from 75 to 42 MB, and the menus' town, which builds its street the same way, from about 0.85 GB to 0.45 GB. Chunks streamed in as the camera moves are still only uploaded, never planned, and the street looks the same.
- **Shadows that cost less while residents move:** the sun's shadows follow the residents as before, but redrawing them no longer redraws everything else. The sun's map keeps a picture of all that stands still; while only residents (or a door, a lid, a seat a use moves) have moved, it copies that picture and draws just them over it: about 40 draws a redraw (the residents' bodies and parts) instead of 220, and most redraws are of that kind (the village orbit on Medium by day, 20 of 27 a second), so 25 to 50 fewer draws a frame in all (WebGPU 527 → 501, WebGL2 550 → 502), with the same picture pixel for pixel on both renderers. A chair pulled in to a table no longer redraws the shadows (and the lamps' shadows) every frame it slides; it follows the shadow rate like a resident. It costs a second shadow map's memory on Medium and High (16 MB, 64 MB on High).
- **A new release downloads only what changed:** the game asks for its data files (models, textures, the residents, icons, the content, the texture decoder) by a hash of their contents instead of the build's id, and the isolation service worker keeps them, and the build's own scripts, styles and wasm, from one release to the next while a release still ships them (what it no longer lists is let go). Upgrading to a build with one model changed downloaded 1.06 MB instead of 28.5 MB (a production build on a Pages-like server, the menu and the game data it streams, median of 3), and the visit after it 0.21 MB instead of 5.5 MB (the last build's worker, still in charge for the first moments of an upgrade, passed the new build's files through without keeping them). The first upgrade from 0.8.0 still downloads everything once, as 0.8.0's worker doesn't know the new URLs. The page also reaches the menu without the session's and Build mode's code (fetched once the menu is up, in 30–50 ms, long before Play needs it) and the living-town debug fixture: the scripts it waits for are 1.64 MB (579 KB gzipped) instead of 1.80 MB (631 KB). `bundle.json` grows by 18 KB gzipped, the list of hashes.
- **Less work each tick in a big town:** where a point meets the roads is found by looking at the nearest stretches of road first, and stops as soon as nothing further off could be nearer; each house's door has where it meets the roads noted once, as the public places' doors had, so a resident out on an outing working out when to head home no longer looks it up every minute; the public parking spots' bays are found once, not for every spot whenever a car looks for parking; the residents' state at the start of each tick is gathered with less asking twice; and the hourly look at what residents think of their homes goes through each home's rooms and things once, not once per resident. Everything happens exactly as before (the same save after six game hours on every world measured). On Rowan Hollow a tick takes about a sixth to a quarter less time; the road lookups went from 8 % of it to under 1 %, and the hourly look at the homes from a few milliseconds to almost nothing.
