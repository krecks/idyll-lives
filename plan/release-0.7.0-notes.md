Getting about town: bikes and e-scooters and lifts from the neighbours. And a host who offers a bite to eat, a thing asked for carried in and set up where you can see it, promotions after a working week, visitors let in, and only your household's news in the feed and the Journal.

- **Bikes and e-scooters:** most households in a new town have something to ride, standing in the garden by the house or the garage, clear of the driveway, the paths and the doorsteps: about a city bike for each teen or grown-up (up to three, more often in a home with no car), a kids' bike with stabilisers for each child of four or more, and an e-scooter in about one home in six. Off to work or school with no seat in the household's car, a resident takes the fastest free one at home that fits them when the ride out of town is longer than 60 m: they walk over to it, get on and ride out of town (the city bike three times as fast as walking, the kids' bike a little over twice, the e-scooter four times), pedalling as its wheels and cranks turn, or stood on the deck, and its spot stands empty while it's out. Home again, they ride back to its spot and get off. Their way to work is timed by the ride, so they leave later and still arrive on time. The household strip and the town map say who is "Cycling to work" or "Riding the e-scooter home". Furnish sells all three under Outdoor › Bikes & scooters ($260, $120 and $420), and the bikes can be tuned up. Towns saved before keep what they had: no bikes.
- **Lifts from the neighbours:** someone off to work or school with no seat in their own household's car gets a lift from a friend next door (friendship 30 or more) whose car stands on its driveway within 25 m of their front door and who sets off at about the same time, when there's a seat free: they walk over, get in by a door of their own and ride along, and come home with the driver, or make their own way if the driver works late.
- **A host who offers a bite to eat:** a quarter of an hour after a guest comes in, a free host asks if they'd like something (*Offer a bite to eat*), and again every two hours or so, only with something at home to bring; a hungry guest says yes more readily. The host takes the snack (or whatever home has that a guest could have, toast say) out, carries it over and hands it to them; the guest sits down with it, the plate is the host's to wash up, and the story tells it ("Ada brought Bo a snack"). Whoever eats with company, at home with a guest or at a host's, sits down beside them, within talking range, so the two talk over the meal.
- **Things asked for, set up where you can see them:** a yes to "Mika would like to buy a cookbook lectern" no longer makes it appear somewhere in the house unnoticed. It's paid for and comes as a parcel to the front door: Mika picks it up, carries it in both hands to where it goes, puts it together there (15 minutes), and it springs into place as things bought in Build mode do, the camera looking on; the story says who set it up. Whoever asked does it as soon as they're home and free (a grown-up does it for a child); after two hours any grown-up at home may, and after a day it's set up for them anyway. With no room for it, it goes back to the shop and the money comes back. Neighbours nobody plays still buy for themselves at once, now only with the next week's bills left over. Build mode's catalog still places things at once.
- **Promotions after a working week:** a shift filled 2.5 % of the performance bar at every grade, so a promotion took 19 to 31 workdays. Now a worker in a fine mood is promoted after about five workdays at first (four when happy) and seven to ten near the top; an unhappy one clearly later, though a shift worked in the worst mood no longer takes any of the bar away (only a missed one does). A shift started more than half an hour late counts half. The next grade's skills still hold a full bar back. The Career tab says when the next promotion comes at this pace ("about 3 more workdays"), or that it waits on the skills shown.
- **Visitors let in:** a visitor knocking while someone awake is at home is let in: answering the door comes before the next step of a routine, and a busy host is waited for. Residents no longer set off on a visit hours away or to a house where everyone is asleep: a visit they choose, or are invited to on the phone, is one they can reach within 45 minutes, in visiting hours, with time to stay; it lasts from when they're let in.
- **Your household's news:** the story feed and the Journal keep to your household's own news: no more neighbours' jobs and goals in the corner, and the Journal's *Everyone* view is gone (*Our home* and *Milestones* stay).
- **Food and cooking as it looks in life:** a dish is carried to the table and set down bent over it; clearing away picks an armful up bent over the table, one kind at a time, dirty plates as a stack, and a half-eaten plate keeps its food until it's scraped off, stepped up to the pedal bin; a diner takes the chair whose place is clear, and eating standing steps off the spot something is used from. The cook holds a knife, a wooden spoon, a turner or a whisk and stirs the pot that is then carried away, what they carried in goes down on a cool free spot, and food eaten by hand is bitten from the hand. The cookers leave their hobs and grates free for the cook's food, their knobs turn by the burner that lights, the toaster, blender, stand mixer and espresso machine move while used, and the held food looks as it's served (a salad, a barbecue and a tasting plate, a stack of dirty plates). A step a recipe can't do without is waited for longer before dinner is given up, and the pizza oven is an oven only.
- **The Console TV, a set of today:** a thin-bezel flat screen on a fluted oak media console, where it was a CRT with rabbit ears.
- **Fixes:**
  - a picked door's resize arrows go with the door (undone or replaced), instead of staying up into Live mode;
  - no fence posts left standing round the basement when it's in view;
  - with an upper floor in view, the windows, door leaves and the wall over and beside them that go with its walls cut away no longer come down into the rooms below as flat white frames about knee high, along the walls of the storey below (as it was since storeys came in);
  - models whose file the browser decoded in parts (seen in Firefox: swing sets, sandboxes and sectionals) are no longer drawn as boxes;
  - riders in a car backing out no longer sit facing past a full turn; residents meeting halfway over the stairs meet by the stairs, not across town; an adopted child stands beside a parent on the bed or sofa, not shut in on it;
  - walkers and riders keep their whole body (a bike's wheels and handlebars too) clear of cars, beside a country road out on the verge, and wait clear of the ground in front of one; a car never drives through someone in its way, and one parked out on a trip takes up its bay; a car waits for those beside it only when its next move would bring it nearer to them (a car turning tight into a lane among riders beside it waited for them, they for it, for hours);
  - cars coming home at the same time no longer drive into town on top of each other: each waits out of sight at the way in till the one before it is a car's length and a gap on; two cars meeting on a 7 m street pass each other in their lanes, clear of each other (they drove 0.8 m either side of the middle, too close for cars 1.74 to 1.88 m wide); and no two cars on trips park in the same spot;
  - cars no longer drive into each other: they take turns where their ways cross or meet, in the order they came, each going on only once its way through is clear (out the far side too, so nobody stops in a junction): into a junction (where two roads cross or touch between their ends too), out of a driveway or a parking bay across the lane, and round a 6 m lane's tight bend. Two cars meeting on a 6 m lane pull over onto the pavement to pass (the lamps, hydrants and bins there stand back from the kerb for it), a car keeps a little short of where another is about to drive, and one held nose to nose backs up a little out of the way. A new Big world's roads are 7 m wide, wide enough for two cars to pass in their lanes;
  - whether to cycle or walk to work is decided once, when the way is timed (a child timed at bike pace walked, and was late); a walk to work from a neighbour's is timed to the way out taken from there; late for work is judged by when the way gets them there;
  - a guest promised a bite stops waiting for it once nothing is coming;
  - the armoire, the party booth and the shower stall no longer have faces turned inside out, and the potting bench's seedling and the rolled yoga mat no longer show flat before they grow; the monitor takes the room on a top its screen's corners need.
