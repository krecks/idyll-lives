**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

Getting about town: bikes and e-scooters, lifts from the neighbours and a shared taxi to work and out. And a host who offers a bite to eat, a thing asked for carried in and set up where you can see it, promotions after a working week, visitors let in, and only your household's news in the feed and the Journal.

- **Bikes and e-scooters:** most households in a new town have something to ride, standing in the garden by the house or the garage, clear of the driveway, the paths and the doorsteps: about a city bike for each teen or grown-up (up to three, more often in a home with no car), a kids' bike with stabilisers for each child of four or more, and an e-scooter in about one home in six. Off to work or school with no seat in the household's car, a resident takes the fastest free one at home that fits them when the ride out of town is longer than 60 m: they walk over to it, get on and ride out of town (the city bike three times as fast as walking, the kids' bike a little over twice, the e-scooter four times), pedalling as its wheels and cranks turn, or stood on the deck, and its spot stands empty while it's out. Home again, they ride back to its spot and get off. Their way to work is timed by the ride, so they leave later and still arrive on time. The household strip and the town map say who is "Cycling to work" or "Riding the e-scooter home". Furnish sells all three under Outdoor › Bikes & scooters ($260, $120 and $420), and the bikes can be tuned up. Towns saved before keep what they had: no bikes.
- **A shared taxi to work, and out:** off to work or school a long way from town's way out on foot (longer than 10 game minutes), with no seat in a car and no bike or e-scooter for a ride out of an hour and a half at most, a resident in an open-world town rides a taxi the work sends when it gets them out sooner than the walk. Those going out of town the same way share one, whatever their work or school, when they start within 15 minutes of each other, four to a taxi, housemates side by side. The taxi comes in by that way out and pulls in at each rider's kerb: parallel to the lane and in it, on a straight stretch clear of every driveway's mouth (theirs too, by a car's length where it can be), of junctions, public parking and the lamps, hydrants and bins along the pavement, one taxi to a stretch at a time (another comes later rather than go without); riders leave home in time to wait at their kerb ("Waiting for the taxi"), sooner from upstairs or the end of the garden, walk over to it as it stands and get in by a door of their own ("Taxi to work · there about 08:40"). It stands only a short while: one not at the kerb or at its door two minutes after it came is left and booked again. It drives out in time for the first one's start, round a junction or a street where cars are held up when that's not much longer than waiting it out, else waits at the edge of town for it to clear, a while at most, and its riders' times follow the way it comes; one held still half an hour where nothing gives turns back, those it was to pick up are booked again, and those it was taking home wait at work for another, or walk if it's a short way; it comes in by another way in where cars queue at its own, and runs into town by one way are spread a few minutes apart. Those already too late to walk (as a game begins, say) share a late run their way, and someone the taxi missed is booked again once, before they make their own way; a rider's leaving home, and their free time before it, follows the taxi as it runs late. Home again it puts them down at their kerb ("Taxi home"), and those with a long walk home from work go by it too. Visits and outings out of walking reach go by taxi as well, for those with no car or bike of their own to go by, when it beats walking there: they wait at home till it's near, it puts them down at the place's kerb, and a taxi back comes when their stay is over; those off to the same place, or home from it, about the same time share one, and the taxi's way counts in the hour and a half an outing may be away and in being home by 23:00. In place of the bus 0.7.0 was to have: a Big world's bus stops (ways out on foot until now) are gone, and riding there is by taxi; the shelters at the ends of the rows of houses are taxi ranks now (one a street at most, none within 60 m of another), the Market Square's too, and the taxi is drawn yellow with a chequered band and a TAXI sign.
- **Lifts from the neighbours:** someone off to work or school with no seat in their own household's car gets a lift from a friend next door (friendship 30 or more) whose car stands on its driveway within 25 m of their front door and who sets off at about the same time, when there's a seat free: they walk over, get in by a door of their own and ride along, and come home with the driver, or make their own way if the driver works late.
- **A host who offers a bite to eat:** a quarter of an hour after a guest comes in, a free host asks if they'd like something (*Offer a bite to eat*), and again every two hours or so, only with something at home to bring; a hungry guest says yes more readily. The host takes the snack (or whatever home has that a guest could have, toast say) out, carries it over and hands it to them; the guest sits down with it, the plate is the host's to wash up, and the story tells it ("Ada brought Bo a snack"). Whoever eats with company, at home with a guest or at a host's, sits down beside them, within talking range, so the two talk over the meal.
- **Things asked for, set up where you can see them:** a yes to "Mika would like to buy a cookbook lectern" no longer makes it appear somewhere in the house unnoticed. It's paid for and comes as a parcel to the front door: Mika picks it up, carries it in both hands to where it goes, puts it together there (15 minutes), and it springs into place as things bought in Build mode do, the camera looking on; the story says who set it up. Whoever asked does it as soon as they're home and free (a grown-up does it for a child); after two hours any grown-up at home may, and after a day it's set up for them anyway. With no room for it, it goes back to the shop and the money comes back. Neighbours nobody plays still buy for themselves at once, now only with the next week's bills left over. Build mode's catalog still places things at once.
- **Promotions after a working week:** a shift filled 2.5 % of the performance bar at every grade, so a promotion took 19 to 31 workdays. Now a worker in a fine mood is promoted after about five workdays at first (four when happy) and seven to ten near the top; an unhappy one clearly later, though a shift worked in the worst mood no longer takes any of the bar away (only a missed one does). A shift started more than half an hour late counts half. The next grade's skills still hold a full bar back. The Career tab says when the next promotion comes at this pace ("about 3 more workdays"), or that it waits on the skills shown.
- **Visitors let in:** a visitor knocking while someone awake is at home is let in: answering the door comes before the next step of a routine, and a busy host is waited for. Residents no longer set off on a visit hours away or to a house where everyone is asleep: a visit they choose, or are invited to on the phone, is one they can reach within 45 minutes, in visiting hours, with time to stay; it lasts from when they're let in.
- **Your household's news:** the story feed and the Journal keep to your household's own news: no more neighbours' jobs and goals in the corner, and the Journal's *Everyone* view is gone (*Our home* and *Milestones* stay).
- **Food and cooking as it looks in life:** a dish is carried to the table and set down bent over it; clearing away picks an armful up bent over the table, one kind at a time, dirty plates as a stack, and a half-eaten plate keeps its food until it's scraped off, stepped up to the pedal bin; a diner takes the chair whose place is clear, and eating standing steps off the spot something is used from. The cook holds a knife, a wooden spoon, a turner or a whisk and stirs the pot that is then carried away, what they carried in goes down on a cool free spot, and food eaten by hand is bitten from the hand. The cookers leave their hobs and grates free for the cook's food, their knobs turn by the burner that lights, the toaster, blender, stand mixer and espresso machine move while used, and the held food looks as it's served (a salad, a barbecue and a tasting plate, a stack of dirty plates). A step a recipe can't do without is waited for longer before dinner is given up, and the pizza oven is an oven only.
- **A smaller voice download:** the voices are 121 MB to download instead of 219: most of the voice model's weights are stored in 8 bits instead of 16, and turned back into full floats when it loads, so it speaks as fast and sounds the same (as close to before as one line is to itself said twice). With the language model too, the runtime both use is downloaded and kept once, not twice. Players who have the voices downloaded get the new files once.
- **A smaller language model download:** the model that writes residents' lines (LFM2.5 350M) is 196 MB to download instead of 255, and writes the same lines word for word: the file kept one part of it twice and tables for 128,000 positions where a line needs under a thousand. Players who have it downloaded get the new file once.
- **The Console TV is now the Portable TV, a tube set:** one dark plastic case on two pads, a big 4:3 tube under a thin rounded mask and a telescopic stub aerial that lifts while it’s watched, where it was a wooden CRT with rabbit ears on splayed legs (saves keep it).
- **Residents who read as men and women:** grown men and women differ by more than their hair and clothes. A man's jaw is wider and squarer, his nose larger, his shoulders and chest broader over a waist nearly as wide as his hips; a woman's jaw is narrower with a small chin, her eyes a little larger and her lips fuller, her waist defined over wider hips, with a gentle bust (her waist was pinched to 21 cm, and a man's was too). The brows lie over the eyes, from the inner corner to just past the outer one, where they ran on round the temples: a man's lower and straighter, a woman's higher and arched. Children look as before, and teens part way.
- **Homes that look lived in:** a cake is baked now, not bought: *Bake a cake* at an oven (a mixed batter, 40 minutes in the oven) leaves a layer cake on its stand for four to eight, eaten a slice at a time, its stand left with crumbs to wash up; the cake on a stand is no longer sold or put out in new houses (one already standing stays). There are two sinks: the kitchen sink is for washing your hands and the dishes (*Wash the dishes*), a bathroom basin for washing your hands and brushing your teeth; new houses and the ready-made ones have a basin in the bathroom. A chest of drawers or a writing desk that comes with its own things on top (a vase, a box and a picture; papers and a lamp) gets nothing more put on it in new houses. Clicking the floor or the ground with someone selected offers *Walk here* instead of sending them at once. The toilet takes its time: residents sit for the whole visit rather than getting up the moment the bladder is empty, so sitting down and getting up no longer look rushed (one with nothing to go for still gets up at once). Ceiling lights are brighter, and rooms get as many as it takes: a pendant or track light counts for the pool of light under it (7 to 9 m²) rather than a whole room, so a kitchen gets a ceiling light that lights all of it.
- **Big towns keep moving:** in a big town, traffic out of sight no longer jams. Where the camera looks (what's on screen and a little way round it) cars take turns, keep their distance and wait for each other as before; out of sight they simply carry on, so a hold-up nobody can see is gone at once instead of keeping cars stuck for hours. A car driving into view waits just out of sight till its way in is clear, and where the camera comes to two cars that were driving through each other it finds them apart: no two cars are ever drawn on top of each other, and no car you're watching vanishes. A jam right where you're looking can still happen (Big towns are a preview, see *Known issues*); when cars in view have been stuck on each other for a few minutes, one of them does what a driver would. It backs up slowly into clear room, round the corner it came by or into a driveway's mouth, and goes another way, or waits out of the way while the others go by. A work taxi stuck in view never vanishes from the road: it turns round and drives off out of town. Cosy and normal towns, drawn whole, get the same.
- **Fixes:**
  - someone out on an outing gets up from the park bench and heads home when it's time, and leaves from where they are rather than from where they came in, so they're home by 23:00;
  - faces, necks and shirts close up (the creator, a near camera) no longer show thin dark stripes: residents cast their shadow from a simpler copy of themselves that stood a little outside them, so they shaded themselves in lines; the Portable TV's aerial lifts off the case when it's watched instead of swinging sideways over it;
  - a car meeting a work taxi standing at the kerb on a narrow lane pulls over to get by it, instead of waiting behind it till it left (13 minutes once); a taxi standing for its riders no longer keeps the junction ahead of it from others;
  - a car coming up behind a work taxi standing at the kerb for its riders goes out round it into the other lane when the way is clear and back in past it, instead of waiting behind it a quarter of an hour; one coming the other way waits its turn short of the taxi meanwhile, and they take turns there in the order they came;
  - two cars meeting on a 7 m street's sharp bend take turns round it instead of meeting side by side, too near to pass; a car backing up a little out of another's way no longer backs into a junction it has left; a car waiting its turn at a junction no longer lets every car coming up go first, and a stream of cars leaves it a gap once it has waited a while;
  - a picked door's resize arrows go with the door (undone or replaced), instead of staying up into Live mode;
  - no fence posts left standing round the basement when it's in view;
  - with an upper floor in view, the windows, door leaves and the wall over and beside them that go with its walls cut away no longer come down into the rooms below as flat white frames about knee high, along the walls of the storey below (as it was since storeys came in);
  - models whose file the browser decoded in parts (seen in Firefox: swing sets, sandboxes and sectionals) are no longer drawn as boxes;
  - riders in a car backing out no longer sit facing past a full turn; residents meeting halfway over the stairs meet by the stairs, not across town; an adopted child stands beside a parent on the bed or sofa, not shut in on it;
  - walkers and riders keep their whole body (a bike's wheels and handlebars too) clear of cars, beside a country road out on the verge, and wait clear of the ground in front of one; a car never drives through someone in its way, and one parked out on a trip takes up its bay; a car waits for those beside it only when its next move would bring it nearer to them (a car turning tight into a lane among riders beside it waited for them, they for it, for hours);
  - cars coming home at the same time no longer drive into town on top of each other: each waits out of sight at the way in till the one before it is a car's length and a gap on; two cars meeting on a 7 m street pass each other in their lanes, clear of each other (they drove 0.8 m either side of the middle, too close for cars 1.74 to 1.88 m wide); and no two cars on trips park in the same spot;
  - cars no longer drive into each other: they take turns where their ways cross or meet, in the order they came, each going on only once its way through is clear (out the far side too, so nobody stops in a junction): into a junction (where two roads cross or touch between their ends too), out of a driveway or a parking bay across the lane, and round a 6 m lane's tight bend. Two cars meeting on a 6 m lane pull over onto the pavement to pass (the lamps, hydrants and bins there stand back from the kerb for it), a car keeps a little short of where another is about to drive, and one held nose to nose backs up a little out of the way. A new Big world's roads are 7 m wide, wide enough for two cars to pass in their lanes;
  - whether to cycle or walk to work is decided once, when the way is timed (a child timed at bike pace walked, and was late); a walk to work from a neighbour's is timed to the way out taken from there; late for work is judged by when the way gets them there;
  - a guest promised a bite stops waiting for it once nothing is coming;
  - the armoire, the party booth and the shower stall no longer have faces turned inside out, and the potting bench's seedling and the rolled yoga mat no longer show flat before they grow; the monitor takes the room on a top its screen's corners need;
  - someone out on a visit or an outing as their household's move comes round moves another day, instead of being taken from the host's home mid-visit.

### Also
- **A slower day, a finer clock:** a game day lasts 30 minutes at normal speed instead of 24 (a game minute is a second and a quarter), so a day goes by more calmly, and every faster speed is slowed alike. The simulation runs 60 times a second instead of 20, so residents and cars react within a sixtieth of a second, not a twentieth. Walking and driving keep their pace, so the same walk takes a little less of the day than before. A Big world keeps up at the fastest speed: planning that was worked out again every moment for what hadn't changed (a taxi's kerb, the way out of town) is kept.
- **Saves:** format 36, for the new clock: a game saved by 0.6.0 opens at the same day and minute, its cars where they were; 0.6.0 can't open 0.7.0's saves (it keeps them and lists only the ones it can open). Riding and a host's offer aren't saved: after a load, the bikes are home. A monitor on a turned corner desk it no longer fits is left out on load.
- **For pack authors and modellers:** new content: an object's `vehicle`, `visits.offer`, `objectRules.setUp` and `careerRules.performanceSlowdown`, `lateGraceMinutes` and `lateShare` ([docs/content-packs.md](docs/content-packs.md)). The model gate's checks with one right answer (a top's radius, the footprint, the wall, a floor thing floating, flipped faces and eleven more) are errors now, not warnings, unless the pack entry accepts one by name with a reason ([tools/art/README.md](tools/art/README.md), "The gate").
- **Known issues:** Big towns are a preview (*Big (preview)* on Create World, with a line saying what that means): at rush hour traffic can still jam at a busy junction near the camera, and taxi riders caught in it arrive late; looking elsewhere clears it, and 0.7.1 finishes it.
- **Known:** the fix for models drawn as boxes is not yet checked in Firefox itself.

### Open before the release (for the owner: take this section out before `gh release create`)
- The money balance after faster promotions: the balance test's richest household went from $30k to $50k (measured $37–43k), median weekly savings from about $624 to $1.6–2k; the knobs are the grades' pay in `careers.json` and the living costs, unchanged so far.
- The 30-minute late grace (`careerRules.lateGraceMinutes`): about half of the arrivals at work were late when it was set; lower it once the bikes and the work taxi make commutes punctual.
- Only 4 idea toasts show at once (`MAX_OPEN`), so a purchase request can be pushed off screen (it is still answerable in the Goals panel).
- Nothing to drink to serve: a host's offer brings only food (an `eat` use a guest could have themselves); drinks are plain interactions.
- The Firefox model-load fix (6ada1144) is not verified in Firefox.
- Two trips' cars no longer park in the same spot: with every spot near the place taken, the driver walks. In the 8 sample Big worlds over two days, 211 trips by car from 291.
- Parked-car tiles are coarse.
- **The bus is dropped** (the owner's call, 2026-11-05): `bus.rs`, its stops (Cosy and Normal towns' and a Big world's), the content's `bus`, the drawing and the town map's stops are out; a shared work taxi takes its place (`taxi.rs`). A town file's or a save's stops are no ways out and are left out as they load (still format 35). A Big world's residents with a long way out on foot and no car or bike for it ride the taxi from their kerb (no stops near every house as 0.6.0 had); Cosy and Normal towns are as before.
- Cars take turns where their ways cross or meet (`sim-core/src/traffic.rs`): one queue of claims, granted in the order asked, each only with its way through clear to its far side; claims for junctions' boxes (6 m round the middle, on along an arm only where it meets another at a narrow angle; where two roads cross or touch between their ends too, `travel::crossings`: a big world's country roads lie across lanes and along each other), driveway and bay mouths, and a 6 m lane's tight bend. A grant never waits on one that waits for it, however far round (the last tick's who-waits-for-whom), an ask older than 20 minutes yields only to one it waits for itself, a car held nose to nose with one waiting for it for 5 minutes backs up a little (3 minutes' driving at most, never into anyone), and nobody pulls over in a junction's box (the claims count no room to pass there). Stuck, as `generated_worlds` checks it: a car whose wait-for chain makes no progress for 10 game minutes, or that waits over 60. Measured over 12 game hours on every kept sample set (the CI set and 8 more, 9 × 48 Cosy and Normal worlds and 9 × 8 Big ones), with no bus:
  - Cosy and Normal: no overlaps, nobody stuck, the guard 0 ticks; drives out 30.3 to 32.6 minutes, home 28.8 to 30.2.
  - Big: no overlaps; one car stuck in the 72 worlds (Willowley: 66 minutes at a junction among a crowd of walkers on their long way out); the guard 207 ticks in one; drives out 76.5 to 83.6 minutes, home 80.3 to 83.5.
