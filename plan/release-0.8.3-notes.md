**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

New game in five steps: choose a land from nine places (a river valley, a heath and an island among them) and shape it with brushes from 1 to 120 m, its rivers flowing the way you draw them; lay out its streets and paint where homes, terraces, shops, parks and public places go; build the town from that and tweak it; choose one of the town's own households or make your own; and pick their home on the whole world. A world code makes the same world and the same people again. Towns are fuller, up to 768 residents, nearly every home lived in once you move in; the land can be shaped in the game too, under the roads and homes; four speeds, and super speed while the household sleeps or is out. Cars and riders no longer wait for each other for good, visits aren't cut short, the noon hitch is gone, a full town ticks faster, and the way into a game takes less memory, less still on an 8 GB machine.

- **New game in five steps:** a new game goes Land, Streets & zones, Build, Household and Home. The land is made first, with no town on it; its streets and what goes where are laid out on it; the town is built from that; then you choose who to play and the home they live in. You can go back to any step, and a step keeps what it made until what it was made from changes (a household made in Creation is kept). No world is made before the main menu any more: the menu shows a picture of the last world made (a shipped one on a first visit), and New game makes a fresh world in step 1, a panel ticking off its steps. Measured headless against 0.8.2 when it was made: the menu comes up in 1.55 s instead of 5.7, going back to it is instant instead of 2.1 s, and New game has its world built in 4.9 s.
- **Nine places, and the land's options:** step 1 offers nine places, each with a glyph of its own: the countryside, farmland, seaside, lakeside, a river valley, mountains, forest, heath and an island. Each is made with Size (Small, Medium or Large: 640, 832 or 960 m across), Hills (Flat, Rolling, Hilly or Mountains) and Woods (Open, Some or Wooded); a change makes the same world again with it. The choices sit in a slim panel down the left and the land fills the rest of the screen, its size, water and woods in figures. The heath is moorland: heather and bracken, more meadow, few trees with bushes among them. A river valley's river meanders across the world, 14 to 22 m wide, with the town on one bank; an island has a ragged coast and the sea to every edge, its high ground in the middle. The town's size (Hamlet, Village, Town or City) and its villages (None, Few or Many) are chosen in step 2, a size the land is too small for ruled out.
- **Bridges and causeways in stone:** a river is crossed by the town's bridge, square across to past the far bank, and by one or two more out in the country with Few or Many villages; an island's ways out are causeways, straight out past the edge of the world. They are stone: a deck 4 m wider than the road, parapets 0.9 m high with a coping and a post at each end, and a pier every 8 m or a little less, climbing with the road. Where a bridge meets the next country road there are no stop lines or crosswalks.
- **Shape the land:** step 1's Shape the land tab puts brushes on the land itself: Shape (raise, lower, smooth, flatten), Water (a lake, which pushes the coast back if begun on the sea; a river, which never runs uphill and takes the level of the water it meets; dry), Cover (woods, meadow, fields, clear) and Mountains (a peak or a range, drawn out as a ghost while you drag and rising when you let go, and level, which wears the land down). Brushes run from 1 to 120 m on one slider (mountains 60 to 400 m), and at 4 m and under change a single point of the land's 2 m grid. The left button paints, the cursor red where the brush would do nothing; undo, redo and reset are there, and the town is laid on the land as you leave it. A 120 m stamp takes under 3.1 ms (95th percentile) on both backends.
- **Rivers that flow:** the water moves now. A river runs the way it was drawn (a generated one along its course), its ripples carried downstream, quicker where it narrows or falls; where a drawn river steps down the land its water slopes down in a rapid with streaks of white water instead of standing in flat steps. Lakes and the sea ripple slowly with no way to them. A river drawn from dry land back over one already drawn turns it round. The water goes on moving while the game is paused and in New game's steps while it is in view (drawn 30 times a second then, still as before when none is in view), and slows to a quarter with Reduce motion. The sun's glare on water is held to a sheen, so a river seen from above no longer shows white. The frame costs no more on either backend that we could measure (M3 Pro).
- **Towns that follow the land:** a place's town is graded into its slopes instead of standing on flat ground: the roads are brought within the climb a road can make (the ways between places first, then each road out, then each trail; footpaths lie on the land as it is), each lot stands on a level pad by its road with a stone retaining edge where the land beside it is higher or lower, and the ground under a town or a village eases toward a plane tilted 6 % at most. The street, its kerbside things, its fences and bridges stand on that surface, cars pitch with the ground under their axles, residents ease over a step, and the far houses in the town view stand on their pads. Among mountains a village goes only where a country road can climb to it, and what a slope still leaves too steep (a lot dug in too deep, a building edged too tall, a dead end) is given up.
- **Streets & zones:** step 2 proposes a layout: the streets, and the town painted as zones of Homes, Terraces & flats, Shops & work, Parks & green and Public places. The zone brush paints them from 8 to 64 m, beside the streets only or anywhere, or erases; each stroke is one undo. Land too far from a street to be built on is hatched, and a summary counts the homes, terraces, shops, parks and public places Build will make (exactly, for a layout left as proposed). Clear all leaves bare land, Clear zones only the zones, Propose again gives another layout; the town's size, its villages and Move the town are here too. The way on is shut while a problem blocks it or fewer than two homes are zoned.
- **A Street tool that joins up:** a street is drawn with clicks or a drag. The pointer is pulled to a road, a dead end or the world's edge within 28 pixels, just past the kerb; a street meeting a road too near a junction slides along it to where there is room; the road it will finish on shows green ("Joins Far Lane — click to finish"), and Enter or a double click carries a street that stops short on to a road within 24 m. Its checks run as you draw (too steep, too near another road, a bend too tight), said in the words the town's settle uses; water it crosses is bridged where a straight deck of 45 m or less will do, and it is refused where not. Whatever the settle takes back shows red where it was for a moment, with why under the tools and in the Problems list.
- **Build:** step 3 builds the town from the zones, as the generator lays a town: the civic places on public land nearest the heart, the public lots, the terraces and the houses, then rows of shops, flats and offices along every road with land painted beside it; land zoned too far from a road builds nothing. Build again gives other houses on the same layout. The tools tweak what was built: Select (a garden snug, roomy or big, another use for a public place, a street, a village or the town renamed), Street (a street, a lane, a country road or a footpath, with what goes along it: homes, the town's buildings, both or nothing), Lot (a home, a farm, a public or an outdoor place, or a civic place by hand, where the generator's own rules would put one), Village (put one down, move it, remove it) and Remove; a street's card builds it up with the town's buildings or clears them. Every edit can be undone, and the Problems list says what is wrong ("No road to Ashbury"). On a big city an edit is settled and on screen again in 0.7 to 1.4 s (2 to 5 s before the work on it; M3 Pro): the land is eased only round what changed, the Problems list is checked in about 85 ms instead of 650, and the town is drawn again only where it changed. Going back to step 2 keeps your tweaks until the layout changes.
- **A household from the town:** step 4 offers 10 to 30 of the town's own households as cards, each least like those shown before it: portraits, who they are with their ages and traits, their home's street and village and its price band. Hovering or choosing a card lights its home and takes the camera there; Show others offers more, New neighbours rolls other people for the same town, and Create your own opens Creation. Game, Lifespan and how many neighbours are chosen here. A household of the town's may stay in its home or move to one for sale, its old home then for sale too.
- **A home picked on the whole world:** step 5 opens on the whole land, with chips that glide to the main town and each village and back out. Within savings (where a Living game opens) lights the homes for sale the household can afford; All for sale adds the rest as a faint stone ring; a Creative game lights them all. The home under the pointer says its price and bedrooms, and a click picks it and glides in. The homes are also a list for the keyboard, the main control on a narrow screen.
- **A world code:** every world has a code, such as `river-mhs-k2x9q` (type a number or paste a code, Copy it, or roll the dice for a new one), which makes the same land, the same proposed town and the same households again; brush strokes and town edits aren't in it. The households come from the code's own people seed, each home's from a stream of its own.
- **The camera is yours in New game:** in every step, right-drag turns and tilts the camera, middle-drag (or Space and left-drag, or left-drag with no tool in hand) pans, the wheel zooms toward the pointer (in to 10 m in steps 1 to 3), W A S D or the arrows pan, Q and E turn, R and F tilt, and Home frames the world again. The new game's town is lit as the game's is: its buildings cast the sun's shadows, and the land fades into a haze at its edges with no seam at the corners and no strip of ground standing out past it.
- **Fuller towns:** a town holds up to 768 residents (240 before) and 384 cars (128). Before you choose a home, the town's households leave about a quarter of the homes for sale; on Move in every home still for sale but a few of the cheapest gets a household, up to 680 neighbours (the largest worlds have about 390 homes), the player's street and two homes in each village first. Newcomers and births stop at the town's own limit. Each resident has about 35 ties to others, likelier with those living near, rather than a number growing with the town. Every graphics preset fills the town in full: 10× needs 600 ticks a second, and an M1 is estimated to run a full city at about 880. Parking by the public places grows with the homes (a space for every 25 in the main town, every 15 in a village).
- **Four speeds, and super speed in quiet hours:** the speeds are ⏸ pause, ▶ normal, ⏩ fast (3×), ⏩⏩ faster (10×) and ⏭ super speed (keys Space or 0, 1 to 3, and 4). Super speed runs as fast as your computer can, up to 60×, but only while everyone at home is asleep or out and nothing has happened there for an hour; the rest of the time its button is greyed out, saying why. When someone wakes or comes home the game drops back to the speed you had. The setting Super speed when quiet (it was Skip quiet hours) goes to it by itself. The 30× and 60× speeds are gone; a game saved at either opens in super speed, dropping back to 10×. At super speed only the streets round the camera are simulated in full detail (a seaside city: 215 residents in full detail on average down to 103, a game day's work 17 % less).
- **Shape the land in the game:** Build mode has a fourth tab, Land (G), with step 1's brushes for a game's own land, under the roads and the homes too: a road or a lot raised or lowered is settled within the cuts the land may take, and the street stands on the land as it now is. Water painted across a road bridges it there, or the stroke is refused with why; water is kept off the lots. The simulation's water changes with it, so residents walk round a new pond. In a Living game the land costs money (the cost so far at the cursor, a stroke stopped short of the funds, Undo refunding it); in Creative it is free.
- **Riders and cars that don't wait for each other for good:** a cyclist stepped out of a turning car's way rode straight back into it, and the two waited for each other for good (at Otter Weir 27 residents on their bikes for over two hours and about 50 cars behind them); those a car waits for now keep out of the ground it is waiting to drive on. A car turning round still counts the ground it backs over, so nobody is let into a stretch it is about to stand in (18 residents on their bikes stuck at Mirrorshore). A car held up by nobody pulls over only as far as it goes on with, rather than pulling over where it couldn't pull back in (36 on their bikes and 65 cars at Clover Acres, round two taxis). And a driver whose time to leave for work comes at the wheel finishes the drive first: they walked off to work and left the car on the road for good (21 on their bikes, 56 cars held up in view at Mirror Reach). The generated worlds that showed each pass with nothing held up in view.
- **Visits that aren't cut short:** someone asked over by phone gives up the taxi booked for an outing of their own (it came for them all the same and took them to the place, and three minutes after getting there they were off to the caller's); and someone out by taxi works out the way to work from home, where it brings them back, not as a walk of hours across the world from where it took them (Nia went home 38 minutes after getting to the place, nearly eight hours before leaving for work).
- **No hitch at noon:** a bed or a crib delivered (an adoption, a newcomer) looked through every object in town several times for each tile it tried; now only the objects that reach the plot. In a full seaside town of 25,941 objects the noon tick with adoptions took 363 to 535 ms; it takes 56 ms, and loading that town 1.1 s instead of 2.1.
- **A full town ticks faster:** what grew with the square of the town no longer does: the people within earshot, a claimed room's people (looked for among those living on its plot), those listening in on a conversation, and the relationships, copied whole for each resident added (a full town's loading grew with the cube of it). The rooms kept shut are worked out once a tick for each room, who is at full detail once a tick, the traffic's claims and the household's cars looked up by key, and each tile's steps kept with the walkable regions. Every save hash measured is as it was. Over a game day at 10×, with the streets round the camera in full detail, the slowest full city runs about 1,220 ticks a second in the browser's WebAssembly on an M3 Pro, against the 600 that 10× needs.
- **Less memory on the way into a game:** the houses, streets and fences are built in typed arrays instead of plain lists of numbers (72 bytes a vertex while built instead of 128): the page's JS heap peaks at 487 to 472 MB on the way in instead of 1,600 to 1,423. Model meshes keep no copy on the processor once on the graphics card (47.6 MB down to 0.4); textures let go of their source pictures once uploaded (the renderer's peak memory about 11 % lower); model files are read three at a time; the ground keeps its finest level only where it is near enough to draw. Measured over two 5-minute runs each on the M3 Pro, a shared machine, so the totals swing by hundreds of MB.
- **On 8 GB or less:** on a machine that says it has 8 GB or less (an 8 GB M1 does), the new game's town is let go as the loading screen comes up, before the game's world is built, so the two are never held together, and the game fades in instead of flying in from it; and the voices' engine and the language model start when a resident first wants a line, not as the page loads (that first line passes; the ones after it are said). With voices on, the page's own memory as the game comes in fell from 3.7–3.8 GB to 2.2–2.4 GB (two 5-minute runs each, M3 Pro, as an 8 GB machine).
- **Tidier generated towns:** a village only where its ground meets the town's within a road's climb; two ways out that face each other joined by the straight road between them; a country road never laid alongside another for more than 45 m; a pocket of land shut in behind gardens and houses joined to a garden; lots kept 3 m off a river's bank; careers whose workplace a steep slope took moved to another in the main town.
- **Under the hood:** `cargo run --release --features profile --example tickbench` prints where a tick's time goes, phase by phase; `tools/m1/` estimates the simulation's speed on an 8 GB M1 from a faster Mac, in a container under a CPU quota (no GPU, so not the frame time); the gameplay bench says where the memory goes (each worker's heap, Chrome's memory dumps, the entry's peaks, `?memtrace`, a census of what is kept).

Saves are format 40: 0.8.2's saves open, and 0.8.2 can't open 0.8.3's.
