**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

Cars on the driveways and the roads, bigger open worlds with hamlets and country roads, real garden fences and a garage of your own, every model rebuilt and every texture made in code, and a quicker, smoother game.

- **Bigger, open worlds:** Create World has a size: *Cosy* (the town as before), *Normal* (a town about 1.6 times as big, up to 256 m) and *Big* (a world of 896 to 1024 m: a main town, hamlets and countryside joined by country roads). The camera roams the whole world, and the town map pans and zooms to a hamlet. Residents travel it: on foot along the roads and trails, by bus from the stops, and in the household's car to work, to the next hamlet and back (whoever of the household goes along rides with them), parking at a public spot there. Out in the country are picnic meadows, lookouts, orchards and fishing jetties. The simulation keeps a Big world's 120 or so residents going at full speed, its ground stored and searched in chunks, and the renderer streams the world in by distance, so a Big world plays as smoothly as a town. Saved games keep their size; a town saved before is Cosy.
- **Country roads without hairpins:** in a new Big world the country roads no longer double back on themselves where they meet a hamlet or a town. A road leaves a place the way a real one does: the end of a street running straight on into it, or a T-junction square off a street on the side facing where it is going (a second road off a busy street end branches off the first), and where two places are close it runs straight from one to a street of the other. Out in the country it bends in wide curves, never turning more than 70° over any 40 m, keeps from looping round, and still meets other country roads square on. A Big world's houses no longer stand right at a street corner or on a tight bend, where a car pulling out of the driveway would cut across the verge; a bigger gap of land left between their back gardens goes to one of them, not shut in behind the fences. Saved games keep their worlds.
- **A big world filled:** a new Big world is a lived-in countryside, not a town in an empty field. Its main town is half as big again, its streets reaching all of its land; five to seven hamlets (a high street with its back lane, or a crescent round a green; the farm's country lanes and the forest's clearings) of 16 to 24 homes each are spread over the whole world; along the country roads stand farmsteads every 150 to 200 m (fields, a barn, an orchard beside them) and cottages where the roads leave a hamlet or meet; farmland in a patchwork with hedgerows lies round the hamlets and farms (not in the mountains or the forest), woods grow in more, smaller copses, and trees dot the open land. It starts with as many residents as before (about 120), so more of its homes are for sale. Saved games keep their worlds.
- **Streets that join up:** a new world's streets are one connected town, not a tree of dead ends. A lane goes on round to the next street (meeting it at a proper junction, its kerbs and sidewalks cut cleanly) or joins another lane into one, so the streets make blocks; a town keeps a court or two at most (three in a big world's main town), none longer than 45 m, with its turning circle. Each kind keeps its character: a dense town is a loose grid of blocks round its roundabout, a medium town's lanes become a back street and run round to its crescent, a small town's high street has a back lane looping off it, the seaside and lakeside towns get an upper street inland, the mountain tracks and a bigger clearing's lanes come back to their road where the land allows. Bigger towns' extra streets are cross streets between two roads. In a big world, a town's main road no longer trails off at its edge: it goes on as a country road to the nearest one, another hamlet or the edge of the world, and a second road from a hamlet branches off the first instead of leaving by the same end. Saved games keep their streets.
- **Trees keep out of the houses:** a tree's crown no longer grows through a house wall. New towns' gardens plant trees far enough from every house, theirs or a neighbour's, and planting one too close in Furnish says so (content `crown`).
- **A garage of your own:** Build › Construct has a Garage tool. Point at your driveway and the garage goes at its end, beside the house, its door to the street, as the town builds them (the panel's *At the end of the driveway* does it in one click); anywhere else on your lot it stands on whole tiles where you point, and R turns its door to face any of the four ways. The preview is green where it fits and red where it doesn't, saying why: one garage per home, outdoors on the ground floor, on your own lot, clear of walls, fences, furniture, residents and where the car parks, and never shutting anyone or anything in. It costs $2,500 (content `build.garage`; free in Creative), and a click on it (or Delete) takes it down for $1,500, like furniture sold; both can be undone. It stands on your lot in every wall view and from the street, and nobody walks through it. A household car whose driveway it stands at the end of, its door towards the road, parks in front of it, nose in, and goes back to its old spot once the garage is gone. Saves keep which way a garage you built faces and where its car parked before (`garages[].rot`, `cars[].driveSpot`; older saves and towns load as they were). A new game's town has no cars of its own in the simulation yet (the street draws the neighbours' parked), so there the garage stands empty.
- **Real garden fences:** a new town's picket fences, clipped hedges and farm rail fences round its back gardens and fields are real fences now: nobody walks through them any more, residents go round by the front of the house or through a gate. Each garden is closed off tidily behind its house: a short fence from each side of the house to the plot's edge with a gate in it, the sides back to the back fence, one fence where two gardens meet (a narrow gap between them goes to one of them), corners joined, and clear of driveways so car doors still open. They are the same whichever lot the camera is on, on the lot in view too, and yours are yours to take down, move or restyle in Build mode, where *Clipped hedge* is a new fence style. Front gardens stay open. Games saved before keep their towns as they were, with fences only drawn as before, but now tidy and the same whichever lot is in view, and still walked through.
- **Cars:** most households have a car on their driveway, the one you play too: your home keeps the driveway and car the town showed. Only its owners drive it, and only from 18: the household's grown-ups when the town was made (a household left without one hands it down to its grown-ups). Those going by car leave early enough to be at work on time (the walk to the car, the drive out of town and on to work), and their shift starts at its hour, not as they get in. An owner walks to the driver's door, it opens, they step in and sit, and it shuts before they're gone from view; they wait a moment for the rest of the household leaving at the same time (children off to school too), each getting in by a door of their own, never one with no room to swing open (next to the house, say). The car backs or pulls out and drives off along the street out of town. Whoever finishes first waits at work for the others if they're done within the hour, and the car brings them home, nose in; its doors open and they step out. A rider whose driver works on longer walks home as before. A parked car takes up its spot: nobody walks through it and nothing can be put there. A car on the move stops for anyone in its way (who steps aside after a moment), and nobody walks into one driving by. Garages stand where the town showed them, your home's too, and can't be walked through or built on. When a household moves house, its car goes with it to the new driveway. The neighbours' cars come and go as well. A car whose spot was built over while it was out stays put, unused, and isn't drawn.
- **Bedrooms are their sleepers' own:** only those who belong together share a bedroom. While someone sleeps in one, its door is open to them, their partner, parents, children, brothers and sisters with a bed there, and whoever minds a crib in there, and keeps everyone else, guests included, out. The other half of a double bed is the partner's (or a child's for the night); a bunk bed is a bed for each. A lodger, even one sent to the bed, waits outside like anyone else; walking through a bedroom to a room beyond is fine.
- **A calmer interface:** the top bar is three small islands in one row, with the lot showing between them (the clock and speeds take a second row only in a small window or at a large interface scale), and Undo, Redo and the eyedropper sit in the Build dock beside its tabs. The Furnish catalog has a fixed height: the categories along its top with their names, a grid of items below, and a search; the sort, the filters and the group chips are gone (each category still lists its groups in order), as is the *Build catalog* size setting. A quiet interface while watching can't be clicked by accident.
- **Newcomers and bed places:** households moving into town are a single, a couple (perhaps with a grown child) or siblings, never strangers, and bed places count a double bed as one place unless a couple sleeps in it (who moves in, the no-bed notice, the beds the town buys for its children).
- **Furniture measured by itself:** what a model only carries is drawn but no longer counted in its size: a desk's chair, the picture, vase and jewellery box on a chest of drawers, the blocks in front of a toy box, the climbing frame's flag, the block and bottle beside a rolled yoga mat, and the dressing table's pouf. The chest of drawers is 0.89 m tall again, not 1.35 with the picture; the dressing table is 0.42 m deep, not 0.86 with the pouf out in front. Those parts are joined into one node named `<what>_aside` (`G.aside`), which the art build and `cargo check-glb` leave out of the size; the content's heights (the dresser's surface, the desk's seat) stay the furniture's own.
- **The trunk door of the grandfather clock opens:** *Wind the clock* turns the key and swings the trunk's door
  open for a moment instead of only the pendulum's door above. A piece of furniture that wants a fifth moving part
  can now say so (`"draws": 5` in its pack entry), where the draw-call gate used to allow four at most.
- **No talking through walls:** residents talk, and listen in, only where they can see each other, never through a wall, a window or the shut front door; someone going over to talk to a person the other side of a wall goes round by the door, and nobody calls out through the front door. Whoever answers the door steps out onto the path to say hello to the visitor, rather than talking to them through the shut door.
- **The neighbours' gardens:** the things in your neighbours' gardens (garden furniture, planters, loungers, swings) stand there while you play, as the town seen from the menus shows them, instead of bare patches in the grass where they should be.
- **Grass past the edge of town:** the lawn's grass no longer stops in a line a little way out of town. Its blades grow on over the whole landscape round the town, however far out the camera goes, thinning out with distance from the camera as they do in town, and the land beyond keeps the lawn's colour and its gusts of wind, fading gently into the far hills.
- **A basement you can see:** looking at the basement, your lot's garage, car, driveway and garden paths go with the rest of the ground instead of floating over the dug-out lot, the earth round the basement is earth-brown instead of a near-black slab, and rooms nobody is in (their lamps off) are softly lit enough to see what's in them, still well below a room with its lamps on. That soft light is as bright at night as by day, with no moonlight's blue down there. A new game's lamps are on only in rooms someone is in (they all came on before, and stayed on in a basement nobody went down to); residents switch them as they come and go, as before. The basement's walls have clean tops: no dark stone ledge along them and no dark notches at every post, which showed once it was lit.

- **Textures:** every surface map, the 19 texture sets and the sidewalk, is now generated in code by `cargo tex synth` (`crates/tools/src/tex/synth.rs`) from tileable height fields, and is original CC0. The download/process pipeline for them is gone; `web/public/assets/CREDITS.md` and `tools/art/README.md` describe the new flow.

- **An apron for the servers:** the town's vendors and shopkeepers wear a green apron over their white shirt and
  dark trousers, as a counter hand would. It is a garment of its own (`top.apron`), so it is never picked for a
  resident: a shell over the front from the waistband to just above the knee, turned back on every edge, and it
  lies on the lap when they sit.
- **Smoother moving parts, cleaner edges:** every model was rebuilt. Anything that loops (a rocking chair, a fan, a
  treadmill's belt, a merry-go-round) no longer stops at rest for a moment each time round: its clip started 1/24 s
  late, and the game held it until then. Clips are now keyed 30 times a second, as the game plays them. Round tops
  and caps (a table's top, a lamp's base, a can's lid) stay flat to their rim instead of shading into their side,
  rounded edges read as rounded instead of as a flat facet, thin round legs and tubes look round, and furniture
  darkens a little where it meets the floor. The models are about 18 % smaller for the same triangles.
- **Doors that swing like doors:** cupboard, wardrobe, fridge and cabinet doors (45 of them) open with a quick swing
  that carries a little past open and settles back, and close with a hair more opening before they swing shut,
  instead of the same slow-in, slow-out as everything else. Moving parts are sampled 60 times a second, so a
  quick swing or a fast spin no longer shrinks a hair between samples. Far versions of shop counters, stalls,
  playground rides and street props no longer have faces turned inside out, which showed dark where the sun
  fell on them.
- **Wood, fabric and leaves that follow the shape:** the textured finishes are laid on each part of a model as it
  was made, not projected along the model's axes: wood grain runs along each leg, rail and board and up a turned
  base, bark runs up the trunk and round it, and a tree's clumps of leaves are leaves all over instead of smeared
  into streaks where they turn. Polished marble, worktops and fruit no longer shine like mirrors.
- **Shadows shaped like the furniture:** the soft shadow on the floor under each thing is its own shape, turned
  with it, instead of the same round blob for everything: darkest where it stands on the floor (a cabinet's base,
  a chair's feet, a table's legs), lighter under a seat or a table top the higher it is, and spilling a little
  past its edge. All of them are still one draw.
- **Quicker to load, smoother to play:** a game loads behind one loading screen whose bar only goes forward: the models, textures and pictures play needs are fetched eight at a time (none can hold the screen for ever), read off the page, and kept in the browser's cache for the next visit, and the catalog's pictures and the residents' portraits are drawn ahead in idle moments. New worlds and town files are made off the page, so choosing another world on the new-world screen no longer stalls the menus, and the grass and the land's shading are built off it too. In play, cars coming and going no longer make the whole town find its way again, the town's commutes aren't all worked out again at midnight, and the world's changes reach the renderer as small updates, applied once a frame. Far-off residents and the street are drawn more cheaply (the street's far things in their far versions, nothing drawn that is hidden, the sun's shadows only redrawn where something moves), the open world's distances follow the graphics preset, and Low draws a leaner picture. The voices and the language model wait while a game loads and its town streams in, then speak.
- **Riding in the car:** whoever rides in a car sits in their seat and moves with it as it drives, instead of trailing behind it; the wheels turn and steer.
- **On the way:** a resident's panel says where they are off to and about when they'll be there ("On the way to the Smiths · there about 14:20"); the town map says where the others are going.
- **Keys and menus:** Esc closes what is on top first (a dialog, a panel), then opens the pause menu; the game's keys rest while a dialog is open; the middle mouse button turns the camera; the town map's clicks and labels land where they should on the bigger maps. A saved game goes on at the speed it was saved at once it is loaded.

### Also
- **Saves:** format 35: where every car is, who owns it, every house's driveway and the garages (34), and the open world's size, roads and hamlets (35). Older saves open as Cosy towns with no cars in the simulation: their neighbours' cars stay parked, as before. 0.5.x can't open 0.6.0's saves: it keeps them and lists only the ones it can open.
- **Known:** loading a Big world holds the game for up to a second once, while every resident's way to work is worked out.
