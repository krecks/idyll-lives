
**Play it in your browser:** https://krecks.github.io/idyll-lives/

Big towns that work: every town is a big one now, every junction is a real one with traffic lights and crosswalks, and cars, taxis and bikes go at the pace of the game. Residents who look like real people, each with a face of their own, and a world in daylight: a lawn with life in it, warm sun and cool shade, cloud shadows, worn streets, leaves on the trees. And stairs in three shapes, four more bikes, beds and sofas for a family with a grown child, and resolution as 720p to 2160p. Save format 37: 0.7.0's saves open, and 0.7.0 can't open 0.8.0's.

- **Only big towns:** a new game's town is always a big one, its hamlets joined by country roads; Cosy and Normal are gone from the neighbourhood creator. Every town houses up to 160 residents. Games saved in a Cosy or a Normal town still open.
- **Junctions with real traffic lights:** every junction where three roads or more meet is lit (in the biggest towns only the first few hundred lights were), and the arms across from each other go green together: a crossroads in two turns, a T in two (the road through, then the side road), a car turning across the way of those coming the other way waiting for them to pass. Each arm has a crosswalk at the corner and a stop line just short of it, where cars wait on red, the light's pole beside it on the kerb; a car let through just as the light changes but held up before the line stops after all. Someone on foot crossing at a junction waits at the kerb for the walk, which their waiting brings round: every arm red, a few seconds to step out and time to get across, then the cars again. The lights where a lane begins at a road's kerb stand round the middle of the road, not off towards the lane. The roundabouts work as before.
- **Vehicles at the pace of the game:** cars, the work taxi, bikes, e-scooters and the kids' bike go on the traffic's own clock, four times as fast as real time: cars drive at 32 km/h in the hamlets (21.6 before) and 58 on country roads, speed up quicker, keep their speed straight through a junction (the lights stop them) and take bends a little faster, so a drive across a big world takes 20 to 25 game minutes, 50 at most, instead of two to five hours, and a bike's ride reaches four times as far in the same game minutes. Everything about how they move and take turns keeps its shape (speeding up and slowing down, bends, how far ahead they look, turns at junctions, jams and the traffic lights' timing all run on the same clock), so a junction lets four times as many through as before and traffic flows as it did, only faster; walking, getting in and out and the taxi's stand for its riders keep their pace. Etas follow: the taxi beats a walk sooner, and visits and outings further off fit in the day by taxi or bike; the town's taxis for visits and outings are six at a time, there and back (with no limit a big world's evening had twenty out and jammed in view), and with all six out someone setting off goes another way or somewhere nearer. Saves from before (format 36) load with every car and taxi where it was along its way (format 37).
- **Junctions that work as junctions:** two side roads meeting a road a few metres apart, either side of it, are one crossroads with one set of lights (each was a T with its lights standing in the middle of the other); a car doesn't drive onto a crosswalk without room to clear it, so nobody waits on one; and where two lit junctions are too near each other to wait between, a car waits at the first's stop line till it can go through both on their greens. Those on foot cross at a lit junction on its zebras, on the walk, never through the middle of the junction or just past a zebra, and someone walking by a crosswalk on the pavement no longer stands there waiting for the walk; keeping them off the road there never shuts a house's gate or a bit of pavement off. A new world no longer lays a country road off a street's end where another street carries on.
- **Junctions that look like junctions:** where roads meet, one surface of asphalt joins them, its corners rounded by a 3 m kerb return, and the sidewalks turn the corner with their kerb, where they used to stop square and leave a bare square of road at each corner. Every arm of a junction into a street or lane has a white zebra crosswalk across it just out past the junction and a stop line across the lane coming in, where cars wait on red; a country road's arm gets only the stop line, while its lights are lit. Town sidewalks stop at a country road's mouth instead of running across it. Each traffic light's pole has a small head for those on foot facing across the road: a red standing figure, and a green walking one while the junction's walk phase is on, when every car head shows red. Roundabouts look as before.
- **Resolution as 720p, 1080p, 1440p or 2160p:** Settings › Performance › Resolution picks how many lines of pixels the game draws, 1080p to begin with, never more than the screen has (on a 1080p screen 1440p and 2160p draw its own 1080). On a Retina or 4K screen 1440p or 2160p is sharp, and 720p or 1080p runs faster. Auto and the 50–100 % slider are gone; a setting saved with either starts on 1080p.
- **Dev play log** (dev server only): every page load writes what happens while playing to `dev-logs/<time>.jsonl`: the browser, screen and GPU, console warnings and errors, screens and settings changes, games opened and closed, a sample every 5 seconds (frames, the resolution drawn, draw calls, the simulation, memory, the game's time and speed), resolution changes and stalls between frames.
- **Garden gates against houses and hedges:** a fence or gate meeting a house wall hangs from the wall instead of a post standing in it, a hedge beside a gate stops short of the gate's post instead of burying it in its rounded end, and a wooden gate's brace no longer flickers in the rails behind it.
- **Residents on the land:** out on a big world's hills between the hamlets, residents walk and ride on the ground, not sunk into it or floating over a hollow.
- **Bedtime for a family with a grown child:** the parents' double bed is theirs. A son or daughter with no bed of their own sleeps on the sofa (*Sleep on the sofa*: lying along it under a blanket, curled up on a two-seater under a throw pulled up to the neck and tucked round the seat, at full length on a three-seater) rather than in the parents' bed, and takes it only with nowhere else at all; a child shares a parent's bed for the night only with the other parent out. Before, an eighteen-year-old who went to bed first took half of it and his father, kept out of the bedroom, fell asleep on the landing. A race car bed is a child's: a generated house no longer gives one (nor toys only a child may use) to a teenager, a neighbour household swaps it for a bed when its child turns thirteen, and the player is told the teenager has no bed. Worn out with the bladder low, a resident sees the toilet through before going to bed, instead of being up from one for the other until they fall asleep on the floor. The quilts of a bed upstairs are drawn on its storey (they were drawn on the ground floor, under the house, so sleepers upstairs lay uncovered).
- **A house looks the same from the street and inside:** a neighbour's house with a porch at the front door (a gabled canopy on two posts over a white step, a lamp beside the door) still has it when you go into that household, where the door used to open straight onto the path. It is the same porch, drawn over the front door the garden path meets, so a door moved off the path or out of the front wall in Build mode takes it with it; it is only for looks (the doorway and the path are walked as before), and in the cutaway or with the walls down its canopy and lamp go with the front wall, its posts drop to stubs and its step stays. The player's house in the menus' town has it too. A two-storey house's upper floor and roof are those of the house below, the same as from the street: its look was picked again for the upper floor (brick upstairs over siding, say, under a roof of another colour), and the roof the player chose for a two-storey house showed only from the street.
- **Roofs that meet at corners are one roof:** where the storey above stands on the middle of the one below and the walls are square to each other, the roof left round it (an L, a U or a ring) was roofed a rectangle at a time, each rectangle with its own hips, ridge and eaves: two surfaces lay over the same tiles where they met and butted together at every corner, a step in the slope, a ridge that stopped and started again, a fascia inside the roof. The area is roofed whole now: one line of eaves runs round its outline, and the faces meet in hips, ridges and valleys, as a house with that plan would be roofed.
- **Stairs in three shapes:** the Stairs tool picks from three: the closed flight as before, an open flight of 14 oak boards on two slim steel plates, and a spiral of 14 wedge treads winding round a steel post over two tiles by two. A spiral is walked onto from any side of its square and left from the corner its last step reaches.
- **Four more bikes:** beside the city bike there is a mountain bike (knobbly tyres, a suspension fork), a road bike (drop bars, thin tyres, the quickest), a cargo bike (a box up front) and an e-bike (a battery in the frame, a motor at the pedals). A new town's gardens mix them, the city bike still the most common.
- **Residents with real proportions:** a head is a real body's share of its height (it was a seventh larger on a grown-up, nearly twice on a baby), the eyes a real size and as far apart, the arms and feet a real length (the shoes were clogs). Ears have a rim and a hollow and are a real ear's size; the teeth have lines between them and the nose's underside its shade; a hand at rest curls a little more towards the little finger.
- **A face of their own:** every resident's face is their own: jaw, chin, cheeks, nose, brow, eyes, mouth and the face's length vary from one to the next, a child's face is blended from the parents', and the household creator has a slider for each. Residents from saved games get a face of their own as they load.
- **Skin, eyes, hair and clothes in the light:** skin lets a little light through, warm at the edge of a shadow, with a softer highlight; the whites of the eyes lie in the lids' shade; hair has a sheen across its strands where the sun or a lamp catches it. Clothes stand off the body as far as they are loose: a tee hangs from the chest, a trouser leg falls straight over the shoe, a cuff stands off the arm. Tees, v-necks, polos and long tops are worn out over the trousers, not tucked in.
- **Classic shading by default:** the game draws its lit surfaces with physically based shading, so skin, grass and wood keep their own colours. The painterly Soft look is still in Settings; a game set to Soft before starts on Classic once, since Soft was only the old default.
- **Things shade what they stand on:** on Medium and High a sofa shades the wall it stands against, a chair's legs the floor and a pot the sill (contact shading), so a lamplit room has depth too. Low leaves it out.
- **Rooms in the shine:** a glossy thing indoors reflects the room round it, not the open sky: a chrome tap is bright in a lit room and dark in a dark one, by day and at night.
- **Leaves on the trees:** the oak, maple, birch, cherry, apple, magnolia, Japanese maple, willow and lemon tree have crowns of leaves, sprays of them over a darker core, where they had lumps under a pattern of leaves. Far off they are as before.
- **A calmer picture:** the lawn, most of what you see while you play, is quieter: its blades are close to one tone from root to tip instead of dark roots under bright yellow tips, and its green is a little deeper and less yellow, so the house and the people in it stand out. The picture's corners are less darkened, and a garden at night is dim under the moon, not near black.
- **A lawn with life in it:** the lawn is no longer one green from fence to fence: it has drier patches and lusher ones, a few metres across, the same ones every time you come back to a lot. From where you play it is calmer too: the ground between the blades is nearly the blades' own tone, so the grass no longer flickers as the camera moves. A lot is mown short and even, and the land between and beyond the lots is rough: longer, darker grass in clumps. Most lots show the mower's stripes, faintly. The grass is darker and lusher at the foot of walls, hedges and fences, most where their shade lies, and under trees. Feet have worn it to bare earth outside a door, at a gate, where a path ends, under a swing and before a bench, and beds and trunks stand in soil. Its flowers grow in drifts, as daisies and clover do, instead of one here and one there, with a patch of daisies, buttercups or violets here and there in a garden.
- **Warm sun, cool shade:** by day the sun is warmer and stronger and the sky's light cooler, so a lawn in the sun, the shade of a tree on it and a room lit through its windows differ in colour, not only in how light they are.
- **Cloud shadows:** by day soft, light shadows of clouds drift slowly over the land, so part of a garden lies in the sun and part in a cooler shade. They hold still with Reduce motion.
- **Things sit on the lawn:** the grass is shaded round a house's walls, along fences and hedges, under and round garden chairs, benches, beds and the water butt, and round a tree's trunk, so they stand on the ground instead of on a picture of it.
- **Cut walls look cut:** the tops of the walls cut away in front of a room are the grey of a wall sawn through, not bright white bars: the eye goes to the people and the furniture, not to the outline of the rooms. Nothing white is left on them: the small white ticks beside a doorway next to an outside wall are gone, and the cut ends of door frames and corner boards are grey too.
- **Floors with a middle and edges:** a floor is darker along the walls and lighter out in the room, palest in the doorways where everyone walks, so a room is no longer one flat colour from wall to wall.
- **Walls with depth:** a room's walls are darker at the floor and into the corners and vary a little over their length, where each was one flat tone.
- **A street that has been driven on:** the road is paler where wheels run and darker between them and along the kerb, with a patch of newer asphalt here and there; the pavement has a grey kerb and a course of grey stone at its back, and a darker gutter.
- **Paths that belong in the garden:** a garden path and a drive are darker and mossy along their sides, where they meet the lawn, instead of ending with a ruler's edge.
- **Finished roofs:** a hip roof has a course of cap shingles up each of its hips, as its ridge has.
- **Petals under a tree in blossom:** under a garden's tree in blossom its fallen petals lie pink on the grass.
- **Trees, shrubs and hedges with the light on them:** seen from above, a tree's crown, a shrub and a clipped hedge have a lighter, sunlit top over darker sides and undersides, where they were one dark green all over, and two trees or two shrubs of a kind differ more in their green. A clipped hedge is no longer ruler-straight: its top and sides are in soft mounds. The borders along the houses have beds of lupins and marigolds among their shrubs, in dark soil.
- **A smaller download for the residents:** the bodies, faces, hair and clothes are 3.9 MB to download instead of 5.1. A finer copy of the body, for close views, was sent with every game and used by none; it now comes only to the household creator, whose resident stands close enough for it to show: the outline of the head, the jaw, the shoulders and a tee's neckline are smooth curves there, where they had corners.
