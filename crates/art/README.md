# Item models in Rust: porting and authoring a family

The game's item models (`web/public/assets/models/<family>/<id>.glb`) are generated by code, a family at a time.
A family not yet ported is a Python script run inside Blender 5.2 (`tools/art/blender/families/`, on `gamekit/`);
a ported one is a Rust module here that builds the same files without Blender. This is the guide for porting a
family, and later for writing a new one. The standard a model meets is
[docs/design/objects.md](../../docs/design/objects.md); the gate that holds it to it is described in
[tools/art/README.md](../../tools/art/README.md).

## What is where

| Crate | What it is |
|---|---|
| `crates/artkit` | The kernel: gamekit's builders, Blender's own tools a script calls, the export. It mirrors gamekit name for name, and Blender's own arithmetic wherever a point, a normal or a ray hangs on it ([src/lib.rs](../artkit/src/lib.rs) lists the modules) |
| `crates/art` (`ll-art`) | The runner, `cargo art` ([src/lib.rs](src/lib.rs)): the registry, where a family is built, `--out`, `--check`, `--preview` |
| `crates/art/<group>` | The families, six crates so they build side by side |
| `crates/tools` (`ll-tools`) | `cargo check-glb`: the gate, and the comparison with another build (`--against`) |

| Crate (folder) | Families (`cargo art <name>`) |
|---|---|
| `art-nature` (`nature`) | trees, plants, garden_things, garden_decor, ground, street |
| `art-wet` (`wet`) | kitchen, kitchen_cooking, kitchen_counters, bathroom, laundry |
| `art-rooms` (`rooms`) | living, dining, office, decor, lighting, bedroom, bedroom_storage, bedroom_dressing |
| `art-packs` (`packs`) | pack_bookworm, pack_cheerful, pack_couchPotato, pack_energetic, pack_example, pack_foodie, pack_gloomy, pack_hotHeaded, pack_kind, pack_lazy, pack_loner, pack_natureLover, pack_neat, pack_outgoing, pack_romantic, pack_slob |
| `art-outside` (`outside`) | yard, outdoor, playground, farm, street_furniture, stalls, shops |
| `art-things` (`things`) | hobbies, kids, held, tabletop, media, fitness, birds |

A family is a folder, `crates/art/<group>/src/<family>/` (snake case: `pack_couch_potato` for `pack_couchPotato`):

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `mod.rs` | The palette, the family's constants, and `pub fn family() -> Option<Family>`: every model with its facts. `None` until it is ported |
| `common.rs` | The script's own helpers (`_slab`, `_turned`, `_finish`), a function each |
| `<group>.rs` | The models, a file a group of them as the script had its sections (`chairs.rs`, `tables.rs`), a `pub fn <model>() -> Vec<Obj>` each |
| `NOTES.md` | The script's `<family>.md`, moved here: what the models are, the facts they keep, the faults found |

The group's `src/lib.rs` lists its families by name (`families()`); all 49 names are there already.
`crates/art/rooms/src/dining` and `crates/art/nature/src/trees` are finished ports to read first.

## The loop

```sh
OUT=/tmp/art/dining                                  # a scratch root, outside the repo
cargo art dining --out $OUT                          # build: the files, the pack and centres.json under $OUT
cargo art dining --only barStool,sideboard --out $OUT
cargo check-glb --against ref/blender --family dining --dir $OUT   # compare with Blender's build
cargo check-glb --against ref/blender model.barStool --dir $OUT    # after --only: by key
cargo art dining --only barStool --preview --sheet --out $OUT      # look: pictures, nothing else written
cargo art dining --check                             # once switched: the committed bytes, nothing written
cargo art --all --check                              # every switched family
cargo check-glb model.barStool                       # the gate, on the files in the tree
cargo check-glb --validate                           # every model, with the Khronos validator
```

**Build.** `--out <dir>` writes under a root laid out as the repo is (`web/public/assets/models/<family>/`,
`packs/<pack>/manifest.json`, `tools/art/blender/centres.json`; the manifests it needs are copied in). Each model
prints its measured size, its triangles and what it wrote; a model that fails its size check prints the measured
size and centre to copy. No gate runs on a scratch root: the comparison's `gate` row does that.

**Compare.** `ref/blender` is a tag: the last build of every family by Blender. The comparison reads only the
files, the packs and `centres.json`, and prints a line a model, a far version (`<key> far`) and a manifest:

```
same     model.tableBarHigh
near     model.diningOak  (differs in shape, uv, shading; surface 0.000 mm mean, 0.000 mm at most; texture 0.68 % off; shading 0.05 greys)
29 models, 0 far versions and 1 manifests: 8 same, 22 near, 0 CHANGED
```
```
CHANGED  model.<id>  (...)
         surface: <part>: the new file's point (x, y, z) is 3.100 mm from the old surface, at most 2.000 mm
```

- `same`: the same nodes, materials, triangles and clips to the bit, in whatever order the file has them, and the
  same pack entry and centre.
- `near`: not the same, and every metric within its limit. `differs in` names what of the file differs (`nodes`,
  `materials`, `shape`, `uv`, `shading`, `clips`); the numbers are the worst part's: its surface's mean and
  largest distance from the other build's, the share of its normals turned over 15°, the share of its texture
  over 2 mm off, its shading's mean difference in greys (of 32), a material's change in triangles.
- `CHANGED`: a metric over its limit, each on a line of its own: the metric, the part, the value, the limit and the
  worst point (in the part's own axes, which are glTF's: x is the game's -x). The command exits 1.

The metrics and their limits open [against.rs](../tools/src/checkglb/against.rs): origins within 0.1 mm, the
surface within 0.2 mm on average and 2 mm at most (0.5 mm for a moving part and for fruit), clips sampled at
60 Hz, perches within 1 mm, sizes and functional heights within 0.5 mm, no new finding of the gate, the pack entry
and the centre the same. After `--only`, name the keys: `--family` asks for every model of the family, and the
ones not built are `CHANGED` for want of a file.

**Look.** `--preview` builds in memory and draws each model from its file as the game reads it, into
`tools/art/blender/previews/<family>/` (ignored by git): `<id>.png`, `<id>@<clip>.png` (each clip where it is most
open), `<id>.far.png`, `<id>@perches.png`; `--sheet` adds `<id>.sheet.png` (four sides, the baked shading, each
clip along its length). Before the switch give it `--out` too, or the pictures are the script's, rendered in
Blender. The lit, textured view is the game's: `bun run dev` in `web/`, then `/gallery.html?keys=model.barStool`
and `/inspect.html?key=model.barStool`; they draw the files in the tree, and a `.glb` dropped onto the inspector
is shown too (tools/art/README.md, at its end).

**Switch**, when every model is `same` or `near` and each `near` is understood (below): delete
`tools/art/blender/families/<family>.py` (unless other scripts import it), move `<family>.md` to `NOTES.md` with
what the comparison found, end the `Family` with `.switched()`, and have its comment (the pack's `$comment`) name
the module that builds it. The family's committed files are rebuilt when the branch is merged, not by the port.

**Check.** `--check` builds a switched family in memory and fails on any byte that differs from the committed
files, pack entries or centres: a build gives the same bytes every time. It holds only where the files were built
by the tree's own kernel: after a kernel fix it names the files that moved until they are rebuilt.

**The gate.** `cargo check-glb [keys]` prints `ok` or `FAIL` a model, with its errors and warnings; `cargo art`
without `--out` runs it on what it built. `--validate` needs `bun install` in `web/` once.

**CI** runs, of this (`tools/ci-local.sh <step>`): `fmt` and `clippy` over the workspace, the families too;
`tools`, the tests of `artkit` (the kernel against what was captured from Blender, `crates/artkit/tests/golden`),
of `ll-art` (the routing, one model end to end) and of `ll-tools` (the gate over every shipped model); `models`,
`cargo check-glb --validate`; `sizes` and `uses`, `cargo review sizes --check` and `cargo review uses --check`. It
runs neither the comparison nor `--check`: those are the porter's.

**Where a family is built** is the registry's to say (`route` in the runner), not whether its script is left:

| The family is | `cargo art <family>` | with `--out` or `--check` | `--all` |
|---|---|---|---|
| switched (`.switched()`) | here | here | here (`--all --check`: these only) |
| ported, not switched | its script, in Blender, with a line saying so | here | its script; `--all --out`: here; `--all --check`: left out |
| not ported (`None`) | its script, in Blender | an error | its script; left out with `--out` or `--check` |

## The call style

A port reads as the script did. Each Python function is one Rust function; its keyword arguments are a struct of
options with `Default`, and a macro a function sets the ones named, so a misspelt option does not compile:

```python
F.box('top', 1.2, 0.03, 0.8, (0, 0.735, 0), M.oak, bevel=0.004)
G.cylinder('leg', 0.02, 0.7, at=(0.5, -0.3, 0.35), material=M.oak, verts=G.low(12, 6))
with G.new_parts() as door:
    ...
door = G.hinged(G.join(door, 'door'), F.at(-0.49, 0.5, 0.19))
G.settle()
G.swing(door, 'door_open', -100, seconds=0.9, ease='back_out')
```
```rust
fbox!(F, "top", 1.2, 0.03, 0.8, (0, 0.735, 0), M.oak, bevel = 0.004);
cylinder!("leg", 0.02, 0.7, at = (0.5, -0.3, 0.35), material = M.oak, verts = low(12, 6));
let door = new_parts(|| {
    // ...
});
let door = hinged(join(&door, "door", None), F.at(-0.49, 0.5, 0.19));
settle_all();
swing!(door, "door_open", -100, seconds = 0.9, ease = Ease::BackOut);
```

- **Arguments** go as written: what the script gave by place first, then the options in the order it wrote them
  (the macro works them out in Python's order). A whole number stays whole (`0`, not `0.0`: see the traps), a tuple
  is a point, an option that may be absent takes the bare value. The options are the kernel's structs
  (`shapes::Cube`, `Round`, `Sphere`, `Revolve`; `forms::Sweep`, `Loft`, `Extrude`; `soft::SoftBox`;
  `motion::Swing` ...): read their defaults there.
- **A family's own helper** is a plain function with every parameter, the defaults written at each call
  (`#[allow(clippy::too_many_arguments)] // (as the script called it)`).
- **The palette** is in `mod.rs`: `M.oak` is the material, `M.palette()` what the family is given. The finishes
  are the base manifest's `material.finish.*`; after one, `mirror(..)`, `glow(..)`, `screen()`, `roughness(x)`,
  `metallic(x)`. A model whose colours differ from the family's takes its own (`Model::palette`).

  ```rust
  palette! { M:
      oak = ("#CDB08A", wood), // a comment says what wears it
      glass = ("#DCE6EA", gloss, mirror(Mirror::Front)),
      lamp = ("#F2E6CC", fabric, glow("#FFD9A0")),
  }
  // in the family's list: another colour, or another material under the name
  Model::new("pond", pond, (1.9, 0.3, 1.4)).palette(M.palette().recoloured(&[("oak", "#8A6446")])),
  Model::new("post", post, (0.1, 1.2, 0.1)).palette(M.palette().with(&[Mat::new("oak", "#3A3C40", "iron")])),
  ```
- **A build** is `pub fn <model>() -> Vec<Obj>`: it fills an empty scene (one a thread; the models of a family are
  built side by side, so a family keeps no state between them) and returns its parts, or none for every mesh in the
  scene. `Build` is a function pointer: a script's `lambda` or closure becomes a named function that calls the
  shared one.
- **Parts.** `new_parts(|| { ... })` is the `with` block: the parts made inside, in order.
  `join(&parts, name, origin)` (or `join!(&parts, name, origin = p)`) makes them one part, `join_all(name)` the
  whole scene; `hinged(obj, pivot)` puts a moving part's origin on its hinge; `settle_all()` and `settle(&objs)`
  stand the model on the floor and centre it (`settle!(centre = false)`: on the floor only, where it was built).
  Settle before the clips: the kernel refuses after.
- **Clips**: `swing!`, `slide!`, `squash!`, `grow!`, `spin!`, `rock!`, `wobble!`, `stages!`, and `motion::keys`,
  `path`, `scroll`, `turns`, `grow_in_turn`, `flat`, `attach`. A swing's angle is in degrees, a slide's offset in
  Blender's axes (the front is -y), as the scripts gave them.
- **A model** is `Model::new(id, build, (w, h, d))` and what the script's `G.Model` said:

| `G.Model(...)` | Here |
|---|---|
| `key=`, `tolerance=`, `centre=(x, depth)` | `.key("model.x")`, `.tolerance(0.1)` or `.tolerance((0.02, 0.1, 0.02))` (a share a side; 0.02 if not said), `.centre(x, depth)` |
| `motions={...}`, `bird={...}` | `.motions(json!({"uses": {"fetch": "door_open"}, "hold": 1.5}))`, `.bird(json!(..))`; a value worked out goes in brackets: `{"hold": (HOLD * 2.0)}` |
| `paint=[(material, label)]` | `.paint(&[("top_oak", "Top"), ("legs_oak", "Legs")])` |
| `floor=False` | `.on_wall()` |
| `far=far_of(build)`, `far=other`, `far=0.25` | `.far_low()`, `.far(other)`, `.far_ratio(0.25)` |
| `habitat=`, `sink=`, `draws=`, `ground=`, `code=True` | `.habitat("tree")`, `.sink(0.05)`, `.draws(5)`, `.ground(false)`, `.code()` |
| `accept={'footprint': 'why'}` | `.accept("footprint", "why")`, once a check |
| `G.Family(name, palette, models, comment=, pack=)` | `Family::new(name, M.palette(), vec![...], comment)`, `.pack("gardenDecor")`, `.switched()` |

One model whole, the kitchen's plain counter (`crates/art/wet/src/kitchen_counters`): the still body joined with
its origin on the floor's middle, the drawer a part of its own, the model settled, then the clip; and its line in
`mod.rs` (`unit66`, `doors`, `drawer` and `uses` are the family's helpers):

```rust
// --- the plain counter: 1x1, a drawer over two doors, the drawer sliding out for a sandwich -----------
pub fn counter() -> Vec<Obj> {
    let f = &GameFrame::new(0.15);
    let still = new_parts(|| {
        unit66(f, 1.0, None, None);
        doors(
            f,
            0.974,
            0.14,
            0.12,
            0.72,
            None,
            None,
            Some(0.62),
            0.0,
            0.019,
        );
    });
    join(&still, "body", Some((0, 0, 0).into()));
    let drawer = drawer(
        f, "drawer", 0.974, 0.815, 0.16, M.cab, None, 0.18, true, 0.019, 0.04, 0.17,
    );
    settle_all();
    slide!(drawer, "drawer_open", (0, -0.25, 0), seconds = SECONDS);
    meshes()
}
```
```rust
Model::new("counter", counters::counter, (1.0, 0.95, 0.66))
    .key("model.counter")
    .centre(0.0, -0.10)
    // sandwich slides the top drawer out
    .motions(uses("sandwich", "drawer_open", HOLD))
    .paint(&BASE_PAINT),
```

## The rules of a port

1. **One function, one function.** Each function of the script, the private ones too, is one function here, under
   its name, in the script's order, with its doc comment said again in the repo's plain style. No merging, no
   splitting, no tidier arithmetic: `(a + b) / 2.0` stays that, `a * 0.5 + b * 0.5` stays that.
2. **Numbers verbatim.** Every literal as the script has it (`3.1415` where it wrote that:
   `#[allow(clippy::approx_constant)]`), whole where it was whole, in the same order of operations.
3. **Every part, in order, and every draw.** The parts are made and joined in the script's order under its names,
   at low detail too, and every random number is drawn where the script drew it.
4. **No fixes.** A fault of the script (a part inside another, a face inside out, a seat a centimetre low) is
   ported as it is and written into `NOTES.md`: the comparison can only prove a port that changes nothing.
5. **The kernel is not a family's to edit.** What a port needs and `artkit` lacks is asked for (the table's last
   row, an option a builder ignores, a number that is not Blender's). Until it lands, a local copy stands in,
   marked `// KIT: <what it stands in for>` over it, so it is found and replaced by the kernel's call
   (`rg '// KIT' crates/art`).
6. **Proved, not believed.** A model is done when the comparison says `same`, or `near` with every number
   accounted for; the port's `NOTES.md` says which and why.

## The traps

- **Vector against tuple.** A `mathutils.Vector` is a `V3`: single precision, with mathutils' own rounding (a
  scalar is cast down before it multiplies, `v / k` multiplies by the single `1 / k`, a dot product sums in
  double). A tuple of Python floats is `f64`s, a `P3` or `(f64, f64, f64)`. Keep each where the script had it:
  `Vector(p)` rounds there (`V3::from(p)`, `v3(x, y, z)`), `v.x` read into a float widens (`v.x as f64`), and
  the same sum in the other kind lands a unit in the last place away.
- **Meshes are single precision.** `v.co.z += d` reads a single, adds in double and stores a single:
  `v.z = (v.z as f64 + d) as f32`. The same for a part's place (`set_location_axis`).
- **Maths through the kernel.** `math::sin`, `cos`, `pow` and the rest are computed in software, the same on every
  machine; `f64::sin`, `powf` and `powi` are the platform's or the compiler's. Python's `round` is half to even,
  `sum` carries its rounding error, `math.hypot` is CPython's own, `%` takes the divisor's sign: `py::`.
- **The order of the draws.** A far version and a model stand alike because both draw every random number in one
  order. Rust changes that order quietly: a lazy iterator not yet collected, a `zip` or a `filter` that stops
  early, a struct's fields written in another order than the script's keywords (a call's arguments are worked
  out as written), a `HashMap` where the script walked a dict (use a `Vec` of pairs), `sort_unstable` for
  `sorted`. Draw into a `let`, in the script's order, when in doubt.
- **Whole zeros.** `F.at` negates x and depth, and Python's `-0` is `0` where `-0.0` is `-0.0`: a part's place
  keeps that sign. So `(0, 0.735, 0)` where the script wrote that and `(0.0, 0.735, 0.0)` where it wrote floats
  (`place::Coord`); a number worked out is an `f64`, as it was a float.
- **The bevel is lazy**, as Blender's modifier. `bevel = ..` only asks: the part is rounded when it is joined or
  written. Until then `o.mesh` and `world_points()` are the unbevelled part, which the build may still move, scale
  or cut; `measure`, `settle` and the size check count the bevel. Its UVs are made on the unbevelled mesh and
  carried onto the bevel. A width below zero is no bevel. Two shapes the kernel does not bevel panic and say so.
- **Vertex order holds, face order does not.** Blender numbers a primitive's points the same on every run and its
  faces not. Pick points by number freely; pick faces by what they are. Where the kernel's order is known not to
  be Blender's (a slab's far face, a cylinder's caps among its sides, the new points of `bisect_plane`), a build
  that reads those numbers differs.
- **A slab's far points are not near + n.** `extrude` numbers the copy's points as its triangles first name
  them, as Blender's extrusion does: find the far point of an outline's point by its place, not by adding the count.
- **Names.** A name in use gets Blender's number, the lowest free: a second `leg` is `leg.001`, and a part joined
  away leaves its mesh's name taken. The file's nodes are written by name (digits as numbers, no case), a child
  before its parent, so a name decides the file. Make and join in the script's order, and write a float in a name
  with `json::float_repr` (`legs-0.26`).
- **Turned by a quaternion.** A `rod!` is, and so is what is joined onto one: an Euler turn does nothing to it
  until its mode is set, as in Blender. `swing!`, `spin!`, `rock!`, `wobble!` and `path` set XYZ themselves.
- **A stale `matrix_world`.** Blender answers with the part's matrix as of the last operator or
  `view_layer.update()`: a part just made and placed still has the identity, one moved since the matrix from
  before. A script that reads it then (ground.py's cuts) depends on that: port the read as `matrix_world_seen()`
  and each `view_layer.update()` or operator of the script's own as `scene::update()`. The kernel's builders,
  `join`, `measure`, `jitter` and `attach` update where gamekit's did.
- **Two faces at once.** The baked shading casts rays from every vertex; where a part lies exactly on another, a
  ray meets two faces at one distance and takes the one Blender's tree comes to first, which hangs on the faces'
  order. The kernel walks Blender's tree, so most fall alike; one that does not is a grey at that vertex, in the
  `shading` number. Do not move a part a hair to settle it: that is a fix. Over the limit, say which part: it is
  the kernel's to settle.
- **Doubled faces go at export.** As Blender's exporter does, the writer drops a face on the corners of an earlier
  one, after the shading: the file can have fewer triangles than the build's line says.
- **Decimated far versions** (`far_ratio`) are the kernel's own, the same on every run; Blender's were not (two
  of its builds lay up to 55 mm apart). Where such a far version fails the comparison's rows it is held to the
  `decimated` rule in their place (the old size within 2 %, on the model's floor, at most a tenth more triangles,
  its surface within 6 cm of the old one's), once
  `"<key> far": {"decimated": "<why>"}` is in [port-accepted.json](../../tools/art/port-accepted.json).
- **What `near` may hide.** The limits leave room: 5 % of a part's texture and of its shading, 1 % of its normals,
  2 mm of surface. A port done right has its points on Blender's (`surface 0.000 mm`), and what is left is a
  normal's fourth decimal (`shape`), a bevel's band of texture (`uv`), a grey at a tied ray (`shading`), a key's
  last bit (`clips`). A surface off zero, normals turned, or triangles changed is a difference in the port until
  shown otherwise: find it. A row that is right as it is (Blender's own build does not repeat it) is asked for as
  a line in port-accepted.json, with the measured row as its reason; one that is not needed is an error.
- **Never build in place in a shared worktree.** `cargo art <family>` without `--out` or `--check` rewrites the
  models, the pack and `centres.json` in the tree (an unswitched family's by running Blender). Always `--out`, to
  a folder outside the repo.

## Authoring a new family, or a new model

The layout and the calls are the same; there is no reference to compare with, so the loop is build, gate, look:

1. **Content first.** The object's entry (footprint, heights, interactions) and what the model owes it are in
   docs/design/objects.md, "The short version". A model's key must be one the content or a manifest names, or it
   takes `.code()`; a misspelt key is refused before anything is built.
2. **The module.** A new model is a build function and a `Model` in the family's `mod.rs`. A new family is a
   folder as above, `pub mod <family>;` and a row in its group's `families()`, `.switched()` from the start, and
   its pack listed in the base manifest's `packs`.
3. **Build to its real size**, in game numbers through a `GameFrame`, materials named for what they are. Take
   `size` and `centre` from the measured line once it looks right. Shared parts come from `artkit::kit` and
   `artkit::botany`; one that two families would make belongs there: ask, do not copy.
4. `cargo art <family> --only <id>` in a worktree of your own writes the file, the pack entry and its centre, and
   runs the gate: its strict checks are errors (footprint, wall face, centre, flipped faces, unbevelled edges,
   texels, a clip no use plays ...), its warnings are read one by one (tools/art/README.md, "The gate"). The
   budgets (triangles for the size, one to three draw calls), the finishes, moving parts and far versions are
   objects.md's.
5. **Look**: `--preview --sheet`, then the gallery and the inspector, by day and at night, as objects.md's review
   checklist asks.
6. **Before the commit**: `cargo art <family> --check` (the same bytes again), `cargo check-glb --validate`,
   `cargo review sizes --check` and `cargo review uses --check`, and the model's notes in `NOTES.md`.

The traps that are about matching Blender fall away; those about one build giving the same bytes on every machine
do not: maths through `artkit::math`, draws in a fixed order, no `HashMap` walked, no time or thread in a number.
